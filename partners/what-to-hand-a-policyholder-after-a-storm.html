After a Storm, What Can I Hand My Policyholder to Help Them Find a Contractor?

Otter Quotes · For insurance agents · Updated October 2026

← Referral Partner Program

After a storm, an insurance agent can hand a policyholder a way to get a written scope of work for the repair and bids from more than one contractor. Otter Quotes creates the scope of work and sends it to multiple contractors, so the policyholder can compare bids online. An agent can share a personal referral link.

Why does a policyholder need more than one bid?

A single bid gives a policyholder nothing to compare it to. Contractors price a repair or replacement from a scope of work, a written list of the work to be done, and a comparison is only fair when every bid prices that same scope. A policyholder with more than one written bid can see how the prices differ.

Bids written for different work do not compare, because each contractor may be pricing something different. When bids are written for the same scope, the differences between them are easier to see. Our side-by-side worksheet for roofing bids is a table a policyholder can fill in, one column per bid.

Materials and warranties belong in that comparison. Roofing and siding manufacturers make higher grade products with better resistance to wind, hail, and algae. When contractors know they are competing for a policyholder's business, they are more likely to include better materials and warranties.

How does Otter Quotes work for a policyholder?

Otter Quotes creates a written scope of work for the project and sends it to multiple contractors. Every bid prices that same scope of work. The policyholder compares the bids online, next to reviews and licensing information, with no salesperson in the home, and chooses which bid to accept.

Here is the path for a policyholder who comes from an agent's referral link:

  1. The policyholder opens the agent's referral link and creates an account.
  2. The policyholder provides a copy of their insurance estimate if they have one, or purchases professional measurements of the property.
  3. The policyholder selects what needs to be quoted, such as roofing, siding, gutters or windows.
  4. Otter Quotes sends the scope of work to contractors, who can submit bids.
  5. The policyholder compares the bids, chooses one, and signs the contract online.

Otter Quotes does not inspect roofs or assess damage, does not work with the policyholder's insurance company, does not negotiate claims, and does not sell upgrades. Contractors pay Otter Quotes a fee on the accepted bid.

What does it cost a policyholder, and what does it cost an agent?

There is no obligation to work with Otter Quotes contractors, and no cost for a policyholder who provides a copy of their insurance estimate. An agent pays nothing to sign up as a partner or to share a referral link.

A policyholder without an estimate pays an upfront charge of as little as $15 to purchase professional measurements that every contractor uses. If the policyholder completes a job on the platform, that money is refunded to them.

What can a policyholder ask a contractor before choosing?

A policyholder can ask every contractor the same short list of questions, whether the contractor arrived at the door, came from a search, or bid through Otter Quotes. The same questions make the answers easier to compare. These questions are for contractors. Questions about a claim or a policy are for the policyholder's insurer.

  • Does this bid cover the same written scope as the other bids?
  • Which materials are included, by manufacturer and product line?
  • What does the warranty cover, for how long, and who stands behind it?
  • What license information can you show me, and where can I look it up?
  • Will your own crew do the work, or a subcontractor?
  • What are the payment terms, and what is the proposed start date?
