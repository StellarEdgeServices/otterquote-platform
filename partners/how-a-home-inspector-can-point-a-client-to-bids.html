How Can a Home Inspector Point a Client to Bids Without Recommending a Contractor?

A home inspector can point a client to a way to collect written bids from several contractors on one scope of work, without naming any contractor. Otter Quotes creates that scope of work and sends it to multiple contractors, who can submit bids. The client compares the bids and chooses. You share a link, not a recommendation.

Why does a client ask the inspector who to call?

Your report just told the client that something is wrong, and you are the person they trust most on the house, so the question is natural. The risk is that a name from you carries your credibility into a contractor's price and workmanship, which you have not seen and cannot control. A path to bids avoids that.

Clients ask on site, by phone after the report arrives, and again when a contractor's number seems high. A client may have one bid in hand, or none, and a name from you would settle the question for them. That is the moment your wording matters most. A useful answer gives the client a next step and keeps your findings separate from any contractor's work.

What is the difference between recommending a contractor and pointing a client to bids?

Recommending puts a contractor's name next to yours. Pointing to bids gives the client a way to find out for themselves, and no contractor is named. The client sees several written bids on the same scope of work and decides. Your part stays limited to the findings in your report.

What can I write in my report or message?

Keep it to a pointer and a plain statement of what you are not doing. Say that the client can get written bids from more than one contractor on the same scope of work through a link, and that you do not recommend or endorse any contractor. If your report format allows, put it in a separate next-steps note rather than inside your findings.

Wording for a report:

If you want written bids from more than one contractor on the same scope of work, this link goes to a service that sends one scope to multiple contractors. I do not recommend or endorse any contractor, and the link does not change my findings. The decision is yours.

Wording for a text or email:

Here is the link I mentioned. It lets you get bids from several contractors on one written scope of work. I am not recommending anyone, so use it or not as you see fit, and compare whatever bids you get.

Change the words to fit your own voice and your own report. The parts to keep are the two statements: the link is a way to get bids, and you recommend no one.

Check your state's standards of practice and your association's rules before you add any next-step language to a report or agreement. Otter Quotes cannot tell you what they require. If you are unsure, ask the body that licenses or governs you.

What should I avoid saying?

Avoid anything that sounds like a promise or a pick: that a contractor is the best, what the repair will cost, or when bids will arrive. You do not control any of that, and Otter Quotes cannot promise it either.

  • A name. "Call this company" and "my guy" are recommendations, however casual.
  • A promise about bids. Do not say the client will get the best price, a certain number of bids, or bids by a certain day.
  • A price for the repair. Your report names what you found. Contractors price the work.
  • A description of Otter Quotes that is not true. It does not assess damage or recommend a contractor. It sends one scope of work to multiple contractors and puts their bids side by side.

What if the client asks me which bid to choose?

You can say the choice is theirs and point them to the way to compare: the same scope, the written bids side by side, and a set of questions for each contractor. You have not seen the contractors' work, so you are not in a position to rank them. Our side-by-side worksheet for roofing bids gives the client a column for each bid.

Questions your client can ask each contractor

  • Does your bid cover the same scope of work as the other bids?
  • Which materials are named, by brand and product line?
  • What does the written warranty cover, and who stands behind it?
  • Will your own crew do the work, or a subcontractor?
  • What is not included in this bid?
  • How will the schedule be confirmed in writing?

Frequently asked questions

Is pointing a client to bids the same as recommending a contractor?

They differ in what you say. Recommending names one company. Pointing to bids names none: you share a link to a service that sends one scope of work to multiple contractors, and the client decides. Whether a particular rule treats the two differently is a question for your state's standards of practice and your association's rules.

Does Otter Quotes recommend or choose a contractor?

No. Otter Quotes sends the written scope of work to multiple contractors, and contractors who choose to bid compete for the client's business. The client compares the bids online and decides which one to accept. Otter Quotes is not a contractor and does not sell upgrades. It is paid by the contractor whose bid the client accepts.

Does Otter Quotes inspect the property or change my findings?

No. Otter Quotes does not inspect roofs or assess damage, and it does not change your report. Your findings stay as you wrote them. Otter Quotes creates a written scope of work for the project the client wants quoted and sends that scope to contractors, who bid on it.
