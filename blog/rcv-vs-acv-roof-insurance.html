ACV vs. RCV Roof Insurance: What They Mean

ACV and RCV are two ways a policy can value a roof. Here is what each term means.

ACV and RCV are two terms for how a roof can be valued, and depreciation is the word that separates them.

Key Terms

These terms are listed in alphabetical order.

Actual cash value (ACV)

A way of valuing the roof that accounts for depreciation based on its age and condition.

Source: Texas Department of Insurance, Home Insurance Guide.

Depreciation

A decrease in value because of age and wear.

Source: Texas Department of Insurance, Home Insurance Guide.

Recoverable depreciation

Depreciation that an estimate marks as recoverable. Your policy states the conditions.

Replacement cost value (RCV)

The cost to repair or replace the damaged property at current prices, with no deduction for depreciation.

Source: Texas Department of Insurance, Home Insurance Guide; Texas Department of Insurance, Home Insurance Glossary.

Which One Applies

The Texas Department of Insurance says that “Home policies provide either replacement cost coverage or actual cash value coverage”. Your policy states which one applies.

Other policy words, such as declarations page and endorsement, are defined in Roof Damage and Home Insurance: Policy Terms.
