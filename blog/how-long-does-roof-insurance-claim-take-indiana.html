How Long Does a Roof Insurance Claim Take in Indiana? A Realistic Timeline

From the first call to your insurer to the final depreciation payment — here's what a Central Indiana roof claim actually looks like, stage by stage, and where the delays usually happen.

By Dustin Stohler · September 22, 2026

← All Articles

"How long will this take?" is the first question almost every Indiana homeowner asks after a storm damages their roof — and the honest answer is a range, not a number. A straightforward claim with a responsive insurer can be substantially settled in three to five weeks. A claim filed during a regional hail event, when every insurer in Central Indiana is working through the same backlog at once, can stretch to two or three months before repairs even start. This guide walks through the realistic timeline stage by stage, so you know what's normal, what's slow, and when it's worth following up.

1

Documenting the Damage: 0–72 Hours

The clock effectively starts the moment the storm passes, not when you call your insurer. Photograph every damaged area — roof surface, gutters, siding, and any interior water intrusion — before any temporary repairs disturb the evidence. This stage doesn't have a "deadline" in the way later stages do, but thorough documentation now is what keeps every later stage moving quickly instead of stalling on a disputed detail.

In Central Indiana specifically, spring and summer hail events tend to arrive in clusters — a single storm system can generate thousands of claims across the metro in one afternoon. If you're filing in the days immediately after a widespread event, expect every downstream stage below to run toward the longer end of its range.

2

Reporting the Claim: Same Day to About 1 Week

Most insurers let you report a claim by phone, app, or website within minutes. The reporting step itself is fast — what varies is how quickly you're ready to do it. Have your policy number, the date of the storm, and a factual description of the damage on hand. You'll be assigned a claim number immediately; every conversation after this point should reference it.

3

Adjuster Inspection: A Few Days to 2+ Weeks Out

This is the first stage where storm volume really shows up in the timeline. In a normal week, an adjuster can typically get to your property within several days of your report. After a widespread regional hail event, that same appointment can slip to two weeks or more, simply because every adjuster in the territory is booked solid.

You can shorten this stage. Ask whether your own contractor can attend the adjuster's inspection. A contractor who walks the roof alongside the adjuster catches missed damage in real time, instead of surfacing it later as a supplement request — which adds its own delay (see Stage 5).
4

Written Estimate: A Few Days to 2 Weeks After Inspection

After the inspection, the insurer issues a written estimate — typically within a few days to two weeks, depending on the carrier's caseload and how straightforward the damage is. This is the point where it's worth getting a contractor's independent bid, if you haven't already, so you have something concrete to compare the estimate against.

5

Supplement Requests (If Needed): 1–4 Weeks to Review

A gap between the insurer's estimate and a contractor's bid is common, not a sign of bad faith on either side — the two documents are generated differently, and insurance estimating software can miss line items a contractor's on-roof bid catches. If your contractor's bid includes damage the estimate doesn't, that gap is the basis for a supplement request. Submitting it promptly, with photos and the bid attached, is what keeps this stage on the shorter end of its range; an informal phone call that's never followed up in writing is the single most common reason this stage drags on for months instead of weeks.

6

Choosing a Contractor and Starting Repairs: Timing Is Yours to Control

Once the estimate (and any supplement) is settled, the repair timeline is no longer the insurer's clock — it's the contractor's. This is also the stage where getting multiple bids pays off in more than just price: a contractor with a backlog might not start for six to eight weeks, while another with capacity could start within days. Comparing bids side by side, not just settling for whoever knocked on your door first, is usually the fastest lever you have left in the whole process.

7

Recoverable Depreciation: 180 Days to 1 Year From the First Payment

If your policy is Replacement Cost Value (RCV), the insurer's first payment is actually just the depreciated (ACV) amount — the remainder, called recoverable depreciation, isn't released until repairs are complete and you've submitted proof: a signed contractor invoice and photos of the finished work. Most policies give you 180 days to a year from the first payment to submit for it. Missing that window can mean forfeiting money you're otherwise owed, so set a reminder the day repairs finish, not the day you remember.

The Timeline at a Glance

Stage Typical Timeframe
Document damage 0–72 hours
Report the claim Same day to ~1 week
Adjuster inspection A few days to 2+ weeks (longer after a regional storm)
Written estimate A few days to 2 weeks after inspection
Supplement review (if needed) 1–4 weeks
Repairs Depends on contractor availability — get multiple bids
Recoverable depreciation released 180 days–1 year from first payment (RCV policies)

These are general timeframes, not a promise about any specific claim — your insurer, policy terms, and the scale of the storm event all affect the actual schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's a realistic total timeline for a straightforward Indiana roof claim?

Outside of a major regional storm event, three to five weeks from reporting the claim to a signed contract with a contractor is a reasonable expectation, with the physical repair itself typically taking one to three days once work begins. Recoverable depreciation on an RCV policy follows separately, after the work is documented as complete.

Why does my claim take longer after a big hailstorm?

Because every insurer's adjusters in the affected area are working through the same surge of claims at once. The inspection and written-estimate stages are the ones most affected — a claim that would normally see an adjuster within days can wait two or more weeks when a widespread storm has hit Central Indiana.

Does getting multiple contractor bids slow my claim down?

No — if anything, it speeds up the parts of the process you control. A contractor's independent bid is what surfaces a supplement request early (Stage 5), and comparing bids on availability, not just price, is often the fastest way to shorten the repair stage, since contractor backlogs vary widely.

What should I do if my claim has gone quiet?

A short written status-check email referencing your claim number is a reasonable first step. Written follow-up creates a paper trail that a phone call doesn't, and it's often what moves a stalled claim back onto someone's desk.
