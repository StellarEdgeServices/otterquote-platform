Why Two Roofers Quote Different Prices for the Same Job

Two written quotes for one roof can differ in price because they may not describe the same job. Here are the parts of a quote that can differ.

Otter Quotes · Updated October 2026

← All Articles

You ask two roofers to quote the same roof and the prices come back apart. Before comparing the totals, compare what each quote describes: the scope of work, the materials named, what goes into the price besides materials, and how complicated the roof is. This page covers each, and links to the pages that define the terms.

Are the Quotes for the Same Scope of Work?

Scope of work, square, flashing, valley and underlayment are defined in How to Read a Roofing Contractor Estimate, which also has a table of line items. Two quotes for one roof can describe different jobs:

  • Replaced or reused. One quote may list new flashing, drip edge and pipe boots while another leaves them out or reuses the existing ones.
  • Underlayment and ice barrier. The model residential code’s ice barrier requirement begins “In areas where there has been a history of ice forming along the eaves causing a backup of water as designated in Table R301.2(1), an ice barrier shall be installed”, so whether a quote includes one can depend on the local code and on what the contractor specifies (Indiana Department of Homeland Security).
  • Decking. One quote may state how damaged decking found after tear-off is priced and recorded, and another may not. The Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry lists “the total contract price or how the price will be calculated” among the things a written contract should include (Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry).
Compare scope before price. Otter Quotes creates a scope of work and submits it to multiple contractors. When two quotes list the same work and the same materials, the remaining difference is the part to weigh.

Do the Quotes Name the Same Materials?

“Architectural shingles” names a category, and a product line such as GAF Timberline HDZ names a product. A quote that names the manufacturer and the product line says which product it prices. The line-item table in the estimate guide defines shingles and underlayment, and two quotes can price different underlayment under the same word.

Warranties are a further difference, because they depend on the product and on who installs it; see Roofing Warranty Tiers Explained.

What Else Is in the Price Besides Materials?

A price covers more than shingles. It covers the contractor's labor and the cost of running the business, which can include sales commissions, marketing, general contractors' expenses, and trucks and fuel. Quotes can also differ in terms: the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry lists a “payment schedule making clear when payments are due” and “specific timelines” among the parts of a written contract (Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry). How Complicated Is the Roof?

The line-item table in the estimate guide defines flashing and valley. A roof with more valleys, edges and penetrations has more places where flashing is installed, and a quote may price each one.

Roof area is also stated in squares, which the same table defines. If two quotes state different numbers of squares, they are not priced on the same roof area. How Do You Compare Quotes?

Put the quotes side by side and line them up before you compare totals:

  • Match the scope. The same line items, the same number of squares and the same flashing and ventilation plan.
  • Match the materials. The same manufacturer and product line, and the same underlayment.
  • Match the warranties. The manufacturer warranty by name and the workmanship warranty, each in writing.
  • Match the terms. The payment schedule and the timeline.
  • Then compare price. What remains after the items above are matched is the difference between the bids.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is one roofing quote higher than the others?

Quotes can differ in scope, in the materials named, in what the price covers besides materials, and in the complexity of the roof as the contractor measured it. Compare the line items of the quotes before you compare the totals.

Should I pick the lowest quote?

Not on price alone. The Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry’s list of contractors to avoid includes those that “are willing to do the job at an unusually low price” (Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry). A lower total can only be compared with a higher one when both list the same scope, materials, warranties and terms. Line the quotes up first, then compare price.

Why do two quotes state different numbers of squares?

A square is defined in the estimate guide. If two quotes state different numbers of squares, they are not priced on the same roof area. Ask each contractor where the number came from.

Does Otter Quotes set the prices contractors quote?
