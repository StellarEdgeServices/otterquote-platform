What to Ask Your Roofer on an Insurance Job

The repair itself is only part of the job. Here is what you can ask a roofing contractor about: materials, warranties and workmanship.

By Otter Quotes · April 5, 2026 · Updated October 2026

← All Articles

A roof repair has two parts: the repair itself, and the choices around it, such as materials, warranties and cleanup. When contractors know they are competing for your business, they are more likely to include better materials or stronger warranties. This page lists what you can ask a roofing contractor about, so you can compare bids on the same points.

Ask for everything in writing. Whatever a contractor offers, ask for it in the bid or the contract, with product names and warranty terms, so you can compare it with other bids.

1

Shingle Quality

Shingles come in different grades and ratings. Ask each contractor which shingle they are quoting and how it is rated, and ask what a higher grade would add to the bid.

  • Ask about a thicker, higher-rated shingle. Ask what the price difference is, in writing.
  • Ask about impact-resistant (IR) shingles. Many insurers offer a discount when you install impact-resistant roofing, and the amount varies by company and state. Ask yours what it offers.
  • Compare brand tiers. Not all shingles are the same. Ask each contractor which brand and product line is in the bid (for example GAF Timberline HDZ, Owens Corning Duration or CertainTeed Landmark Pro), whether the manufacturer makes a higher line, and what it would add to the price.
2

Workmanship Warranties

Shingle manufacturers publish warranties for their products. Workmanship warranties come from the contractor and are a separate document or a part of the contract. Warranties differ, so read the terms of each one.

  • Ask how long the workmanship warranty is and what it covers. Compare the answers from each contractor, and ask who stands behind the warranty.
  • Get it in writing. A verbal promise is hard to rely on. The workmanship warranty should be a separate document (or clearly stated in your contract) specifying what is covered and for how long.
  • Ask about manufacturer warranty options. Ask whether a manufacturer warranty beyond the standard one is available for your project, who issues it, what it covers and what it costs, and ask to see the terms in writing.

Warranty terms differ between contractors. Compare the length, what is covered and who stands behind it.

3

Underlayment and Ice & Water Shield

The underlayment is the protective layer between your roof deck and the shingles. It is not visible once the roof is finished, so ask what is being installed.

  • Ask which underlayment is in the bid. Ask whether it is synthetic or felt paper, and what the difference in price would be.
  • Ask about ice and water shield. It is a self-adhering membrane used in valleys, around chimneys and skylights, and along eaves. Ask whether it is included in the bid, in which areas, and what it adds to the price.
  • Ask about full-deck ice and water shield. Ask whether it is an option for your roof and what it would add to the price.
4

Ventilation

Attic ventilation is part of how a roof system is built. When a contractor is already replacing your roof, ask whether ventilation is part of the bid and whether anything should be added or upgraded.

  • Ask about ridge vent installation. If your roof does not already have a ridge vent, ask whether the contractor recommends one for your roof and why.
  • Ask about intake vents. Ventilation depends on air moving in and out. Ask the contractor whether your soffit vents are clear and adequate.
  • Ask about older components. If your roof has old turbine vents, powered attic fans, or many box vents, ask the contractor what they recommend.
Why this matters: Manufacturers publish installation and ventilation requirements for their shingles. Ask your contractor which ones apply to your roof, and read the warranty terms.
5

Flashing and Drip Edge

Flashing and drip edge are the metal components that seal transitions and edges on your roof — around chimneys, walls, vents, and along the eaves and rakes. Ask what is being installed at each of these points.

  • Ask what drip edge is in the bid. Ask what material it is and what a different material would add to the price.
  • Ask about step flashing. If your roof meets any vertical walls (dormers, chimneys, second-story walls), ask whether the step flashing is being replaced or reused.
  • Confirm chimney flashing and pipe boots. If they are not being replaced as part of the reroof, ask why.

6

Cleanup

A roof replacement generates debris — old shingles, nails, packaging, and dust. Ask each contractor how they handle cleanup.

  • Ask about a magnetic nail sweep. Roofing nails can end up in lawns, driveways, flower beds, and sidewalks. Ask whether a rolling magnetic sweeper is included, and ask for it in writing.
  • Ask about property protection. This includes tarps over landscaping, plywood protection for air conditioning units, and care around windows and siding. Ask what the contractor does.
  • Ask about daily and final cleanup. Ask what the plan is for debris at the end of each day and after the job, and get it in writing.
7

Use Competition to Your Advantage

Contractors who know they are competing for your project are more likely to include better materials or warranties to earn it. Asking the same questions of each contractor makes bids easier to compare.

  • Get more than one bid. Bids on the same points are easier to compare side by side.
  • Share what matters to you. Tell each contractor what you are looking for, such as better shingles, longer warranties or upgraded underlayment, and see how each one responds.
  • Don't choose on price alone. A lower bid with a shorter warranty and basic materials is not the same as a slightly higher bid with a longer warranty and upgraded materials. Compare the whole package.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Otter Quotes work with my insurance company?

No. Otter Quotes does not work with your insurance company, negotiate claims or act as a public adjuster.

Why might a contractor include upgrades when bidding against others?

Contractors on Otter Quotes know they are competing for your business, so they are more likely to include better materials or warranties to earn it. Nothing is promised: each bid states what is included. Read each bid's scope, materials and warranty, and ask the contractor about any difference.

What if my contractor says upgrades aren't possible on an insurance job?

Ask the contractor to explain why in writing, and what it would take. You can also ask other contractors to bid on the same scope of work.

How do I know if a contractor is offering real value vs. just saying what I want to hear?

Get everything in writing. A written upgrade offer should be documented in the contract with specific product names, model numbers, and warranty terms. Ask for manufacturer specification sheets for the materials they are proposing. A verbal promise is hard to rely on, so ask for it to be added to the contract.

How do I decide whether an upgrade is worth an added cost?

Ask each contractor to price the upgrade as a separate line in writing. Compare the added cost with what the upgrade offers, such as a longer warranty or different materials. The decision is yours, based on your own budget.
