What Starter Strip and Ridge Cap Shingles Should Your Roof Replacement Bid Name?

Otter Quotes · Updated November 2026

← All Guides

Starter strip shingles run along the eaves and rakes, and ridge cap shingles cover the peaks. Some manufacturer warranties name matching versions of both as a condition of eligibility. A bid that says only "shingles" may leave them out. Ask each contractor to name both, by brand and product, before you compare totals.

What are starter strip and ridge cap shingles, and why does a bid need to name them?

Starter strip shingles are the first course installed at the eaves and rakes, and ridge cap shingles finish the ridge and hips. Manufacturers sell both as separate products. A bid should name them because a line that says only "shingles" does not tell you which brand or product goes at the edges and the peak.

These are small lines in a bid, and they are easy to overlook. Once the job is done, the written bid and contract are the only record of which products went on your roof.

This page does not say any roof needs a particular brand, and it does not rank products. It shows what three shingle makers' own warranty documents say, so you know what to ask the contractor to write down.

Why do shingle makers tie warranties to matching accessories?

In the documents we read, the manufacturer makes certain accessory products a condition of a warranty. GAF's sample Golden Pledge warranty says its wind coverage is "specifically conditioned on your meeting all eligibility requirements." The documents state the requirements; they do not explain the maker's reasons, so this page does not guess at them.

What matters to you is the effect. If a warranty lists starter strip or ridge cap shingles among its eligibility requirements, the shingles on your bid are not the whole story. The accessories on the bid decide whether you meet the conditions the manufacturer wrote.

Each warranty is different, and each is tied to a specific shingle line, a specific year of document and a specific type of property. GAF's sample warranty applies its lifetime terms to "a single-family detached residence owned by individuals," and sends other buildings to a different warranty. Owens Corning's documents likewise set different coverage periods for other structures. Read the document for your own shingle.

Which accessories do GAF, Owens Corning and CertainTeed name?

Each manufacturer names accessories differently, and not every warranty requires every product. GAF's Golden Pledge brochure requires GAF starter strip and ridge cap shingles; its System Plus brochure asks for three of five accessory categories. Owens Corning lists Hip & Ridge shingles for its System and Platinum warranties. CertainTeed's warranty names starter and ridge for its higher wind warranty.

Does using another brand's accessories affect the warranty?

It can, depending on the document. GAF's sample warranty excludes damage caused by products "not sold by GAF" and says the warranty "will not take effect unless all eligibility requirements have been satisfied." GAF's Golden Pledge brochure lists GAF starter strip and ridge cap shingles among its eligibility requirements.

A bid that quietly swaps in an unnamed or different-brand product may therefore be different from the roof the warranty describes. We cannot tell you how a given manufacturer would treat your roof. Only that manufacturer's warranty document for your shingle can.

The practical step is to ask the contractor to write down the brand and product of the starter strip and the ridge cap, and to point to the warranty document that says they qualify. If the answer is vague, treat it like any other vague line in a bid.

How do ridge caps connect to algae warranties?

Some algae warranties carry a ridge cap condition, and it varies by brand and shingle line. Owens Corning's System Protection document and CertainTeed's 2026 warranty both tie their longest algae terms to their own hip and ridge shingles. Here is how we explain the algae side:

Algae causes the dark streaks on your roof that make your roof look old. Shingle manufacturers fight algae by adding copper to the granules on the shingle surface. Over time, that copper leaches out as rain washes over the roof, and the shingles lose their algae protection. Most algae-resistant shingles have an algae warranty of at least 10 years, but in the last few years those warranties have grown to 25 or 30 years on higher grade shingles.

Algae warranties depend on the brand and line of shingle you use. Some shingles earn a longer algae warranty by releasing copper over a longer period, through more copper, a time-release design, or both, and some brands also require their matching ridge cap shingles for the longer term. So if you want a longer algae warranty, choose a shingle line that carries one, and check whether the brand's ridge cap shingles are required to get it.

Our guide to algae-resistant shingle warranties goes deeper. For your bid, the question is the same: which ridge cap is named, and does the warranty for your shingle require it?

Why can two bids differ on starter strip and ridge cap?

Because the lines are short and easy to leave vague. One bid may say "starter and ridge included." A second may name a brand and product for each. A third may say nothing, because the contractor assumes the shingle price covers them. All three can look alike on the total.

That is why a lower total is not always a like-for-like total. Before you decide one bid is cheaper, check that each names the same shingle, starter and ridge cap, and that each matches the warranty it offers. NRCA, the National Roofing Contractors Association, advises homeowners to "insist on a written proposal" with complete descriptions of the work and specifications. For these lines, that means brand, product and where each goes. Our roofing contract checklist before you sign shows how to get that into the contract.

What to ask every roofing contractor about starter strip and ridge cap

Put the same questions to every bidder and keep the answers in writing, so you can set them side by side. Copy this list into a note and send it to each contractor before you compare totals. The wording is plain on purpose, so any contractor can answer it.

  • Which brand and product is the starter strip, and where does it go?
  • Which brand and product is the ridge cap, and does it match the shingle?
  • Which manufacturer warranty is being offered, and which document is it?
  • Does that warranty list starter strip or ridge cap among its requirements? Where is that written?
  • Which other accessories does the warranty require, such as underlayment, a leak barrier or ventilation?
  • Are the starter and ridge cap priced as their own lines or inside the shingle price?
  • If the product changes before or during the job, how is the change priced and agreed in writing?
  • Will the manufacturer's installation instructions for these products be followed, and where can I read them?

Frequently asked questions

Do I need GAF, Owens Corning or CertainTeed starter and ridge cap shingles?

It depends on the manufacturer's warranty for your shingle. GAF's Golden Pledge brochure requires GAF starter strip and ridge cap shingles. Owens Corning's System Protection document requires its Hip & Ridge shingles unless the roof uses Supreme shingles. Read the document for your shingle and ask the contractor to point to the requirement.

Is a starter strip the same as the first row of shingles?

Not necessarily. Manufacturers sell starter strip shingles as separate products, and GAF's System Plus brochure lists them as their own accessory category, installed at the eaves. A bid line that says only "shingles" does not show which product starts the roof. Ask each bidder to name the brand and product in writing.

Can the contractor use a different brand's ridge cap?

Check your shingle's warranty. GAF's sample warranty excludes damage caused by products "not sold by GAF" and says it does not take effect until all eligibility requirements are met. GAF's Golden Pledge brochure requires GAF Ridge Cap Shingles, and Owens Corning's documents name its own Hip & Ridge products. Get the qualifying product confirmed in writing.

Does a ridge cap affect my algae warranty?

It can. Owens Corning's System Protection document requires its own Hip & Ridge shingles for 25-year algae coverage, and CertainTeed's 2026 warranty limits algae coverage to 10 years without its algae-resistant hip and ridge. The terms depend on the brand and shingle line. Ask which ridge cap is named and where the warranty requires it.

Where can I see which starter and ridge cap I am getting?

Look in the written bid and contract. If it says only "starter and ridge" or "shingles," ask the contractor to name the brand and product for each. NRCA advises homeowners to "insist on a written proposal" describing the work and specifications. The contract is your record of which products were named.
