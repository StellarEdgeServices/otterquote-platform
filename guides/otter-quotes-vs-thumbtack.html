Otter Quotes or Thumbtack: Which Fits Your Exterior Project?

Otter Quotes · Updated November 2026

← All Guides

Thumbtack and Otter Quotes start a job in different ways. On Thumbtack, you describe your project and choose pros to contact. On Otter Quotes, we write one scope of work and send it to multiple contractors, who may reply with written bids. Pick the start that fits how you want to shop.

Otter Quotes is not affiliated with Thumbtack. The facts on this page about Thumbtack come from two Thumbtack help pages written for professionals, which we opened on November 4, 2026, and each statement links to the page it came from. The suggestions about who may prefer which site are ours. Thumbtack can change those pages at any time. We did not open the pages you see when you request a job there, so this guide does not describe that step by step. One more thing to weigh: contractors pay Otter Quotes a fee on the bid you accept, so we benefit when you choose a contractor on our site.

What is the main difference between Otter Quotes and Thumbtack?

The main difference is what you end up comparing. On Thumbtack, according to its help page for pros, customers describe their project, answer a few questions, see pros and reach out to the ones they choose. On Otter Quotes, you describe the project once and contractors who choose to bid price the scope of work we write.

On Thumbtack, your first task is choosing whom to contact. The same page says that once a customer starts a conversation with a pro, it is up to the pro to win the job, so what you have to compare depends on what each pro sends back.

On Otter Quotes, you set up the project once, and then you read bids, because each one answers the same written scope. The bids can still differ in materials, warranties, schedule and what they leave out. Those differences are the part to read closely.

How do you find contractors on each one?

On Thumbtack, you search and choose. The same help page says customers describe the project in their own words, or with a photo in the app, and Thumbtack shows the pros that fit. Customers then reach out to the pros they like. That suits you if you want to look at profiles and decide who hears from you.

On Otter Quotes, you do not pick from a list. We send your scope of work to multiple contractors, and those who choose to bid reply with written bids. You compare the bids online, next to reviews and licensing information, and you sign online. The number of bids can vary by job and area.

Contractors do not see your name, phone number or street address until you choose one.

What do contractors pay on each one, and why does it matter to you?

Contractors pay for different things on each. Thumbtack's page on lead prices says a pro pays for leads, which it describes as new customers who reach out to the pro directly. It says prices vary with the type and size of the job, how many pros are available and the market. Pros set their own lead prices.

For what it calls Opportunities, the same page says the pro reaches out first and pays the price shown only if the customer responds.

On Otter Quotes, contractors do not pay per lead. Our Terms say Otter Quotes currently charges a service fee of 5% of the accepted bid amount, that the fee is charged to contractors, not directly to homeowners, and that it is charged once the contract is signed by both you and the contractor.

A payment model does not tell you whether a particular contractor is good, and we are not saying it does. It tells you when a contractor's main charge applies. On Thumbtack, per its page, that is when a customer reaches out or responds. On Otter Quotes, the platform fee is charged once the contract is signed. A contractor can also order a fuller roof report, which is a separate optional charge. Judge every contractor, on either site, by license, insurance, reviews, references and the written terms of the bid.

How does a bid on the same scope change the comparison?

A scope of work is a written list of the work to be done: what comes off, what goes on, which materials, which parts of the house, and what is not included. When every contractor prices the same list, a difference in price is easier to read, because you are not guessing whether two prices cover the same job.

The bids will not be identical in every way. One contractor may name a better underlayment, another may offer a longer labor warranty, a third may exclude deck repair. Read each bid next to the scope and note what each one adds or leaves out. Our roof bid comparison worksheet gives you the rows to line up.

If you order the aerial roof report, you get professional measurements that contractors use to bid. A contractor who needs more can order a fuller report. If you would rather read prices at your own pace than take calls, this format may suit you. If you would rather talk first, Thumbtack's approach may suit you better.

What if the job is an insurance claim?

If you have an insurance estimate, you can give Otter Quotes a copy, and there is no cost to you. Contractors then bid on the same scope. The insurance guides on our site explain how ACV and RCV policies pay and how to compare bids. We do not work with your insurance company, negotiate claims or act as a public adjuster. What your policy covers and pays stays between you and your insurer.

For a roof, if you do not have an insurance estimate, you can upload measurements you already have or order an aerial roof report from us for as little as $22.63 up front. It is refunded in full when you complete a job through the platform; the conditions are in our Terms.

The Thumbtack pages we opened do not discuss insurance claims, so this guide says nothing about how Thumbtack handles them. If you use any site for a claim job, ask each contractor whether the price is in writing and whether it covers the same work as the other bids you are comparing. This guide does not tell you what your insurer will or should cover. For anything about your coverage, check your policy or ask your insurer. When might Thumbtack suit you better?

Repairs are not our specialty and we cover only roofing, siding and gutters, so for a small repair or another trade you may want a site that handles it. Thumbtack may also suit you if you want to talk with a pro before anyone writes a price.

If you want to look at pros yourself and decide whom to contact, that is how Thumbtack's help page describes the process.

Otter Quotes may suit an exterior project where you want written bids on the same scope, compared side by side, without a salesperson in your home. What should you ask before you choose anyone?

Copy this list and use it with any contractor, on any site.

  • Is your price in writing, and does it say what is included and what is not?
  • Does the bid name the products by brand and line, and say who backs each warranty?
  • Is this price for the same work and the same measurements as the other bids I have?
  • How would you price extra work, such as rotted boards found after the old material comes off?
  • Who will do the work: your own crew, or a subcontractor?
  • What are your license and insurance details, and where can I check them?
  • Who is my contract with, and what happens if the start date moves?
  • What reviews and references can I look at?
