Roof Repair, Overlay or Replacement? How to Get Bids You Can Compare

Otter Quotes · Updated November 2026

← All Guides

Three bids are not a comparison if each one prices a different job. A repair, an overlay and a full replacement are three different jobs. Have each contractor price the repair and the replacement as separate options for the same roof, and write down what each price includes, what it leaves out and which warranty applies.

What is the difference between a repair, an overlay and a tear-off?

A repair fixes part of a roof. An overlay, which roofers also call a recover, puts a new roof covering on top of the old one. A tear-off removes the old roof covering, repairs any damaged deck underneath and then installs a new covering. Bids for these jobs are not interchangeable, even when they are for the same house.

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (ARMA) defines the last two this way. Roof replacement is "the process of removing the existing roof covering, repairing any damaged substrate, and installing a new roof covering." A roof recover is "the process of installing an additional roof covering on an existing roof covering."

What should a roof repair bid say?

A repair bid should say exactly what will be replaced and how the repaired area will be finished. GAF's technical bulletin on replacing storm-damaged shingles confirms it is possible to replace individual shingles, and that hidden damage to neighboring shingles is hard to judge. The bid should name the area it covers.

The same bulletin gives specifics a bid can address. Nails removed from surrounding shingles must be replaced, and surrounding shingles must be resealed by hand. It adds that shingles that were nailed through, for example to hold a tarp, must be replaced, and that caulking those nail holes is not a permanent repair. It also notes that starter strips, ridge caps and other accessory products may be needed to complete a repair.

If the damage is limited to one slope, GAF says replacing just that roof plane may be possible. Find out whether the contractor is quoting a few shingles, a section or a whole slope, and why. Ask what their warranty says about repair work. Our guide to tarps on a roof while you wait for repairs covers the temporary steps.

When might a bid recommend tearing off instead of covering over?

A bid may recommend a tear-off when the deck or the existing layers make covering over a poor choice. ARMA says a roof is likely to need replacement if it has two layers, sagging, damaged deck boards, or shingles too uneven to flatten. A contractor who recommends a tear-off should tell you which of these conditions they saw.

ARMA also says a recover is appropriate only if the roof deck stays structurally sound and holds fasteners well, and that rotten, warped or otherwise damaged boards must be replaced before new shingles go on. Among the benefits of replacement it lists the chance to inspect the roof deck and repair any damage, and it notes that some roofing professionals insist on replacement because it puts a completely new roofing system on the house.

Find out what the contractor found and how they found it. A recommendation to tear off is easier to weigh when it names a condition you can look at yourself. Our guide to change orders and hidden damage explains how to get a price in the contract for damage that only shows up once the old roof is off.

When does an overlay fit?

Whether an overlay fits depends on what you can inspect, what local codes allow and what the manufacturer recommends. ARMA notes that local building codes may limit your options, and that generally they do not allow more than two roof coverings on a building. It tells readers to consult the shingle manufacturer for specific recommendations.

ARMA also says that most manufacturers do not recommend installing new shingles over existing laminated shingles, and that impact resistance ratings for asphalt shingles "may be affected when new shingles are installed over existing shingles." Those are reasons to check with the manufacturer, not only the contractor, before you accept a bid that covers over the old roof.

So find out how many layers are on your roof now, what the bid assumes about the deck, and whether the shingle manufacturer allows the new shingles to be installed over the existing ones. Have the contractor put the answer in the bid, and settle what happens to the price if the deck turns out to need work.

How do you compare a repair bid, an overlay bid and a tear-off bid?

Have each contractor price the same roof as separate options, and put each option in its own column. A repair, an overlay and a tear-off do not compete on total price alone. They differ in scope, in what you can inspect and in warranty terms.

What if an insurance claim is involved?

If an insurance claim is involved, check your policy and ask your insurer how it handles repairs before any work begins. Those questions belong with your insurer, not with a contractor. A contractor can still give you a written scope and price for the work.

Whoever pays, get the scope in writing and keep it. The National Roofing Contractors Association (NRCA) advises homeowners to insist on a written proposal with complete descriptions of the work and specifications, plus start and completion dates and payment procedures.

How do you choose among the contractors?

Choose among contractors the way you would for any roof. NRCA advises homeowners to ask for proof of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage, to ask for client references and a list of completed projects, and to keep a healthy skepticism about the lowest bid, since price is only one criterion.

For a repair, one more question matters. Does the contractor stand behind repair work in writing, and for how long? Pick the contractor who will put the scope, the price and the warranty on paper.

What to ask every roofing contractor

Copy this list into a note and send it to each contractor before you compare totals.

  • Please price a repair and a replacement for the same roof as separate options.
  • What exactly will you replace in the repair: which shingles, which section, which slope?
  • How do you treat the shingles around a repair: are nails replaced and surrounding shingles hand-sealed?
  • How many layers are on my roof now?
  • If you recommend a tear-off, which condition did you see that led to it?
  • If you propose covering over the old roof, does the shingle manufacturer allow it, and does that change the warranty?
  • What do you assume about the deck, and what is the price per sheet or per board if the deck is damaged?
  • What warranty comes with a repair, and what warranty comes with a full roof?
  • What does the bid leave out, and how are extras priced and agreed?
  • What are your license number and proof of insurance?
  • Who does the work: your own crew or a subcontractor?
  • When can you start, and how long will the job take?

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a roof overlay and a tear-off?

ARMA defines a roof recover, or overlay, as installing an additional roof covering on an existing roof covering. Roof replacement, or tear-off, removes the existing covering, repairs any damaged substrate and installs a new covering. A tear-off exposes the whole deck. An overlay does not. Have each bid say which one it prices.

How many layers of roofing can a house have?

ARMA notes that local building codes may limit the options, and that generally they do not allow more than two roof coverings on a building. Find out how many layers your roof has now and what your local rules allow, and have the bid state whether the old layers come off.

Can a few damaged shingles be replaced without replacing the whole roof?

GAF's technical bulletin confirms it is possible to replace individual damaged shingles, though hidden damage to neighboring shingles is hard to judge. If damage is confined to one plane of the roof, replacing just that plane may be possible. Have the contractor say what area the repair bid covers and why.

Does covering over my old shingles affect the new shingle warranty?

It may. Check with the shingle manufacturer, not only the contractor. ARMA reports that most manufacturers do not recommend installing new shingles over existing laminated shingles, and it tells readers to consult the manufacturer for specific recommendations. Have the contractor state in writing whether the manufacturer allows it.

Should I get a repair bid and a replacement bid from the same contractor?

Yes, it helps. Have each contractor price both for the same roof as separate options, with the scope, exclusions and warranty written out. That lets you compare the repair against the replacement from the same company, and against what other contractors offer for each.
