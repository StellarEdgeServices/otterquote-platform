Otter Quotes or Angi: Which Fits Your Exterior Project?

Otter Quotes · Updated November 2026

← All Guides

Angi and Otter Quotes both connect you with contractors, but the first step differs. Angi's help page for pros says a request is matched with no more than five pros. On Otter Quotes, we write one scope of work and send it to multiple contractors, who may reply with written bids.

Otter Quotes is not affiliated with Angi. The facts on this page about Angi come from one Angi help page written for pros and one Angi page written for homeowners, which we opened on November 4, 2026, and each statement links to the page it came from. The suggestions about who may prefer which site are ours. Angi can change those pages at any time. We did not open the pages you see when you request a service there, so this guide does not describe that step by step. One more thing to weigh: contractors pay Otter Quotes a fee on the bid you accept, so we benefit when you choose a contractor on our site.

What is the main difference between Otter Quotes and Angi?

The main difference is what you end up comparing. Angi's help page for pros says that each time a homeowner submits a project request, Angi matches the homeowner with no more than five pros. On Otter Quotes, the contractors who choose to bid all price the same written scope of work.

On Angi, the people you hear from are the pros matched to your request. Angi's page for homeowners says you can request and compare quotes from pros near you, and that for some services, such as plumbing and cleaning, you can see a price and book in an instant. On Otter Quotes, the replies are written bids on one scope of work, so you can read them side by side. They can still differ in materials, warranties, schedule and what they leave out, and those differences are the part to read closely.

How many contractors will I hear from?

On Angi, the help page says a request is matched with no more than five pros. That is a ceiling stated on the page, not a promise of five. The page also says the number of pros who get a lead depends on the type of lead and the pro's service location.

On Otter Quotes, we send your scope of work to multiple contractors, and those who choose to bid send written bids. The number of bids can vary by job and area.

On either site, what matters is whether each reply is specific enough to compare.

What do contractors pay on each one, and why does it matter to you?

Contractors pay for different things on each. Angi's help page says a lead is created when a pro and a homeowner both show interest in connecting, and the pro is then charged the lead fee. The page also says lead fees change with the task, the homeowner's location and demand for the work.

The page also says a pro is not charged for an Opportunity that does not become a lead, and it describes a subscription option, under which a pro is charged for leads each month.

On Otter Quotes, contractors do not pay per lead. Our Terms say Otter Quotes currently charges a service fee of 5% of the accepted bid amount, that the fee is charged to contractors, not directly to homeowners, and that it is charged once the contract is signed by both you and the contractor.

A payment model does not tell you whether a particular contractor is good, and we are not saying it does. It tells you when a contractor's main charge applies. On Angi, per its page, that is when you and the pro both show interest, or, on a subscription, monthly. On Otter Quotes, the platform fee is charged once the contract is signed. A contractor can also order a fuller roof report, which is a separate optional charge. Judge every contractor, on either site, by license, insurance, reviews, references and the written terms of the bid.

What happens to your contact details?

Angi's help page for pros says an Opportunity shows a pro the homeowner's name, general location and task, and that the pro can contact the homeowner directly once both show interest. It then becomes a lead, which the page says includes the homeowner's name, location, project details and contact information.

On Otter Quotes, contractors do not see your name, phone number or street address until you choose one. Before you choose, a contractor who is bidding sees your city and zip code and the scope of work. You compare the bids online first, and then decide. So on both sites, contact details are held back until you take a step. On Angi that step is showing interest in connecting with a pro, and on Otter Quotes it is choosing a bid.

Whichever site you use, decide before you start how you want to be contacted.

How does a bid on the same scope change the comparison?

A scope of work is a written list of the work to be done: what comes off, what goes on, which materials, which parts of the house, and what is not included. When every contractor prices the same list, a difference in price is easier to read, because you are not guessing whether two prices cover the same job.

If you order the aerial roof report, you get professional measurements that contractors use to bid. A contractor who needs more can order a fuller report. Our roof bid comparison worksheet gives you the rows to line up, and our comparison of competitive bids and a single referral covers a related choice.

What if the job is an insurance claim?

If you have an insurance estimate, you can give Otter Quotes a copy, and there is no cost to you. Contractors then bid on the same scope. The insurance guides on our site explain how ACV and RCV policies pay and how to compare bids. We do not work with your insurance company, negotiate claims or act as a public adjuster. What your policy covers and pays stays between you and your insurer.

For a roof, if you do not have an insurance estimate, you can upload measurements you already have or order an aerial roof report from us for as little as $22.63 up front. It is refunded in full when you complete a job through the platform; the conditions are in our Terms.

The Angi pages we opened do not discuss insurance claims, so this guide says nothing about how Angi handles them. If you use any site for a claim job, ask each contractor whether the price is in writing and whether it covers the same work as the other bids you are comparing. This guide does not tell you what your insurer will or should cover. For anything about your coverage, check your policy or ask your insurer. When might Angi suit you better?

Repairs are not our specialty and we cover only roofing, siding and gutters, so for a small repair or another trade you may want a site that handles it. Angi may also suit you if you want to talk with a pro before anyone writes a price.

Otter Quotes may suit an exterior project where you want written bids on the same scope, compared side by side, without a salesperson in your home. Our guide on how online roof bids work without a salesperson shows the steps.

What should you ask before you choose anyone?

Copy this list and use it with any contractor, on any site.

  • Is your price in writing, and does it say what is included and what is not?
  • Does the bid name the products by brand and line, and say who backs each warranty?
  • Is this price for the same work and the same measurements as the other bids I have?
  • How would you price extra work, such as rotted boards found after the old material comes off?
  • Who will do the work: your own crew, or a subcontractor?
  • What are your license and insurance details, and where can I check them?
  • Who is my contract with, and what happens if the start date moves?
  • What reviews and references can I look at?
