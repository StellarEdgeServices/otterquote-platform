Can Gutters Be Installed in Winter? What Your Bid Should Say

Otter Quotes · Updated November 2026

← All Guides

Gutters can be installed in cold weather, but sealants have temperature limits, and NDSU Extension ties ice dams to heat loss and ventilation in the attic. Ask each bidder which sealant they use and what its data sheet allows, and what the bid does and does not cover about the heat and ventilation problems that cause ice at the eaves.

Is it okay to install gutters in the winter?

It can be, and the answer depends on the sealant and the surface. Gutter work uses sealants, and sealant makers set the temperature range for each product. One maker's gutter sealant lists an application range down to 0 degrees Fahrenheit. Another sealant maker says caulking is best applied between 40 and 80 degrees.

Those two statements describe different products, so neither tells you what your contractor will use. A bid that says only "gutters installed" leaves the choice of sealant, and the decision to work in the cold, to the crew on the day. Our guide to gutter bid comparison covers the other line items. This guide covers only what winter adds.

What do sealant makers say about cold weather?

They give temperature ranges and surface rules. DAP's data sheet for its gutter and flashing sealant, dated April 24, 2026, says its application temperature range is between 0 and 140 degrees Fahrenheit, that the surface must be clean, dry and free of old caulk and dirt, and that full cure takes 24 hours.

That is one product. Tremco's general guidance, written for sealants broadly, says caulking is optimally applied between 40 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit, warns about condensation when the air is below the dew point, and says low temperatures slow the cure of most caulks. Ask your contractor to name the sealant and put its data sheet range in the answer.

A bid that names the sealant lets you look up its data sheet yourself, instead of taking a general reassurance on trust. If the bid says only "seams sealed," ask which product, and ask what temperature range the maker lists for it.

Why does a frosty or wet surface matter?

Because the sealant has to touch the metal. DAP says the surface must be clean and dry, and Tremco says to clear dew, ice, frost or water from the surface and let it dry thoroughly. Tremco also says condensation on a surface can block the sealant's adhesion.

Tremco also advises protecting surfaces from rain and snow with a tarp or similar covering. For gutters, the practical questions are what the crew does when the fascia or the gutter is damp or frosty, and whether work stops when the forecast turns. Ask for the answer in writing, and ask who decides. DAP also says to wait at least 30 minutes before exposing the sealant to water, so rain right after installation is another question.

Will new gutters stop ice dams?

Not by themselves. NDSU Extension says the key to preventing ice dams is minimizing heat loss into the attic, and names improper attic ventilation as another cause. It also notes that sun can warm a gutter and the edge of the soffit enough to melt snow, creating icicles and ice dams along the roof edge.

NDSU explains that warm air escaping into the attic melts roof snow, and criticizes treating the symptoms of ice dams with heat tape instead of dealing with the cause. A gutter contractor may not do attic work, so ask what the bid covers and what it leaves out. Our guide to roof ventilation in your replacement bid and our guide to underlayment and ice barrier cover the roof-side questions.

Should a gutter bid include heat cable?

Only if you and the contractor agree it solves the problem you have. NDSU Extension says heat tape treats the symptoms of ice dams rather than the cause, and recommends addressing the cause. A bid that includes heat cable should say what it is for, how it is powered and what it costs as a separate line.

If a contractor proposes it, ask what problem it is meant to fix at your house and what the contractor thinks is causing it. Ask also whether the cause is a roof, attic or ventilation issue that is outside the gutter bid. A reasonable answer names the cause. An answer that skips straight to the product is a reason to ask for a second bid.

What to ask every gutter contractor about winter installation

Put the same questions to every bidder and keep the answers in writing so you can set them side by side. Several of these follow what DAP, Tremco and NDSU Extension describe: sealant temperature, surface conditions and the real causes of ice at the eaves.

Copy this list into a note and send it to each contractor before you compare totals.

  • Which sealant will you use on seams, end caps and outlets, and what is its temperature range?
  • What will the crew do if the gutter or fascia is frosty, damp or snowy?
  • How long does the sealant need to cure before it can get wet, and what is the plan if rain or snow is forecast?
  • Who decides whether the weather is acceptable on the day, and what happens to the schedule?
  • What does the bid say about ice dams, and what does it leave to the roof or the attic?
  • Is heat cable included, and what problem is it meant to fix?
  • If the fascia is damaged when the old gutters come off, how is the repair priced and agreed?
  • Will you put the sealant, the weather plan and the ice-dam scope in the written proposal?

Frequently asked questions

Can you install gutters in the winter?

It depends on the sealant and the conditions. One maker's gutter sealant lists an application range from 0 to 140 degrees Fahrenheit, while Tremco says caulking is best applied between 40 and 80 degrees. Ask each bidder which sealant they use, what its data sheet says and what they do if the surface is frosty.

What temperature is too cold to seal gutters?

There is no single number. DAP lists 0 to 140 degrees Fahrenheit for its gutter and flashing sealant, and Tremco says caulking is optimally applied between 40 and 80 degrees. Check the data sheet for the product in your bid, and ask the contractor to write down the limit they work to.

Do new gutters prevent ice dams?

Not by themselves. NDSU Extension says the key is minimizing heat loss into the attic and names improper attic ventilation as another cause. It also notes that sun can warm a gutter and soffit edge enough to melt snow. Ask what the bid covers and what it leaves to the roof or attic.

Should I pay for heat cable on gutters?

Ask what problem it is meant to fix. NDSU Extension says heat tape treats the symptoms of ice dams rather than the cause. If a contractor proposes it, ask them to explain the cause at your house, price the cable as a separate line and say who handles any attic or roof work.

What should a winter gutter bid say?

It should name the sealant and its temperature range, say what the crew does with frosty or wet surfaces, say who decides to postpone for weather and state what it does and does not cover about ice at the eaves. Ask every bidder the same questions so you can compare the answers.
