What Should Your Roof Replacement Bid Say About Drip Edge?

Otter Quotes · Updated November 2026

← All Guides

Drip edge is metal flashing along the edges of your roof. GAF's technical bulletin says it directs water away from the edge of the roof. A bid may name it in one word, or not at all. Ask each contractor to write down whether it is new, what metal it is and where it goes, before you compare totals.

What is drip edge, and where does it go on a roof?

Drip edge is a metal flashing installed along the edges of a shingled roof. GAF's technical bulletin says it "directs water away from the edge of the roof" and helps stop water from reaching behind the soffit or siding. The bottom edges are the eaves. The sloped side edges are the rakes.

ARMA, the asphalt shingle manufacturers' trade group, says drip edges provide water shedding at a shingled roof's eave, gable and rake. It calls them "a common way to help mitigate the potential rotting of wood materials."

A bid that says only "drip edge" leaves open whether it covers the eaves and the rakes, whether it is new or reused, and what metal it is.

Does the building code require drip edge?

It depends on where you live. The model International Residential Code (IRC) says a drip edge "shall be provided at eaves and rake edges of shingle roofs." Your state or local government adopts its own version, and that version controls. Ask your building department, or each bidder in writing, what applies to your roof.

For reference, a City of Pacific, Missouri code page headed 2018 IRC and a City of Bozeman, Montana handout applying the 2021 IRC give the same details. Adjacent pieces overlap at least 2 inches. The metal extends at least 1/4 inch below the roof sheathing and at least 2 inches back onto the roof deck. It is mechanically fastened at no more than 12 inches on center.

Those handouts also describe the order of the layers. At the eaves, underlayment laps over the drip edge; at the rakes, the drip edge sits on top of the underlayment. The 2024 edition may word this differently, and a state or local government may adopt another edition or change the text. South Carolina's regulation, for one, limits it to asphalt shingle roofs "where required by the manufacturer." In Indiana, the 2020 Indiana Residential Code, based on the 2018 IRC, applies. This page does not say what your area requires.

Manufacturers weigh in too. ARMA strongly recommends drip edge on every shingled roof and notes that local codes may require it. GAF says it strongly recommends drip edges at the rakes and eaves on all GAF shingle roofs and requires non-corroding metal ones "when required by local building code." What should a bid say about drip edge material and installation?

Ask for four things in writing: the metal, where the drip edge runs, how it is overlapped and fastened, and how far the shingles extend past it. The sources below do not agree on every detail, so the bid should say which instructions the contractor follows and which document they come from.

Detail What the sources say What to ask
Metal GAF recommends non-corroding metal such as aluminum or galvanized steel. ARMA says corrosion-resistant material. GAF says "L" metal is not suitable as a drip edge. Which metal, and what shape of edge piece?
Where it runs The 2018 and 2021 code handouts say eaves and rake edges of shingle roofs. Eaves and rakes, all the way around?
Overlap and depth The same handouts say 2-inch overlap, 1/4 inch below the sheathing, 2 inches back on the deck. Does your work follow my local code's version?
Fastening The code handouts say no more than 12 inches on center. ARMA and Owens Corning's 2024 bulletin say 8 to 10 inches. ARMA says not to nail the vertical portion. How is it fastened, and at what spacing?
Shingle overhang GAF says shingles should extend 3/8 to 3/4 inch past it. Owens Corning's bulletin says 1/4 to 3/4 inch. Whose instructions, for the shingle in my bid?

ARMA says its page is informational and that the user is responsible for complying with applicable laws, and Owens Corning's bulletin notes Florida code differences. Treat the table as a list of questions, not a standard for your roof.

How does drip edge fit with underlayment and an ice barrier?

The edge is where several layers meet, so the order matters. Along the eaves, the 2018 and 2021 code handouts say underlayment goes over the drip edge. Along the rakes, it goes under the drip edge. ARMA and Owens Corning's bulletin describe the same order, and Owens Corning describes a different method for Florida.

If your bid also lists underlayment or an ice barrier, ask each contractor to say how the edge is built up: which layer goes first, and which goes over it. The bid does not need a diagram. It needs the same layers named for every bidder, so a lower price is not just a shorter list.

Nothing in this section says your roof needs any particular layer. It tells you which question to ask, so each bid answers it in the same terms.

Will my shingle warranty cover the drip edge?

Read the warranty before you assume it does. GAF's bulletin says its shingle limited warranty "only covers manufacturing defects in the shingles," not the drip edge or damage from installing it. For its Silver Pledge and Golden Pledge limited warranties, GAF also says the eaves need a non-corroding metal drip edge.

That is GAF's warranty only. Other manufacturers write their own terms, and an enhanced warranty can add its own requirements. If your bid names an enhanced warranty, ask the contractor what it requires at the roof edges and where that is written. Our guide to manufacturer-backed roof warranties shows what else to compare.

Can the contractor reuse my old drip edge?

Sometimes, but GAF's reuse bulletin says GAF recommends replacing all metal flashings whenever possible. It says existing drip edge may be reused when it is in "like new" condition, meaning no signs of excessive wear, damage or deterioration. Ask each bidder whether yours will be replaced or reused, and why.

The same bulletin lists corrosion, rust, scoring, tearing or any physical damage from the tear-off as examples of damage. It says existing plastic flashing is not acceptable and must be replaced with non-corroding metal flashing. It says damage due to reusing existing flashings is not covered by the limited warranty, and that approval to reuse metal flashing must be obtained in advance if enhanced warranty coverage is sought.

If wood under the edge turns out to be rotted once the old roof is off, the repair may arrive as a change order. Our guide to roof change orders and hidden damage covers how those are priced.

Why can two bids differ on drip edge?

Because the line is short and easy to leave vague. One bid may price new metal on every eave and rake. Another may reuse the existing metal. A third may not mention drip edge at all. All three can look complete, yet they describe different work, and a lower total may simply reflect a shorter list.

Before you decide one bid is cheaper, check that each names the same items in the same places. Our guides on why one roof bid can be much lower than the others and the roof replacement cost breakdown show what else to line up. NRCA, the National Roofing Contractors Association, advises homeowners to "insist on a written proposal" with complete descriptions of the work and specifications. For this line, that means the metal, the edges it covers and whether it is new. Our roofing contract checklist before you sign shows how to get that into the contract.

What to ask every roofing contractor about drip edge

Put the same questions to every bidder and keep the answers in writing, so you can set them side by side. Copy this list into a note and send it to each contractor before you compare totals. Short written answers are enough.

  • Will new drip edge go on all eaves and rake edges, or only some of them?
  • Is any of my existing drip edge being reused? If so, how is "like new" decided, and by whom?
  • What metal is the new drip edge, and what shape of edge piece will you use?
  • Is there plastic or "L" metal edging on my roof now, and what replaces it?
  • How is it overlapped and fastened, and which document (local code or the shingle maker) says so?
  • At the eaves and the rakes, does the drip edge go over or under the underlayment, and where is that written?
  • How far will the shingles extend past the drip edge, according to which instructions?
  • Does the shingle warranty in your bid require anything at the roof edges, and where is that written?
  • Is drip edge priced as its own line, or inside the roof price? If rotted wood is found at the edge, how is the repair priced and agreed in writing?

Frequently asked questions

Does a new roof need drip edge?

It depends on your local code. The model code says a drip edge "shall be provided at eaves and rake edges of shingle roofs," and your state or local government adopts its own version. ARMA says drip edge is strongly recommended on all shingled roofs. Ask your building department, or each bidder in writing, what applies.

Is drip edge the same as flashing?

Drip edge is one kind of flashing. GAF's bulletin calls it a metal flashing that directs water away from the roof edge. Other flashings sit at walls, chimneys and pipes. A bid can list them separately, so ask each contractor to say in writing which flashings are new and which are reused.

Can my old drip edge be reused?

GAF recommends replacing all metal flashings whenever possible. Its bulletin says existing drip edge may be reused when it is in "like new" condition, and that existing plastic flashing must be replaced with non-corroding metal. Ask each bidder whether yours will be new or reused, and why.

Does my shingle warranty cover drip edge?

Check your warranty. GAF's bulletin says its shingle limited warranty "only covers manufacturing defects in the shingles," not the drip edge or damage from installing it. Other manufacturers word their warranties differently, so ask which warranty your bid names and what it requires at the roof edges.

What metal should drip edge be made of?

This page does not pick a metal. GAF recommends non-corroding metal such as aluminum or galvanized steel, and says "L" metal is not suitable as a drip edge. ARMA says corrosion-resistant material. Ask each bidder to name the metal in the written bid, so the bids are comparable.
