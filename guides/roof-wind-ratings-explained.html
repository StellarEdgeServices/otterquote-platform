Roof Wind Ratings Explained: What “110,” “130” and “No Limit” Really Promise

Otter Quotes · Updated October 2026

← All Guides

A shingle’s wind rating is two different things. A test class such as ASTM D3161 Class F is a lab result. The warranted wind speed is the manufacturer’s promise to cover shingles that blow off, and the top level of that promise depends on how the roof is installed. Ask each contractor which level they will register, in writing.

This guide covers GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed and Atlas. It does not say what your homeowners policy pays. That is a question for your policy and your insurer.

What is the difference between a wind test class and a wind warranty?

A test class is a lab label on the shingle. A wind warranty is a written promise, with a speed, a term and conditions. Only the warranty tells you what the manufacturer will stand behind if shingles blow off your roof, so it is the number to compare between bids.

Two ASTM tests show up on product pages. Professional Roofing magazine describes ASTM D3161 as a fan-induced test, with Class F tied to 110 mph, and ASTM D7158 as a test that compares uplift force with resistance, with Class H rated for design wind speeds up to about 190 mph (Professional Roofing). The same article says D3161 results do not directly correlate to wind speeds experienced in service. The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association also expresses D7158 classes as ultimate design wind speeds (ARMA).

GAF lists both Class F and Class H for Timberline HDZ, and Owens Corning’s data sheets list both for its Duration line (Owens Corning data sheet 10024195). Those are test results. A Class H label does not tell you the speed in a warranty.

What are the standard and top wind warranty levels for each brand?

Each brand sells wind coverage in levels. The standard level comes with the shingle. The top level needs the maker’s required installation and accessories. Figures below come from each manufacturer’s current pages and warranties, read in October 2026.

Why do the required accessories matter?

The top wind level is a system promise, not a shingle promise. If one required piece is missing, the manufacturer’s own wording says the lower level applies. The shingle can carry a 130 or 160 label and the warranty on your roof can still be the standard level.

Owens Corning’s warranty says that if all requirements for the enhanced level are not met, including strictly following its Enhanced Wind Application Instructions, then the standard wind warranty applies. GAF’s chart says Timberline HDZ is covered up to its 130 mph maximum only if it is installed with four nails per shingle and GAF starter strip products at the eaves and rakes. For installs that do not qualify, GAF’s maximum is 110 mph.

The insider point. On GAF Timberline HDZ, a roof installed without GAF starter strips at the eaves and rakes has a 110 mph wind warranty, not 130. On the other brands in the table, missing a required piece drops the roof from the top level to the standard level. The number on the bundle is the ceiling. The level on your roof is the one the installation qualifies for.

Starter shingles are the piece people skip. They sit along the eaves and rakes, the edges where wind gets under a roof first, and every brand in the table above names them. A bid that leaves them out is worth a question. We do not state prices here because they vary by job.

What does GAF’s “no limit” wind warranty require?

GAF’s WindProven Limited Wind Warranty has no maximum wind speed limit, a 15-year term, and no registration step. It requires LayerLock-labeled shingles with four nails per shingle, plus at least four qualifying GAF accessories: starter strips at the eaves and rakes, ridge cap shingles, roof deck protection, and either attic ventilation or a leak barrier.

On GAF’s comparison chart, WindProven is listed with its system warranties, not with the base Shingle & Accessory warranty, whose maximum is 130 mph with Special Installation. So “no limit” is not automatic. It depends on the shingle, the accessories and the warranty the contractor sets up. Our warranty tiers article explains how the tiers fit together.

How long does a shingle wind warranty last?

In the current documents we read, GAF, Owens Corning and CertainTeed each give a 15-year wind period. Atlas publishes its wind term in its warranty, which we did not read for this guide, so ask for it. A 15-year wind period is shorter than the “lifetime” wording used for the shingle itself.

That is another reason to get the wind level in writing. A promise that lasts 15 years should be named on the contract, with the product names behind it, so the paperwork is there if you need it.

What does a wind warranty not cover?

A wind warranty is a product promise about shingles that blow off. It is separate from your homeowners policy. This guide does not say whether your policy pays for wind or hail damage, and a wind warranty does not answer that question either. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

Each warranty has its own exclusions and registration rules, so read them before you sign. If you are not sure whether damage on your roof came from wind or hail, our article on hail versus wind roof damage explains the difference in plain terms.

What should I ask a roofing contractor about wind ratings?

Ask one question first: which wind warranty level will you register, in writing, and which accessories are in the bid? Then ask every contractor the same follow-ups so the answers line up side by side.

Questions to copy:

  • Which shingle line is in the bid, and which wind warranty level will you register for it? Please write the speed and the warranty name.
  • Which starter shingle is on the eaves, and is it on the rakes too?
  • Which hip and ridge product is in the bid, and is it the manufacturer’s own?
  • Which underlayment, ice and water barrier or ventilation product is in the bid?
  • How many nails per shingle will you use, and does that match the manufacturer’s requirement for the level you named?
  • Who files the warranty, by when, and will I get a copy of the confirmation?
  • Does the manufacturer’s current warranty for my install date say the same thing?

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a wind rating and a wind warranty?

A wind rating such as ASTM D3161 Class F or D7158 Class H is a lab test result. A wind warranty is the manufacturer's written promise to cover shingles that blow off, up to a stated speed, for a stated term. The warranted speed on your roof depends on the product and on how it was installed.

Does a 130 mph shingle always carry a 130 mph warranty?

No. On GAF Timberline HDZ, the 130 mph level applies only with four nails per shingle and GAF starter strips at the eaves and rakes. Without that special installation, the wind warranty is 110 mph. Other brands set their own conditions, so ask which level the bid will register.

How long does a shingle wind warranty last?

In the current warranties we read, GAF, Owens Corning and CertainTeed each give a 15-year wind period. Atlas sets its own term in its warranty. Terms change by edition and by install date, so read the manufacturer's current warranty on the day you sign.

What should I ask a roofing contractor about wind warranties?

Ask which wind warranty level the contractor will register, in writing, and which accessories are in the bid. Those usually include starter shingles at the eaves and rakes, matching hip and ridge caps, and underlayment. Ask for the product names, not just the word accessories.
