Roof Insurance Estimate: Key Terms

The terms that appear on a roof insurance estimate, and what to look at when comparing contractor bids.

Otter Quotes  ·  Updated October 2026

Otter Quotes is not a public adjuster. We do not prepare, file, interpret, negotiate or settle insurance claims, and we do not advise you on your coverage or on what your insurer should pay. Your claim stays between you and your carrier.

Insurance adjusters and roofing contractors look at the same storm damage and can produce estimates that differ. Differences can come from different measurement methods and different line-item pricing, and from the fact that an adjuster prepares the insurer's estimate while a contractor prepares a bid for the work.

Who Is Involved

After a storm, different people look at the same roof for different reasons. An insurance adjuster represents the insurer and prepares the insurer's estimate. A roofing contractor prepares a bid for the work they would do. Otter Quotes does neither: we create a written scope of work for your project and send it to multiple contractors.

A contractor you invite can look at the roof and explain what they would include in their own bid.

Key Terms

These terms are listed in alphabetical order.

Actual cash value (ACV)

A way of valuing the roof that accounts for depreciation based on its age and condition.

Appraisal clause

A policy may include an appraisal clause, which describes a way to resolve a disagreement about the amount of a loss.

Declarations page

A summary of your coverage limits, deductible and coverage types.

Depreciation

A decrease in value because of age and wear.

Exclusions

A policy lists what it covers and what it excludes, such as wear and tear, in its own words.

Matching

The question of whether new materials will match undamaged sections in appearance.

Ordinance or law coverage

Coverage for the added cost of meeting current building codes when damaged property is repaired or replaced.

Public adjuster

A public adjuster (PA) is someone a homeowner hires to work on the homeowner's claim, rather than for the insurer. Licensing rules are set by each state; your state's Department of Insurance can tell you what applies. An insurance adjuster works for the insurer.

Re-inspection

A re-inspection is a term for a second inspection of a property after a first claim inspection.

Replacement cost value (RCV)

The cost to replace the damaged property with new materials of like kind and quality, with no deduction for depreciation.

Supplement

A supplement is a term used in claims for a request to revise an existing estimate, for example to add line items or change pricing.

What to Ask About a Contractor Bid

An insurer's written estimate and a contractor's bid are different documents with different purposes. Each is prepared with its own pricing and measurements, so line items can differ.

Ask each contractor to explain their bid line by line, and to tell you what it includes and excludes, such as:

  • Pricing: how each material and labor line is priced in their bid
  • Items that may be missing from a bid: pipe boots, ice and water shield, drip edge, valley flashing. Ask whether each is included.
  • Measurements: how the contractor measured the roof, and whether the square footage matches what other bids use
  • Code-related work: ask whether the bid includes any work your local building code requires

Bids on the same scope can still differ in materials, measurements and price. Ask each contractor to explain any difference you see.

When comparing bids, look at:

  • Squares of roofing material — one square = 100 sq ft; ask each contractor how they measured your roof
  • Material line items — synthetic underlayment vs. felt, drip edge, ridge cap shingles, starter strips, pipe flashing boots
  • Labor rates — tear-off and disposal, re-decking if needed, flashing replacement labor
  • Overhead and profit (O&P) — a line that some estimates and bids list separately; ask what it covers

Keep Your Own Record of the Damage

Your own record of the damage is useful to you: it shows each contractor the same condition, and it is yours to keep. Photograph and video the roof and property with timestamps turned on.

Stay on the ground; do not climb onto the roof. You can ask a contractor to look at the roof surface while preparing a bid. Photograph and video every area with visible damage:

  • Shingles: missing tabs, dents, cracks or curling edges
  • Flashings and penetrations: Chimney flashing, pipe boots, skylights, and valley flashing are areas to look at closely
  • Gutters and downspouts: Look for dents or dings
  • Soffit and fascia: Note any displaced, dented, or cracked sections
  • Interior water intrusion: Photograph any water stains, bubbling paint, or wet insulation visible from inside, particularly in the attic

Create a folder with all photos organized by area. You can share the folder with contractors so each one bids on the same condition.

Photo and Record Checklist

  • Photographs of damaged shingles from multiple angles
  • Photos of flashing, pipe boots, and all roof penetrations
  • Photos of gutters and downspouts
  • Photos of any soffit, fascia, or siding damage
  • Interior attic photos showing any moisture or daylight intrusion

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between ACV and RCV on a roof insurance claim?

ACV (Actual Cash Value) is a way of valuing a roof that accounts for its age and wear. RCV (Replacement Cost Value) is the cost to replace damaged material with new material of like kind and quality, with no deduction for depreciation.

Does Otter Quotes help with my insurance claim?

No. Your claim stays between you and your insurer. What Otter Quotes does is create a written scope of work for your project, send it to multiple contractors, and let you compare their bids online.

What is a roof insurance supplement?

A supplement is a term used for a request to revise an insurance estimate, for example to add line items or change pricing. Otter Quotes does not prepare or submit supplements.

What should I compare when I read contractor bids?

Read each bid's scope of work, materials and warranty, and ask the contractor to explain anything that differs from another bid. Look at what each bid includes and excludes, how the roof was measured, and what is in writing. A contractor's bid is the contractor's own document.

What is a public adjuster?

A public adjuster (PA) is someone a homeowner hires to work on the homeowner's claim, rather than for the insurer. Licensing rules are set by each state; your state's Department of Insurance can tell you what applies.
