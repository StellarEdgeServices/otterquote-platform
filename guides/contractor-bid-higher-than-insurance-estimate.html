My Contractor's Bid Is Higher Than the Insurance Estimate. What Now?

Otter Quotes · Updated October 2026

← All Guides

A contractor's bid and your insurer's estimate are two documents written by different people, so they can describe different work and put different prices on it. Lay them next to each other, find each line that differs, and ask the contractor to explain each difference in writing. Questions about the estimate itself belong with your insurer.

Why can a contractor's bid be higher than the insurance estimate?

The estimate comes out of your claim, and the bid is a contractor's offer to do the work. Each lists work, quantities and materials and puts a price on them, and a different person wrote each list. A higher total can come from a longer list, a different material, a different quantity, a different price for the same item, or a mix.

None of these tells you by itself which document is right. A difference is a question to ask. If the words on the estimate are unfamiliar, our explainer on what "scope of loss" means covers the terms.

How do I compare the bid and the estimate side by side?

Put the two documents in one table with a row for each item, so every difference sits where you can see it. Follow these steps:

  1. Get the whole estimate. If you do not have every page, ask your insurer for a full copy.
  2. Ask for line items. A single lump sum hides what is and is not included.
  3. Make a row for each item. Add a row for every item either document lists, such as removal of the old material, the main material, small parts and trim, permit, disposal and cleanup.
  4. Fill in both columns. Copy what the estimate says and what the bid says for each item: quantity, unit, material and price.
  5. Mark each row. Write Same, Different, On the estimate only, or On the bid only.
  6. Send the questions. Ask the contractor to explain every row that is not Same, in writing.

Copy this table into a document or spreadsheet. Leave the two middle columns blank until you have both documents in front of you.

Item What the estimate lists What the bid lists Question to ask the contractor
Removal of the old material     Does the bid include removal, and how many layers?
Main material     Which manufacturer, product line and color?
Quantity and unit     Where did your measurements come from?
Small parts and trim     Which are listed, and which are new or reused?
Permit, disposal and cleanup     Who handles each, and are they in the total?
Warranty     What does it cover, for how long, and who stands behind it?
Start date and length of the job     What is written, and what could change it?

What should I ask the contractor about each difference?

Ask the contractor to say in writing why each line is in the bid and what it covers. Copy these questions:

  • Which lines in your bid are not on the insurer's estimate, and what does each one cover?
  • Which lines on the estimate are not in your bid, and why?
  • Where did your quantities and measurements come from?
  • Does your bid name a product line where the estimate names only a category?
  • What does your total include: removal, disposal, permit and cleanup?
  • What could change the price after I sign, and how would a change be written down?
  • Can you put every answer in writing, with a date?

Give every contractor the same questions, so the answers compare side by side.

What if the bid includes work the estimate does not list?

A line in the bid that is not on the estimate is a line the estimate does not price. Whether your insurer considers it, and how, is a question for your insurer. Otter Quotes takes no part in that conversation, and neither a contractor nor a website can know in advance how your insurer will respond.

Ask your insurer, in writing where you can, and keep a copy of every answer. Questions you can ask:

  • Can you walk me through how this line on your estimate is described and measured?
  • If a contractor's bid differs from your estimate, do you have a process for that, and who do I contact?
  • What, if anything, do you need from me or from a contractor if I want you to look at a bid?
  • Where do I find the dates and deadlines that apply to my claim?

Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke with and what they said.

What do I owe the contractor if the bid is higher than the insurance payment?

Your contract is between you and the contractor. How any insurance payment relates to the contract price is a question for your insurer and your policy, and Otter Quotes cannot answer it. Before you sign, ask the contractor to show in writing what you owe, when each payment is due and what each payment covers.

Read every page of the contract, and ask what any sentence about your insurance claim means before you sign.

Does a higher bid mean something is wrong?

Not by itself. A higher bid is a reason to ask what is different, not a reason to drop the contractor or to choose the bid. The estimate is one document to hold a bid against. Materials, warranty, schedule and who does the work belong in the comparison too.

With one bid, you hold one contractor's list and price against the estimate. With several bids on the same scope, you can compare side by side and see which differences show up in only one bid and which show up in more than one. Our roof bid worksheet lines bids up row by row.
