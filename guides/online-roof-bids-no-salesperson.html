What is an online roof bid?

An online roof bid is a written price for a described roofing job, sent by a contractor through a website instead of presented at your table. On a site that works from a shared scope of work, the homeowner and each contractor work from the same written description, and the bid states the materials, the price and the terms in writing.

In a bid from a salesperson, the contractor visits, looks over the job and presents a price in person. In an online bid, you read the price and the terms yourself, compare them with other bids and decide when you are ready.

How do online roof bids work, step by step?

Online bidding on a shared scope of work follows five steps. The job is described in a written scope of work. The scope goes to several contractors. Contractors who choose to bid send a bid on the same scope. You compare the bids side by side. You choose one contractor and sign online.

What should an online bid include?

A bid is only as useful as what it says in writing. The FTC says a written estimate should include a description of the work to be done, the materials, a completion date, the price and the contractor's contact information (FTC). Put each bid beside the others and check the same items in each.

ItemWhat to look for in the bid
The workA description that matches the scope of work you were given, line by line.
MaterialsThe product and the grade named, not just a category.
WarrantyWho stands behind it, what it covers and for how long, in writing.
ScheduleAn estimated start date and completion date.
Price and paymentsThe total and a payment schedule, with the amount due before work starts.
Contractor detailsName, address, phone and license number, if one is required.

What do I give up without a salesperson?

You do not get a salesperson walking you through the bid. You read each bid yourself, so you will want to write down every question before you sign and get each answer in writing. Ask each contractor whether the bid was priced from the written scope alone or after seeing the roof, and what could change the price.

Questions to copy:

  • Could the price change after work starts, and what would cause it?
  • Is anything in the scope of work not in your bid?
  • Who will do the work, your own crew or a subcontractor?
  • Which materials and which warranty does the bid name?
  • What is the payment schedule?

How do I check the contractor behind a bid?

The FTC says to confirm a contractor's license with your state or county government, ask for proof of insurance and look up what others say about the contractor (FTC). IBHS, a nonprofit research group, advises choosing a reputable, bonded and insured roofing contractor (IBHS).
