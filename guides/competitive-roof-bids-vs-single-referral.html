Competitive Roof Bids vs. a Single Referral: What Changes?

Otter Quotes · Updated November 2026

← All Guides

A single referral gives you one contractor someone already trusts. Competitive bids give you several written prices to compare on the same work. A referral is faster and comes with a person's word; bids show you what the job costs from more than one contractor. You can use both, by asking the referred contractor to bid alongside others.

What do you learn from a single referral?

You learn how one contractor treated someone you know, on that person's job. That is real information, especially about communication and cleanup. It does not tell you how the price compares, or whether the scope of work is the same as what another contractor would propose.

What do you learn from comparing bids?

Written bids on the same scope let you see differences you cannot see with one price: what is included, which materials are named, what the warranty says, and how the payment schedule works. The lowest number is not automatically the best bid. Comparison shows you what to ask about.

What does comparing bids cost you?

Time, mainly. You read more and decide more. If contractors each write their own scope, you may also have to work out whether they are pricing the same job. That is why a shared written scope helps.

When does a referral still make sense?

A referral makes sense when you already trust the person making it, you have checked the contractor's license where your state requires one, and you are comfortable with the price after reading a written estimate. It also makes sense when timing matters more than comparing.

How can I use both?

Ask the referred contractor for a written bid on the same scope as the others, and check every contractor the same way: license where required, insurance, reviews and warranty terms. The referral then becomes one good reason among several, not the only one.

Frequently asked questions

Is the lowest roof bid the best one?

Not automatically. A lower price can reflect a smaller scope, different materials or a shorter warranty. Compare what each bid includes before you compare totals.

Can a referred contractor bid along with others?

Yes. Ask for a written bid on the same scope as the others, so you are comparing the same work.

How many bids should I get?

There is no required number. More than one written bid gives you something to compare, and the right number depends on how much time you want to spend.
