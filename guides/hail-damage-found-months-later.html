I Found Hail Damage Months After the Storm. What Now?

Otter Quotes · Updated October 2026

← All Guides

Hail damage does not always show up right away. GAF's technical bulletin on hail says outward damage “may not be apparent until months or years later.” This guide covers what to do next: find the storm date, get bids on the work, and ask your insurer what your policy says. This guide states no deadline.

Can hail damage really show up months after the storm?

Yes. GAF, a shingle manufacturer, says in its technical bulletin on hail damage that “Outward damage may not be apparent until months or years later.” The bulletin adds that hidden damage to the shingle mat can “manifest and develop into cracks and tears at a later point in time” (GAF TAB-R-108).

So a roof that looked fine after the storm can still have hail damage that becomes visible later. It can also have other marks that are not from hail, which is why a written description from someone who has looked at the roof is worth asking for.

How can I tell hail damage from other marks?

IBHS, a nonprofit research group, publishes a short guide called “Is It Hail Damage?” It says spatter marks or impact indentations on other items around the building are good indicators that hail has occurred, and that you may find marks made by installation, man-made damage or natural weathering that hail did not cause (IBHS).

The guide sorts what it shows into three groups:

GroupWhat IBHS lists
Marks on other itemsDents in metal flashing and in air conditioner unit fins. Spatter, which IBHS says is not damage but appears when hail knocks oxidation or dirt off metal surfaces.
On the shinglesImpacts in a random pattern. IBHS says a bruise can usually be felt on real hail impacts on shingles.
Look-alikesGeneral granule loss common on shingle edges, blisters, mechanical marks and a manufacturer defect.

How do I find out which storm it was?

NOAA's Storm Events Database lists reported hail by county and date, and the Storm Prediction Center posts daily pages of reports. Search your county for the months before you found the damage. A report describes a place and a time, not your roof.

Our guide on how to find the date hail hit your house walks through the steps. Two limits are worth knowing. NOAA's search page says events take 3 months to appear in the database (NOAA). The Storm Prediction Center says its report list is preliminary and likely does not contain all severe weather reports for any particular event (SPC FAQ).

Is it too late to file an insurance claim?

This guide cannot say. Policies set their own notice and filing rules, and which date a claim uses is a question for your policy and your insurer. This guide states no deadline. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

Questions you can ask your insurer:

  • What does my policy say about notice, and in what form should it be given?
  • What does my policy say about protecting the property from further damage?

What can I do right now?

You can protect the house, write down what you found, look up the storm dates, get written bids on the work and read your policy. The list below takes those five steps in order. Where a source or one of our guides covers a step, the step links to it.

  1. Protect the house. IBHS's guide says to make temporary repairs to prevent further damage (IBHS). Stay off the roof and ask a roofer about a temporary cover.
  2. Write down what you found. Note what you saw, where, and the date you saw it, for your own records.
  3. Look up the storm dates. Use the NOAA steps in our date-of-hail guide.
  4. Get written bids. Give every contractor the same description of the work. Our roof bid worksheet shows how to compare them.
  5. Read your policy. Ask your insurer the questions above.

What should I ask a roofer who looks at the roof?

Ask for a written description of what the roofer saw, where they saw it and what work the roofer proposes. A bid that names the work, the materials and the price in writing is easier to compare with other bids and easier to keep with your records.

Questions to copy:

  • What did you see, and where on the roof and the house?
  • Did you see marks on metal items such as flashing, vents or gutters?
  • Which marks do you think are from hail, and which are from age or installation?
  • What materials and warranty does your bid name?
  • Can you put your answers in writing, with the date you looked?
