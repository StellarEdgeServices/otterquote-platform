Can You Replace Windows in Winter? What Your Bid Should Say

Otter Quotes · Updated November 2026

← All Guides

Yes, windows can be replaced in winter, but the bid should say how the contractor handles cold, wet and frozen conditions. The sources below give different guidance on temperature, so ask each bidder what limit they work to, what happens if the forecast drops, and how they keep the cold out of your house while a window is open.

Is it okay to replace windows in the winter?

It can be, if the installer follows the product's instructions for cold weather. Ply Gem, a window manufacturer, publishes cold-weather installation tips for contractors, and Pella Branch, a Pella window seller in Nebraska, describes how its crews install in winter. Neither says winter is off limits. Both describe conditions the crew has to control.

Those conditions are the point of this guide. A window replacement leaves an opening in your wall for a short time, and the sealants that close it have working conditions. Winter does not rule the job out. It makes the questions you ask before you sign more useful, because a bid that is silent on cold weather leaves each of those decisions to the crew on the day.

What temperature is too cold to install windows?

There is no single number, and the sources differ. Ply Gem says not to install unless temperatures will stay above freezing for the next 24 hours, so sealants do not freeze before they cure. Pella Branch says its caulking is rated for well below zero. Tremco, a sealant maker, says caulking is optimally applied between 40 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit.

Those statements come from three different companies describing different products and situations, so do not pick a winner. Ask each contractor which window brand and which sealants the bid names, what the maker's instructions say about temperature, and who decides on the day whether conditions are acceptable. Ask them to write the answer into the bid.

Why do frost and moisture matter when sealing a window?

Because sealant has to bond to the surface. Ply Gem says not to install on wet, frozen or frosty surfaces, because sealants will most likely not seal properly against them, and says the windows and doors should be clean and dry before installation. Tremco advises clearing dew, ice, frost or water from the surface and letting it dry before sealing.

Tremco also warns about the dew point. If the air temperature falls below it, condensation can form on a surface and block adhesion. It says to protect surfaces from rain and snow with a tarp or similar covering, and that low temperatures slow the cure of most caulks, so the schedule for later work should allow for it. Ask the contractor what the crew does if the opening is damp or frosty when they arrive.

How does an installer keep the cold out of the house?

By limiting how long an opening is exposed and how far the cold travels inside. Pella Branch describes closing the doors to the work room to limit cold air to a small area, installing the new window immediately after the old one comes out and the opening is prepped, and staging the replacement window nearby beforehand.

The same page describes drop cloths from the front door to each work area, and a drop cloth under the window if there is snow outside. It says installers use flashing tape around the window and spray foam around it from inside after shimming. That is one seller's description of its own process, so use it as a list of questions. Ask each bidder how they protect floors, how they limit exposure and what they use to seal the window.

What does a replacement window installation include, and where does cold weather change it?

For a replacement window set into the existing frame, Ply Gem describes sealing all joints of the original frame, removing old balance weights and pulleys, filling the balance pockets with insulating foam, and sealing and insulating the full perimeter to the original frame.

Each of those steps has a sealant or foam in it, which is why temperature and moisture matter. What should a window bid say about weather delays?

It should say who decides to postpone, what happens to the schedule and whether the price changes. This is a question for each contractor, not a fact from a source. A bid that is silent on delays leaves you guessing if a crew cannot start on the scheduled day, or if a job has to wait for better weather.

Ask whether the crew will remove the old windows only when all the new ones are on site, and what the plan is if a window arrives damaged or the wrong size. What to ask every window contractor about winter installation

Put the same questions to every bidder and keep the answers in writing so you can set them side by side. Several of these follow what Ply Gem, Pella Branch and Tremco describe: temperature, surface conditions, exposure time and protecting the inside of your home.

Copy this list into a note and send it to each contractor before you compare totals.

  • What temperature limit does the crew work to, and where does it come from?
  • Which window brand and which sealants and foam does the bid name?
  • What do the maker's instructions say about cold weather for those products?
  • What will the crew do if the opening is wet, frosty or snowy when they arrive?
  • How long will each opening be exposed, and are the windows on site before the old ones come out?
  • How will you keep the cold in one room, and how will you protect my floors?
  • What flashing tape and foam will you use around each window?
  • Who decides to postpone for weather, and what happens to the schedule and the price?
  • What is the plan if a window arrives damaged or the wrong size?
  • Will you put the temperature limit and the delay plan in the written proposal?
