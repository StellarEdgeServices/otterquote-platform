Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Williamson County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Ask any contractor for an itemized written estimate, proof of insurance, and local references. The process can take longer after a widespread storm, when repair schedules fill up. Patience and paperwork usually serve homeowners better than speed and pressure.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

Of those, 83 reports on 20 dates are an inch or larger, and 17 reports on 7 dates are two inches or larger. Three are filed at 3.00 inches. On May 26, 2024 the Leipers Fork entry says "Three inch hail was reported in the Leipers Fork area. Many vehicles sustained damage due to broken windows and car dents." That date has 25 hail reports, the largest tally for any single date in this county's file; May 26, 2024 has 11 and March 27, 2021 has 8, with 2.25 inches at College Grove the largest that day. May is the leading month at 49 reports on 7 dates, and March through June together hold 84 reports on 20 dates. Looking by year, 2025 has 43 reports on 6 dates, 2024 has 18 on 3 and 2021 has 13 on 3.

Six tornado entries fall on six dates, March 1, 2017 through March 30, 2025. The EF1 of March 1, 2017 covers 10.48 miles between the labels Berrys Chapel and Nolensville. On timestamps. Dates follow NOAA's records; its format guide says times are "usually in LST" (local standard time), and every Williamson County row counted here is stamped CST. That is one hour off the wall clock while daylight time is in force. The Nolensville hail entry ("Estimated 2 inch hail was reported southwest of Nolensville") shows the effect: stamped 11:00 p.m. CST on March 30, 2025, it would read March 31 on a wall clock, so the 29 hail dates above are NOAA's count by NOAA's clock and a local clock would make it 30. The 2026 file used here is the one NOAA created on September 18, 2026; NOAA says it updates the current year's file each month, so the 2026 counts can change. Nothing here describes a particular house or predicts a storm.

Permit offices and state licensing

More than one office answers for roofs in Williamson County, and the address decides which. For the unincorporated county, Williamson County Building Codes, 1320 West Main Street, Suite 400, Franklin, 615-790-5718, 8:00 am to 4:30 pm Central, posts a memo dated August 1, 2025, "Code Requirements for Roof Repair or Replacement". The memo states "a building permit and inspection is not required for residential roof repairs or replacements", then adds that the work "shall comply with adopted codes and ordinances, including Chapter 9/Roof Assemblies of the 2021 IRC" and that materials "shall be installed in accordance with the manufacturer's installation instructions."

In the City of Franklin, the Building and Neighborhood Services Department states "A building permit is required for all new construction, additions, renovations, decks, pools and most repair work" and gives 615-794-7012 for questions about whether a project needs one. The Franklin department's hours are 7:30am to 5:00pm Central, Monday through Friday. The page does not name roofing, so put the question to the department before assuming either way. An applicant whose property lies in a Historic Preservation Overlay must contact the Historic Preservation Planner in the Planning and Sustainability Department before any construction permit is issued. The page lets a homeowner pull a permit, and in the page's own words, "If the cost of the work exceeds $25,000 a State of Tennessee licensed contractor must pull the permit." Brentwood, Fairview, Nolensville and Thompson's Station also appear above; this page did not read their permit pages, so ask the office that serves your address.

Tennessee's licensing pages split by county. The Board for Licensing Contractors' Home Improvement page, read in October 2026, applies to counties that adopted the law and lists nine: Bradley, Davidson, Hamilton, Haywood, Knox, Marion, Robertson, Rutherford and Shelby. Williamson is absent from that list. Above that tier, the Board's contractor page ties a state license to prime-contractor work of $25,000 or more, and says the license is needed before "bidding, offering to engage, or negotiating a price". A Verify a License search is linked from the Board's pages; it was not run for this page, so run it yourself before relying on any license number.

Before you request bids in Williamson County

- A written estimate from your insurer, if you hold one, saved where each bidder can see it.

- Your address: inside the City of Franklin, inside another town, or in the unincorporated county.

- A question for each bidder about who applies for a permit, if one is needed, and whether its number goes into the contract.

- A question about the manufacturer's installation instructions, which the county memo mentions.

- A check of whether the contract total is under $25,000 or at $25,000 or more, then the license number each bidder gives you, looked up in the Verify a License search.
