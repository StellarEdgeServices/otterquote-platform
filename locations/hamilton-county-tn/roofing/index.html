A severe storm in Hamilton County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

The East Tennessee climate and your roofing

Hamilton County is part of the Eastern Grand Division defined in Tennessee Code Annotated section 4-1-202. NOAA's Tennessee state climate summary says temperatures decrease as elevation increases, averaging a 3 degree F decline per 1,000 feet. It describes the Cumberland Plateau as averaging 2,000 feet in elevation and Smoky Mountains peaks as reaching 6,000 feet, and it says these higher areas are cooler than lower-elevation areas.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Hamilton County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Ask any contractor for an itemized written estimate, proof of insurance, and local references. The process can take longer after a widespread storm, when repair schedules fill up. Patience and paperwork usually serve homeowners better than speed and pressure.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

NOAA's record for Hamilton County

Hail first. The Storm Events Database files 19 hail reports under Hamilton County across 11 dates, the earliest March 1, 2017 and the latest May 12, 2025, and nothing at all for 2016, 2018, 2019, 2020 or 2021. All but one measure an inch or more across. Two sit at the top, 1.75 inches: a March 1, 2017 report labeled Chickamauga, whose narrative says "Golf ball sized hail was reported at Chattanooga." The other is a June 25, 2023 report labeled Brainerd. Three more come in at 1.25 inches, labeled Salem (March 21, 2017), Rathburn (April 10, 2025) and Montlake (May 12, 2025). Eighteen of the 19 fall between March and June, with May leading at 7 reports on 3 dates. The year 2025 supplies 11 reports on 5 dates, and May 2 of that year, with 4 reports, is the single heaviest date.

The wind record is larger. Between June 15, 2016 and June 22, 2026, NOAA files 135 thunderstorm wind reports under Hamilton County on 78 dates; an instrument stands behind 8 of them and an estimate behind the other 127. Every month shows up except October. June leads with 38 reports on 16 dates, then July with 23 on 16 dates, then May with 21 on 12, while March through August together account for 120 reports on 69 dates. Three dates are level at 6 reports apiece: June 20, 2019, June 7, 2025 and June 22, 2026.

The top estimate is 78 knots, labeled White Oak, on June 7, 2025. Its narrative reads "Several large trees snapped at their trunks on Mountain Creek Road by potent thunderstorm wind gusts." Two 70-knot estimates share that date, labeled Ooltewah and Collegedale Arpk. For the second, the narrative reports branches "up to 10 inches in diameter" torn off by the wind and firefighters sent to a home where a branch was "damaging the porch roof of the home." The highest instrument reading is 59 knots on August 7, 2023, filed under the airport station label "(CHA)Lovell Fld Chat". A NOAA label points to a nearby place or station. It is not a street address.

Outside the thunderstorm count, the file has one High Wind entry and one Strong Wind entry for Hamilton. The High Wind entry, September 11, 2017, estimates 50 knots and says "Several trees and power lines were reported down across the county." The Strong Wind entry, December 23, 2022, lists a measured 37 knots and says "A wind gust to 43 mph was measured at the KCHA ASOS."

The tornado side of the file holds two entries. An EF0 dated March 2, 2023 and labeled New Pt to Georgetown covers 2.12 miles and, per its narrative, "lifted just over the Meigs County line". The other is an EF3 dated April 12, 2020, stamped 10:19 p.m. EST and spanning 12.75 miles between the labels East Ridge and Collegedale Arpk. Its narrative opens on the south side of Fort Oglethorpe in Catoosa County, runs across the Tennessee and Georgia state line toward East Brainerd Road, reaches Ooltewah, and "eventually dissipated in southwestern Bradley County."

Reading the dates. Dates follow NOAA's records; its format guide says times are "usually in LST" (local standard time), and every Hamilton County row counted here is stamped EST. During daylight time a stamp therefore trails the wall clock by an hour: 10:19 p.m. EST on April 12, 2020 was 11:19 p.m. EDT on the wall. Two Hamilton wind entries, stamped 11:20 p.m. EST on July 8, 2016 and 11:00 p.m. EST on May 22, 2020, would land on July 9 and May 23 by a wall clock. The 2026 file is preliminary, and NOAA's README says the current year is refreshed monthly. This is a weather record for the county as a whole and says nothing about any one house or any coming storm.

Offices, permits and licenses

Where your address sits decides who answers the permit question. Hamilton County's Building Inspection Department, 4005 Cromwell Road in Chattanooga, 423-209-7860, open 8:00 A.M. to 4:00 P.M. Eastern, states "A building permit is required for any new, alteration, addition or repair to a structure." The exceptions it lists (storage buildings, fences, finish work like painting and carpeting, and similar items) leave roofing out. Its page also carries a directory of inspection offices for Chattanooga, Collegedale, East Ridge, Lakesite, Red Bank, Signal Mountain, Soddy-Daisy and Lookout Mountain, prefaced by "Inspections and forms may change depending on where your building is located." Ridgeside and Walden do not issue their own construction permits, so those addresses use the county's forms.

Inside Chattanooga, the Land Development Office's Residential Modification Permit page names the job outright. The permit is for "modifications to existing structures that don't alter the footprint", covering single-family units where there is a remodel of the interior, "replacing an existing deck, repairing a porch, re-roofing, windows, exterior doors, or siding replacement." Applying means logging in to the city's online permit portal, and the page lists 423-643-5894. It sets out no contractor-license rule, so ask the office who may apply.

Two Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors pages, read in October 2026, set the state's dollar bands. The Home Improvement page reads "A Home Improvement license is required for residential remodeling projects from $3,000 to $24,999", limited to counties that adopted the law, and Hamilton is third of the nine it names; the page counts roofing as home improvement work. At the top band, the contractor page ties a license to "projects of $25,000 or more". It links a Verify a License search that was not used for this page, so that lookup is yours to run.

Before you collect bids on a Hamilton County roof

- The written estimate from your insurer, if you have one, so every bidder starts from the same sheet.

- Which office serves your address: Chattanooga, one of the other towns on the county's list, or the county department.

- For each bidder, a plain question: who files the permit, and will its number be written into the contract?

- Which band your contract total falls in, $3,000 to $24,999 or $25,000 and up, because the Board's pages treat them differently.

- The Tennessee license number each bidder gives you, looked up in the Board's Verify a License search.
