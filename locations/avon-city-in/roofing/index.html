The central Indiana climate and your roofing

NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says annual average temperature varies by about 10 degrees F from north to south, that temperatures in Indiana have risen almost 1.5 degrees F since the beginning of the 20th century, and that the warming has been concentrated in winter and spring. It also says annual precipitation has been above average since 1990. Hendricks County is in central Indiana.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.

What to expect locally

Homeowners in Hendricks County navigating a storm claim juggle three parallel tracks: the insurance process (adjuster inspection, scope, settlement), the contractor process (bids, scheduling, materials), and their own documentation. Keeping those tracks separate is the single most useful habit — your insurer decides coverage under your policy; your contractor proposes the repair scope; and written bids let you compare scope and pricing side by side.

Ask any contractor for an itemized written estimate, proof of insurance, and local references. The process can take longer after a widespread storm, when repair schedules fill up. Patience and paperwork usually serve homeowners better than speed and pressure.

Season and timing

Post-storm weeks can be congested, and quotes gathered in a hurry may be harder to compare; the weeks after the rush can be a calmer time to review bids. Whatever the calendar says, the sequence stays the same: document first, understand your policy second, compare any written bids you receive third — and take your time rather than rushing to agree with the first person who knocks on the door.

Who issues permits in Avon

The town's Building Department asks for applications online and, for now, takes payment only by cashier's check or credit card. Scheduling inspections through the contractor portal takes a "Contractor Access Code," and the page does not say how that code is issued, so ask the office at 317-272-0948.

On the town's Building Permit Application, "Re-Roof" appears among the boxes under Type of Structural Work, and the form asks for a major subdivision name, a zoning classification and the builder or contractor. In its affidavit the applicant affirms that the permit has to be posted on-site in a conspicuous location, "visible from the street," that it becomes void if work has not started twelve months after issue, and that "the review of this permit may take up to 30 days." The 2026 fee schedule, which the department page marks updated August 2026, gives one-layer and complete-removal jobs separate lines in its residential Roofing section and has its own section for re-inspection fees. Fee amounts are not printed here; confirm the current ones with the office.

Questions for your Avon bids

- Ask the Building Department whether your address is inside the town limits or handled by the county, since the posted sheet lists subdivisions in a Hendricks County column.

- Ask each bidder whether the permit application will mark Re-Roof and whether each price is for one layer or for complete removal, since the fee schedule has a line for each.

- Ask who holds the Contractor Access Code for inspections and who will post the permit where it can be seen from the street.

- Ask whether the quote includes drip edge and flashing, and whether decking will be replaced if it is found damaged.

- Give every bidder the same copy of any written estimate from your insurer.
