The Middle Tennessee climate and your roofing

NWS Nashville's heavy precipitation climatology for Middle Tennessee, based on 1961 to 1990 data from 43 cooperative stations, calls November through May the wet season, when two-thirds of annual precipitation falls. It gives March as the wettest month at 5.76 inches and October as the driest at 3.38 inches. These are regional figures for the area that includes Maury County.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Maury County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Maury County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

State licensing

The state board that licenses contractors puts its trigger at $25,000 for a prime contractor and applies the same figure to roofing work subcontracted by another contractor instead of the owner. Roofing appears among covered work for Tennessee's Home Improvement license, but only nine counties adopted it, and Maury sits outside that group.

Before the bids come back

- Pin down which desk handles the parcel: the county office, Columbia, Mount Pleasant or Spring Hill.

- Ask that desk if putting on a new roof triggers a permit, because none of the pages read answers it.

- Settle who signs for the permit and who books the inspector.

- Flag any bid near the $25,000 licensing mark.

- Your insurer's written estimate, if one exists, shown to every bidder.
