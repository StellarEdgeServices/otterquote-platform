The central Indiana climate and your roofing

In central Indiana, where Madison County is located, NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary notes that the state has a long and deadly history of tornadic storms. It identifies June 2, 1990 as Indiana's largest tornado outbreak, with 37 tornadoes, several of them at F4 intensity, and says spring and summer precipitation has generally been above average since the 1990s.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Madison County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Storm damage in central Indiana can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

Its permit guide, a flow chart, lists "Roof/shingle replacement (full)" among work that needs a permit, while work on "replacing damaged or missing shingles" does "not typically require a permit". The Madison County Planning Commission, Madison County Government Center, 16 East 9th Street, Anderson, (765) 641-9540, has jurisdiction over "all unincorporated areas of Madison County" and acts as the building commission for Orestes; its page states no roofing rule, and its permit portal and fee schedule links were not opened. No page for Elwood's building office could be read, since several candidate sites returned access denied or an expired-domain page, so ask the city directly. The offices in Alexandria, Lapel, Frankton and Summitville were not read.

Roofing licenses in Indiana

The Indiana Professional Licensing Agency publishes a profession list that takes in plumbing, real estate and home inspectors; roofing is absent. Indiana's Attorney General, in a leaflet on storm chasers, puts it as "Licensing requirements vary from community to community" and directs readers to their city or county building department. In Madison County the local test is Anderson's registered-contractor line on the permit form, plus whatever Pendleton, Elwood and the county ask.

Setting up your Madison County bids

- Pin down which office handles the address: Anderson, Pendleton, Elwood, Orestes, another town or the county.

- Ask whether the job is a full replacement or a repair of damaged or missing shingles, because Pendleton's guide treats the two differently.

- Ask each bidder for the contractor's registration number if the city's form asks for one, and who pulls the permit.

- Find out whether the shingle brand being bid requires ice and water shield in its installation instructions.

- Any written estimate from your insurer, so every bidder works from the same document.
