The northeast Ohio climate and your roofing

NWS Cleveland's report on the January 2016 lake effect event says the highest Ohio snowfall totals were recorded in Geauga County, which is in the same NOAA climate division as Trumbull County. It describes Lake Erie as mostly ice free east of the islands, which supplied moisture for a prolonged period of snow under west to northwest winds. It also says lake effect bands vary quickly in intensity over short distances.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Trumbull County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Trumbull County through NOAA's lens

Hail first. NOAA logged 22 hail rows for Trumbull County, falling on 11 different days, from March 30, 2017 through April 15, 2026. Rows exist for 2017, then 2018 through 2020, then 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2026; the years 2016, 2021 and 2025 are blank. April 14, 2024 is the day to know. NOAA filed 8 rows that afternoon under Bristolville, Cortland, Fowler, Howland Corners, Hubbard, Leavittsburg and Southington. The top two were a 1.75 in. stone near Leavittsburg, stamped 16:34 EST (5:34 pm EDT), and a 1.50 in. stone at Bristolville, stamped 16:11 EST (5:11 pm EDT). One narrative says "Hail up to golf ball-size about 1-mile ENE of Leavittsburg." The biggest stone fell on the newest hail date: 2.00 in. at Bristolville at 16:49 EST (5:49 pm EDT), on April 15 of 2026, described as "hen egg-size". Nineteen of the 22 rows, on 8 dates, measure at least 1.00 in. By month, April has 13 rows over 4 dates, June 3 over 2, August 3 over 2, March 2 over 2 and July just one.

Thunderstorm wind dominates the file, at 152 rows over 64 dates from June 5, 2016 to June 25, 2026. Instruments back only 7 of them, all labeled for the Youngstown airport, while 145 are estimates. The highest measured row is 64 knots at 13:37 EST (2:37 pm EDT) on May 5, 2024, with a narrative citing a "peak convective wind gust of 74 mph". August tops the months at 32 rows over 10 dates, then June at 31 over 13, July at 26 over 16, April at 21 over 5 and May at 16 over 9. Among full years, 2024 carries the most (22 rows, 6 dates) and 2025 the least (4 rows, 3 dates), and 2026 already lists 17 rows covering 5 dates.

Ten rows share August 6, 2024, all stamped between 15:57 and 16:32 EST (3:57 and 4:32 pm in standard time, 4:57 and 5:32 pm EDT), a span of 35 minutes. Nine are 60-knot estimates, including North Warren, Howland Corners, Champion Heights, Mecca and Kinsman; the remaining one is a 51-knot airport gust. The North Warren narrative reads "Numerous trees downed along North River Road in Warren, between OH Routes 45 and 82."

Two estimates stand highest. At West Farmington, 70 knots is stamped 18:35 EST on November 5, 2017; standard time had returned that morning, so no daylight conversion applies. The narrative estimates winds reaching 80 mph over "the northern third to half of Trumbull County" and says "Two large buildings were leveled near West Farmington with roofing debris found over a third of a mile away." At Coalburg, 78 knots is stamped 18:24 EST (7:24 pm EDT) on April 15, 2026. That narrative puts the damage in Brookfield Township on Everett East Road, counts about 30 to 40 trees felled toward the southeast, and notes one large tree that "clipped a home, and damaged the roof."

Winds unconnected to thunderstorms are filed by forecast zone, here TRUMBULL: 6 High Wind rows on 5 dates (February 24, 2019 to December 29, 2024), five measured at 51 to 53 knots and one estimated at 50, plus 4 Strong Wind rows on 3 dates in 2019 at 41 to 48 knots. The February 2019 narrative states "A roof and facade of a building in Warren was damaged."

NOAA lists 9 tornado rows on 7 dates, 5 coded EF1 and 4 EF0. The longest track is 12.27 miles, up to 250 yards wide: an EF1 from Phalanx to Howland Corners, 15:12 EST (4:12 pm EDT) on June 16, 2019. Thirty-two minutes later a Vienna to Masury row is coded EF0, yet its narrative describes EF-1 damage and 95 mph winds. The January 8, 2019 EF1 (Bazetta to Cortland, 4.5 miles, 10:20 EST, ordinary winter clock time) left, per the narrative, "shingles were blown off a chapel roof". The August 24, 2023 EF0 near Bristolville is stamped 23:35 EST, which is 12:35 am EDT on August 25, though NOAA files it under the 24th; its narrative lists "siding, shingles, and roofing" on several homes. The EF0 of April 17 of 2024, State Road to Champion, 18:01 EST (7:01 pm EDT), includes "roofing damage".

On the clocks: NOAA's format guide treats these fields as usually local standard time, and every Trumbull row carries zone code EST-5. Counting the houses

Census table DP04, five-year 2020-2024 estimates, finds 94,464 housing units here, 86,550 occupied and 7,914 vacant (8.4 percent). Of the 86,550 occupied, 61,705 are owner-occupied (71.3 percent) and 24,845 rented (28.7 percent). Single detached houses come to 72,079 (76.3 percent). Among the other types, 3,421 are attached, 3,406 in two-unit buildings, 4,233 in structures with 20-plus units, 3,854 mobile homes.

Age is the story. The 1970s decade is biggest with 18,011 units (19.1 percent), the 1950s next with 17,195 (18.2 percent), then the 1960s with 12,647 (13.4 percent). The pre-1940 stock is 17,533 units (18.6 percent). Pre-1980 construction accounts for 72,230 units (76.5 percent); only 7,359 (7.8 percent) are newer than 1999. Table B25035 shows a median build year of 1964. Those figures describe buildings, not roofs; a replaced roof can be decades younger than its house.

Where the permit comes from

Several offices speak here, in different words. Trumbull County Building Inspection, 159 East Market Street, Suite 100, Warren, phone (330) 675-2467, is open weekdays 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Its "Permits Required..." page begins "YOU NEED A PERMIT IF YOU ARE:" and, beneath "Replacing or repairing:", includes "Roofing". It ends with "If you are in doubt as to whether or not a permit is required or have any questions, call the building department at (330) 675-2467." Also linked from the county site is a zoning inspectors' roster, revised 1/10/2024, with contacts for Girard, Hubbard, Cortland, Newton Falls, Niles and most townships.

The City of Warren has its own Engineering, Planning and Building Department, 187 High Street NE, (330) 841-2562. Its overview states "Any work in connection with the location, erection, construction, repair, alteration, demolition, moving or equipping of a building will require application and issuance of a Permit." The department page says permits "can only be issued to registered contractors pursuant to the Codified Ordinances of Warren, Ohio 1305.015 or to homeowners who work on the single family dwelling in which they reside." Filings and registrations are made online. Its FAQ on whether you need a building permit points only to a phone number, (330) 841-2617.

Niles keeps a separate building department, (330) 544-9000, whose FAQ answers "Do I need a permit" with "Any remodeling, roofing, siding, etc. requires permits through the City of Niles." We did not read roofing rules for Girard, Hubbard, Cortland, Newton Falls, Howland or the other townships and villages; ask the building or zoning office that covers your address.
