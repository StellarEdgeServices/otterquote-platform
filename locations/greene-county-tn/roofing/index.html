The East Tennessee climate and your roofing

NOAA's Tennessee state climate summary says temperatures decrease as elevation increases, averaging a 3 degree F decline per 1,000 feet. It describes the Cumberland Plateau as averaging 2,000 feet in elevation and Smoky Mountains peaks as reaching 6,000 feet, and it says these higher areas are cooler than lower-elevation areas. These are statewide statements that include Greene County.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Greene County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Greene County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Greene County, Tennessee: roof weather around Greeneville, Tusculum and Mosheim

This page covers the East Tennessee county whose seat is Greeneville. Several other states have a Greene County too, Ohio included; those counties are excluded here, since every storm figure below came from rows with the state field set to TENNESSEE.

Hail leads: the thinner of the two records. NOAA posts one detail CSV per year for its Storm Events records. Reading the files for 2016-2026 and keeping lines whose county field says GREENE gives 20 hail lines on 16 days, from July 19, 2016 to September 4, 2025. Yearly line counts from 2016 onward are 1, 3, 2, 2, 3, 1, 4, 1, 1 and 2, with 2026 blank so far. Diameter ladder, in inches: 1.00 (9 lines), 1.25 (7), 1.50 (3), 1.75 (1). Nothing is under an inch. By month, lines / days: May 8 / 6, April 4 / 2, June 4 / 4, March 2 / 2, July 1 / 1, September 1 / 1. The spring stretch, March-June, therefore holds 18 lines on 14 days, most of the total.

April 29, 2017 produced a trio: Greeneville, 1.50-inch hail, 14:58 EST (15:58 EDT); Afton, 1.25, 15:10 EST (16:10 EDT); Rheatown, 1.75 (biggest hail line recorded here), 15:15 EST (16:15 EDT). Afton's narrative mentions Tusculum College, so the Afton label is a nearby name, not proof of where the stones fell. May 6, 2022 added Mosheim (1.25; 15:20 EST, 16:20 EDT), Hardin Chapel (1.25; 15:48 EST, 16:48 EDT) and Greeneville (1.00; 16:42 EST, 17:42 EDT). NOAA left the event narratives of those three blank.

Clock handling: the format guide shipped with NOAA's files describes its time entries as 24-hour readings, usually LST, and each Greene line is tagged EST, which lags daylight time. Daylight time prevailed for 65 of the 72 lines; for those the wall clock ran an hour ahead. Two lines roll into the next date after adding: Baileyton, 23:37 EST June 21, 2019, equals 00:37 EDT June 22; Greeneville Arpt, 23:05 EST August 14, 2023, equals 00:05 EDT August 15. Under the plain county name sit no tornado, High Wind or Strong Wind rows. The Census Bureau's geocoder, fed all 72 coordinate pairs, placed every one inside Greene County. Who warns you

The Weather Service county record for zone TNC059 (Greene) points to its Morristown office for forecasts. The Weather Service's national Instruction 10-1605 defines severe as hail of 1 inch and gusts of 50 knots (58 mph); all 20 hail lines clear that bar, and 15 wind lines sit exactly on it. For emergency planning, town and county run a joint desk, Emergency Management and Homeland Security, at 311 CCU Blvd. Suite 2, (423) 798-1729; per the page, Morristown warnings "will be sent out via our SMART911 system" to people who signed up.

Permit counters

Greeneville's town inspectors work from Town Hall's second floor, 200 N. College St., 423-639-7105, according to a Greeneville Sun guidebook entry stamped August 29, 2026. According to that entry, the town demands permits for remodeling jobs and for new construction on residences and businesses. It also lists a county address, 129 Charles St. Suite 2, which the county's current pages replace with another, so it may repeat older details; roofing goes unnamed there. The town's home page shows the same street address and phone, plus a Building Permits link that this review could not open.

Beyond the town line, Building and Zoning, the county's own desk, takes over: 401 Takoma Ave, Suite 102; 423-798-1724; Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 16:30. Code editions: 2018 Residential Code (Appendix J included) and 2018 Building Code, both International. Its Building Commissioner issues the permits; the county says approval and issuance happen on site once an initial inspection is done. The application wants a description of the planned structure, with a site plan or sketch. No county page says roofing, which leaves open whether replacing roofing needs a permit; phone and ask. The Commissioner's page says whoever pulls a permit as a contractor keeps three items filed per permit: state license, workers' compensation papers, and a business license from Greene County. Tusculum, Mosheim and Baileyton are separate municipalities whose permit pages were not read.

At state level, Tennessee's contractor board states that licensing is mandatory before bidding or negotiating a price on work worth $25,000 or above (prime contractors, certain subcontractors, construction managers). The remodeling license is another matter: roofing is among the listed work, it covers jobs between $3,000 and $24,999, and just nine counties have taken the law up, a roster that omits Greene.

Greene County checklist for the bid stage

- Figure out where the house stands, inside Greeneville's limits or outside them; each side keeps a separate counter and number.

- Ask that counter, not a neighbor, whether replacing the roof needs a permit and who files.

- Jot down the date and hour of any storm that hit the roof, remembering that NOAA's clock reads earlier than a summer wall clock.

- See if the quoted job price reaches $25,000, where state licensing begins.

- Your insurer's written estimate, if one exists, shown to every bidder.
