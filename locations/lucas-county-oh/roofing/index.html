The northwest Ohio climate and your roofing

NOAA's Ohio state climate summary says annual precipitation varies regionally and places the northwestern part of the state, where Lucas County sits, on the drier side compared with the southern part. It notes that winter can bring snowstorms and ice storms, and it calls thunderstorms, flood-producing rainstorms, hail and tornadoes common in the warmer months. The summary also reports a significant increase in heavy rain events across Ohio.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Lucas County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Storm record for Lucas County

Lucas County's hail record in NOAA's Storm Events Database holds 31 items on 14 different days, running from March 27, 2016 to March 26, 2026. Dates follow NOAA's records; its format guide says times are "usually in LST" (local standard time), and every Lucas County row counted here is stamped EST. Twenty-one items, on 8 days, give a diameter of 1.00 inch or more. The record high is 2.50 inches, filed under Pt Place for June 15, 2023, where the narrative reads "tennis ball-size near Cullen Park in Point Place neighborhood of Toledo." Two further Pt Place items that day read 2.00 inches and a fourth reads 1.75, as does an item labeled Niles Beach, which NOAA's narrative sets in Maumee Bay State Park. Five items make June 15, 2023 the third-heaviest hail date on record here.

Labels and narratives can disagree, and "PT PLACE" is simply NOAA's shorthand for Point Place, a Toledo neighborhood. The two 1.50-inch items of June 1, 2019 show the gap: the one labeled Vulcan describes ping pong size hail on the northeast side of Maumee, and the one labeled Toledo puts it on that city's south side. June 1, 2019 holds 6 items under Crestview, Holland, Ottawa Hills, Toledo and Vulcan, and March 26, 2026 holds 6 under Centennial, Ironville, Neapolis, Sylvania and Toledo, topping out at 1.25 inches in Neapolis. Counting by month, June has 13 items on 4 days, March 8 on 2 days and May 4 on 4 days. Nothing appears for 2021, 2022 or 2025.

Thunderstorm wind fills a longer ledger: 91 items on 42 days from June 5, 2016 through June 11, 2026 on NOAA's standard-time clock (the last two items begin at 11:41 p.m. EST, which is 12:41 a.m. daylight time on June 12), with an instrument reading behind 8 and an estimate behind 83. June carries 23 items over 11 days, July 20 over 7 and August 15 over 7. July 26, 2023 stands out at 8 items, and August 29, 2022 follows at 6. The strongest instrument reading is 63 knots under Vulcan for August 11, 2021, where the narrative says a trained spotter "measured a peak gust of 72 mph" about 2 miles west-northwest of Rossford. The strongest estimate is 70 knots under Crestview for July 2, 2019.

Zone-filed winds with no thunderstorm behind them number only 3 here, all High Wind items, dated January 10, 2017, February 24, 2019 and December 11, 2021; the file shows no Strong Wind item for Lucas County.

Three tornado items cover 2 days. The EF1 of July 31, 2018, running from Booth to Pt Place on NOAA's labels, measures 6.78 miles; its narrative has it touching down near Oregon and tracking due north to near Point Place, about 25 yards wide at most. June 15, 2023 holds the other two. The EF2, running from National Arpt to Pt Place on those labels, started at 5:06 p.m. EST, which is 6:06 p.m. daylight time. It began 1000 feet east of Telegraph Road along East State Line Road in Toledo, reached 130 mph winds in NOAA's narrative, widened to as much as 300 yards across the Point Place area and ended at the Maumee Mooring Basin of the Maumee River, 3.81 miles on. Permits and local rules

Two building offices cover most Lucas County addresses. The City of Toledo's Division of Building Inspection, at One Government Center, Suite 1600, 419-245-1220, lists a permit type for "Roofing with a complete tear off" on 1, 2 or 3 unit residential buildings, in its page on exterior non-structural permits, under the portal choice "Residential Exterior Non-Structural Alt". Its page says "To apply, you must have a Limited Home Remodeler Contractor's License, a Home Remodeler Contractor License, or be the homeowner", and "A homeowner hiring a contractor to complete the work does not need to apply for a permit. The contractor will apply for all permits." Applications go through the city's permit portal, and the city's permits page adds that properties in historic districts may require a Certificate of Appropriateness before work can begin.

Toledo calls its credential a license. Its remodeling page says "In order to legally perform home repairs or remodeling in Toledo, a contractor must be licensed with the city", and its registered contractors page describes the Home Remodeling Contractors list as professionals who can help with roofing, windows and siding, last updated 04/21/2026. The license renewal page adds that beginning in 2026 home remodeling contractors must complete 10 hours of continuing education each year to renew. The registered contractors page says "The City of Toledo requires that contractors register and maintain a license to do business in the City."

Outside the city, the Lucas County Building Regulations Department, at 1115 McCord Road S in Holland, 419-213-2990, says it holds jurisdiction over residential construction in the county except within the corporation limits of Maumee, Oregon, Toledo, Berkey and Whitehouse. Its FAQ says "You need a building permit for any new construction, addition, alteration, repair, replacement, or demolition except any unattached residential structure less than 200 square feet." The page does not name roofing, so put the roof question to the department directly.

Frequently asked questions

Should I repair or replace after partial-slope damage?

Shingle availability and the age of the roof both come into it; your insurer can tell you how your policy addresses matching. Written bids that price both paths let you compare the two side by side.

Do I need to be home when my contractor or insurer inspects the roof?

For the exterior portion, usually not — but being present means you see the documented damage yourself and can ask questions of whoever inspects the roof.
