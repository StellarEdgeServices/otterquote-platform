The central Indiana climate and your roofing

Delaware County sits in central Indiana. NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says dangerous storms can occur in every season, that winter can bring snow and ice storms, and that thunderstorms capable of producing floods, hail and tornadoes are common in the warmer months. It records that heavy snowstorms during the first week of January 2014 dropped 1 to more than 2 feet of snow over central and northern Indiana.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.

What to expect locally

Homeowners in Delaware County navigating a storm claim juggle three parallel tracks: the insurance process (adjuster inspection, scope, settlement), the contractor process (bids, scheduling, materials), and their own documentation. Keeping those tracks separate is the single most useful habit — your insurer decides coverage under your policy; your contractor proposes the repair scope; and written bids let you compare scope and pricing side by side.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Delaware County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

How old Delaware County's homes are

The five-year American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024 shows Delaware County with 112,280 residents and 51,635 housing units. Of the units, 46,256 are occupied and 5,379 vacant (10.4 percent). Owner-occupied units come to 30,751 (66.5 percent) and rented ones to 15,505 (33.5 percent). The county has 37,648 detached single-family houses (72.9 percent) and 2,443 mobile homes (4.7 percent). The median year built is 1967, give or take 1 year, so about half of all units date from 1967 or earlier. 35,987 units (69.7 percent) predate 1980, 9,403 (18.2 percent) were put up between 1980 and 1999, and 6,245 (12.1 percent) came in 2000 or later. The 1960s lead all decades with 8,310 units (16.1 percent), and 9,024 (17.5 percent) go back to 1939 or earlier. All of this is estimated from a sample, each number with a range of uncertainty, and no table gives a roof replacement date. A general observation: older housing stock means more roofs that are past a first replacement cycle, so the age of the roof in front of you is a question for your own records and your bidders.

Which Delaware County offices handle permits

Three offices turn up. The Delaware County Building Commissioner's Office, at 100 W Main St, Muncie, second floor, (765) 747-7799, open weekdays 8:30 am to 4:00 pm, says it "issues permits for projects located in the unincorporated areas of Delaware County." Among the work it lists as needing a permit are "Remodel of existing structures" and "Other construction," followed by the note "Call the office to see if your project requires a permit"; roofing is not named. The office also says general, electrical, plumbing and HVAC contractors must register with it each year and keep current insurance when they work in the unincorporated county.

The City of Muncie's Building Commissioner's Office, 300 N High St according to the city's own license forms, publishes an "Application for Construction Improvement Plan" whose type-of-project line offers "Roof" beside new construction, addition and remodel/repair. The form is undated and names the 2006 International Residential Code as its inspection basis, and the list of jobs that need drawings does not mention roofs, so ask the office what it currently requires. The Town of Yorktown's Building and Zoning Department, 9312 W Smith St, (765) 759-8521, says "Most construction or remodeling projects" need a permit, tells residents to "contact the Building Commissioner to determine what permits you need," and says contractors "must hold a valid license for their trade and be registered in Delaware County." The pages for Albany, Daleville, Eaton, Gaston, Selma and the other towns were not read, and nothing located for this page lays out a Delaware County reroofing rule.

Licensing for roofers: what the state publishes

The state's Professional Licensing Agency publishes a profession list that carries plumbing, real estate and home inspectors, among others; no roofing profession appears on it. A leaflet from the Attorney General about storm chasers says "Many localities require permits for building projects. Contact your local building department to see if a permit is needed," and that licensing requirements differ between communities. Delaware County bid checklist

- Identify the office with authority over your address: the City of Muncie, Yorktown, another town, or the county.

- Ask every bidder whether a tear-off needs a permit at your address, and who applies for it.

- Ask whether the bidder is registered with the office that covers your address, since the county and Yorktown both mention registration.

- Confirm with each bidder whether the shingle manufacturer's installation instructions require ice and water shield, given that the state table lists "No" for Delaware.

- Ask each bidder how they price any decking that needs replacing, and whether that is in the written bid.

- Hand every bidder the same copy of any written estimate you hold from your insurer.
