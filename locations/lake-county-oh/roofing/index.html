A severe storm in Lake County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

The northeast Ohio climate and your roofing

In its summary of the 2022-23 winter, NWS Cleveland reports that snowbelt counties east of Cleveland received 30 to 60 inches that winter, against normal amounts of 70 to over 100 inches in those areas. The same summary says the mild winter inhibited ice formation on Lake Erie, and that a mid-March lake effect event added 6 to 10 inches across eastern counties. Lake County is one of the Northeast division counties in NOAA's list.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Lake County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Storm damage in northeast Ohio can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

Storm record for Lake County

Hail is a short list in Lake County's file. NOAA's Storm Events Database holds 12 hail rows, scattered over 9 separate dates from July 26, 2018 to February 27, 2024, and only 4 of them, on 3 dates, reach 1.00 inch: a 1.00-inch row labeled Pleasant Vly on January 8, 2019, a pair on May 21, 2022 (1.00 inch labeled East Lake and 1.25 inches labeled Lake Line), and a 1.25-inch row labeled Redbird on March 31, 2023. The other eight measure 0.75 or 0.88 inch. Nothing appears for 2016, 2017, 2021, 2025 or 2026, the rows touch nine different months, and May's three rows make the biggest monthly tally.

A row's label and its note can name different towns. The East Lake row carries the note "Quarter-size hail in Willowick.", the Lake Line row carries "Hail up to half dollar-size in Timberlake.", and the Pleasant Vly row of January 8, 2019 is a social media report of quarter-sized hail in Willoughby Hills. NOAA's format guide calls the bulk-file clock "usually in LST", and all 108 Lake County rows we counted for this page, hail, wind and tornado rows plus the zone-filed High Wind rows, are stamped EST.

Thunderstorm wind is the bigger category, with 78 rows on 33 dates from August 9, 2016 to June 14, 2026; instruments account for 3 and estimates for 75. August tops the months at 27 rows on 8 dates, then June (17 rows, 7 dates), July (16, 6) and May, fourth at 9 rows on 5 dates. Taking years one at a time, 2024 contributes 16 rows that fall on only 2 dates, 2023 adds 11 rows on 4 dates, 2026 brings 10 on 4 dates, and 2017 has a single row. August 6, 2024 stands alone with 13 rows, while July 26, 2018 and June 14, 2026 follow with 5 each. The highest figures in the file, 75 knots, estimated, belong to two rows from that August day. One is labeled ERIESIDE, and its narrative locates the event just north of Lakeshore Boulevard and Reynolds Road in the Mentor area; the other carries a label for an airport in Cuyahoga County, but its coordinates and narrative place it in Willoughby Hills, where the narrative says "Willoughby Hills Fire Department had a portion of its station's roof removed." Seven more rows that day are estimated at 65 knots, including three labeled Mentor on the Lake, while four are estimated at 60. Two of the 65-knot narratives read "Large trees downed along Brooks Boulevard in Mentor-on-the-Lake." and, for the Lake Line row, "Large tree was downed at Lake County Yacht Club in Eastlake."

The zone-filed wind record, which NWS Directive 10-1605 describes as "non-convective winds", is a separate list. Lake County's zone holds 15 High Wind rows on 12 dates between January 10, 2017 and January 13, 2024, with 9 measured and 6 estimated. The strongest is the 58-knot measurement of November 27, 2019, and its narrative says "Trees and power lines were noted down in Mentor." One of the three January 10, 2017 rows reads "An automated sensor at Fairport Harbor measured a 62 mph wind gust." January 2024 supplies four dates by itself, January 9, 10, 12 and 13, and the January 10 narrative reads "At 12:30 PM, a 62 MPH wind gust was measured in Fairport." NOAA files no Strong Wind row for the county.

Only three tornado rows exist, one on each of three dates, and all are rated EF1. On October 21, 2021 a 0.18-mile path 50 yards wide begins on Briar Court in Wickliffe and ends at Pine Ridge Country Club; NOAA leaves its location labels blank; the narrative begins "A brief EF1 tornado with estimated maximum winds of 97 mph moved generally east-northeastward in Wickliffe." The Kirtland to Mentor row of August 24, 2023, timed 2306 EST, which is 12:06 a.m. daylight time on August 25, runs 3.27 miles and 150 yards wide, with estimated peak winds of 110 mph, and its narrative starts the path just west of Dartmoor Road near Mentor Avenue. The last is the Pleasant Vly to Peck Corners row of August 6, 2024, 1.52 miles and 200 yards wide with the same 110 mph estimate, which begins on Worrell Road about two miles south of Waite Hill and leaves for Geauga County near Chesterland. NOAA built its 2026 file on September 18, 2026 and its README says "We will update the current yearly file each month." so 2026 figures can still move.

The structure mix is 76,515 detached houses (72.0 percent), 6,886 attached, 1,993 two-unit buildings, 7,642 homes in buildings of 20 or more units (7.2 percent) and 1,968 mobile homes. The 1950s lead the age table with 18,694 units (17.6 percent), ahead of the 1970s with 17,890 (16.8 percent) and the 1960s with 14,809 (13.9 percent). Altogether, 66,763 units (62.8 percent) predate 1980 and 15,730 (14.8 percent) arrived in 2000 or later. Permits and local rules

The county and its cities split the work. The Lake County Building Department, 105 Main St, Bldg B, 2nd Floor in Painesville, 440-350-2636, sits "ON THE SQUARE IN THE OLD NOLEN BUILDING" according to its home page, which lists office hours of 8:00AM to 4:30PM Monday through Friday, counter hours of 8:30AM to 4:00PM Monday through Thursday and 8:30AM to 1:00PM on Friday, and inspection times of 9:30 A.M. to 3:30 P.M. The department publishes a Jurisdictions sheet marked "Updated 1-23". It names Painesville City, Kirtland City, Madison Village, Willowick, Willoughby Hills and a run of townships, among them Concord, Leroy, Madison, Painesville and Perry, and it carries the line "Wickliffe – All Commercial and Residential Electric Only", so ask which office handles residential building work at a Wickliffe address. Mentor-on-the-Lake is on that sheet, and the City of Mentor is not. The county's pages that we read do not address roof replacement, so put the roof question to the department by phone.

On that front page a notice reads "IT IS OUR GOAL TO COMPLETE OUR PLAN REVIEW PROCESS IN THREE WEEKS" and adds that the office cannot predict its workload, that review may take longer, and that the code allows 30 working days.

The county also runs contractor registration. Its sheet says all contractors doing work in "Lake County Building Departments jurisdictions" must register if the work is residential or commercial. It lists categories for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, hydronics and other trades and a separate "General" category, for which it says "No state license required." It does not list roofing on its own. The department's home page also links to the state's eLicense look-up at elicense4.com.ohio.gov, and the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board's own page describes the board as part of the "Division of Industrial Compliance".

Mentor has its own Engineering and Building Department at 8500 Civic Center Boulevard, 440-974-5785. The city's permits page says "A Building Permit Application is required for all construction including decks, pools, siding, sheds, porches, enclosures, etc." and does not name roofing. It adds "Users can now apply for and pay for permits online through the Mentor Gateway." Its page on contractors says that anyone contracting with the general public in the city must register with the City before starting work, citing Mentor Code of Ordinances 925, 1307 and 1309, asks for proof of liability insurance and a registration bond on the city's Surety Bond Form, and warns that "a bond is written for a specific purpose and their bond may not protect you as the owner." It also cautions that an owner advised by an unregistered contractor to apply under the owner's own name takes on responsibilities and risks. An older city guide still hosted on the city's website, marked "Updated 8/10", lists new roofing and reroofing among work needing a permit and says "No more than two layers of shingles may be applied to roof sheathing." Because that guide is older, confirm its roofing rules with the department first.

What to line up before you ask for bids

- Your insurer's written estimate, if there is one, in a format any bidder can read.

- The jurisdiction for your address on the county's Jurisdictions sheet: the county department, Mentor-on-the-Lake or another listed place, or, for Mentor, the city's own Engineering and Building Department.

- Ask each bidder who files the permit application and which office holds the contractor registration, if one applies.

- The county's posted inspection times, 9:30 A.M. to 3:30 P.M., in case a bidder will need to be on site for an inspection.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be home when my contractor or insurer inspects the roof?

For the exterior portion, usually not — but being present means you see the documented damage yourself and can ask questions of whoever inspects the roof.

Who decides whether my policy applies to roof damage in Lake County?

Your insurer decides coverage under the terms of your policy. Read your policy, ask your adjuster what is included, and document the damage with dated photos. Our guide on filing a property damage claim walks through the process step by step.
