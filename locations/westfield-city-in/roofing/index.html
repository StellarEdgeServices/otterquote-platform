A severe storm in Hamilton County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

The central Indiana climate and your roofing

In central Indiana, where Hamilton County is located, NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary notes that the state has a long and deadly history of tornadic storms. It identifies June 2, 1990 as Indiana's largest tornado outbreak, with 37 tornadoes, several of them at F4 intensity, and says spring and summer precipitation has generally been above average since the 1990s.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Hamilton County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Post-storm weeks can be congested, and quotes gathered in a hurry may be harder to compare; the weeks after the rush can be a calmer time to review bids. Whatever the calendar says, the sequence stays the same: document first, understand your policy second, compare any written bids you receive third — and take your time rather than rushing to agree with the first person who knocks on the door.

Nothing is filed for 2018 or 2019, or for 2026.

Reported stone sizes in inches, with entry counts, are 0.75 (3), 0.88 (1), 1.00 (6) and 1.75 (1), so 7 entries on 6 dates reach one inch. March holds 7 of the 11 entries on 4 dates, April 2 on 2, and June and July one each. Who issues permits in Westfield

The city's offices, at 2728 East 171st Street, Westfield, IN 46074, are open Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and closed from noon to 1 p.m.; the Building Division lists 317-804-3150, extension 5, and building@westfield.in.gov. Westfield has a page devoted to roofing questions. Its Roofing FAQs page says no permit is required to replace shingles or to replace a roof, that the work must still meet the current Indiana Residential Code, and that "Roofing materials must be installed in accordance with the manufacturer's installation instructions." It adds that the city sets no ice and water shield requirement beyond the state code, that shield must go on if the maker's instructions require it, and that ignoring those instructions is treated as a code violation. The Building Division page says all permit applications are submitted through the City's Citizens Portal.

The residential single-family permit page says nothing about contractor registration, so this draft makes no statement about whether Westfield registers or licenses roofers. Ask the Building Division.

What Westfield's Building Division page says about the permit process

The Building Division page describes a portal workflow rather than a counter visit. Applicants use the Citizens Portal to track an application's status, to request inspections and to read the results, and the page puts the burden of asking for an inspection on the applicant, who must also make sure the site can be reached during the scheduled inspection window. It states that fees must be completed, along with all required inspections, approvals and documentation, before a Certificate of Occupancy is released, and it points to a separate Fee Schedule page. The Building Division page lists no contractor requirements, gives no fee amounts and does not say how far ahead an inspection must be requested, so those are three more questions for building@westfield.in.gov or 317-804-3150.

Westfield's historic preservation rules

Westfield's city code has a subchapter that establishes a Historic Preservation Commission, and its definitions section, 33.131, sets the vocabulary. A historic district can be a single property or a larger grouping, with its boundaries shown on a map that the City Council adopts by ordinance. Primary areas hold the main historic or architectural significance, and secondary areas are adjacent zones that must stay compatible with them. Properties are rated Outstanding, Notable, Contributing or Non-contributing, and a Contributing property is one at least 40 years old.

Two definitions matter to a homeowner. An alteration is a material or color change in the external architectural features of a property in a historic district, and routine maintenance is work for which no certificate of appropriateness is required. The definitions section does not mention roofs and does not say what counts as routine, so this page does not claim that a reroof is or is not routine. The Building Division's roofing page, quoted above, says no permit is needed to replace shingles, which answers the building permit question only. For a house in or beside a mapped district, ask the Building Division at building@westfield.in.gov whether any preservation step applies as well.

Roofing licenses in Indiana

Roofing has no entry on the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency's profession list, which does name plumbing, real estate and home inspectors. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet says "Licensing requirements vary from community to community." In Westfield, the city's own page says a permit is not required for a reroof, and the remaining question is the contractor's local status.

Questions for your Westfield bids

- Ask each bidder whether the quote follows the shingle maker's written installation instructions, since the city treats that as a code matter.

- Ask whether the maker's instructions call for ice and water shield on your roof, since the state table says "No" for Hamilton County.

- Ask the Building Division whether the contractor needs to be registered or licensed for your job.

- Ask whether the quote covers replacing damaged decking and how that is priced, since the city's roofing page speaks only to shingles and the roof as a whole.

- Show each bidder the same copy of any insurer estimate in writing.
