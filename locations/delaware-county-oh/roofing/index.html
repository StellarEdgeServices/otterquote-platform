Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.

What to expect locally

Homeowners in Delaware County navigating a storm claim juggle three parallel tracks: the insurance process (adjuster inspection, scope, settlement), the contractor process (bids, scheduling, materials), and their own documentation. Keeping those tracks separate is the single most useful habit — your insurer decides coverage under your policy; your contractor proposes the repair scope; and written bids let you compare scope and pricing side by side.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Delaware County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Permits and local rules

Who issues a permit depends on the address. Delaware County Building Safety, at 1610 State Route 521 in Delaware, 740-833-2200, posts a sheet headed "Permitting in Delaware County", dated 6/5/2025. It puts residential permitting for all townships, Ashley, Galena, Ostrander, Shawnee Hills and Sunbury under Delaware County Building Safety, residential permitting in Delaware City under the Delaware City Building Department (740-203-1600), and Powell under the Powell Building Department (614-885-5380). Dublin, Westerville and Columbus appear with their own offices.

The county department states its mission, in part, as "to protect the life, safety and welfare". Its contact page lists hours of 8am to 4:30pm, Monday through Friday, and its home page carries the notice "Our office will open at 8:30am on the 2nd Wednesday of every month." Its page on applying says all submittals "MUST be in PDF format", that photographs of documents are rejected, and that the status of a permit can be checked 72 hours after all documents are submitted, and it invites a phone call to 740-833-2200 if no status shows after 1 week. The site also keeps a link headed "Today’s Inspection Schedule".

For work under the county, its "Residential Project Requirements" sheet is the closest published answer. It has two columns, "Permit Required" and "Plans Required", and its row "Replace Roof" reads NO in both. In the same table, rows such as "Replace Exterior Siding" also read NO, while "Replace a Furnace or AC Unit" reads YES for a permit. The county's separate "Exempt from Approval" sheet, dated 9/24/24, quotes section 102.10 of the 2019 Residential Code of Ohio and does not list roofing either way. The county pages read for this page do not mention contractor registration.

The City of Delaware writes its own rules. Its residential building and zoning application, revised December 2022, offers "Re-Roof" as a permit type and states that contractors need to register with the City of Delaware. The City Hall Annex, 1 S Sandusky Street, houses that office, and the phone number on the county's sheet is 740-203-1600. On state licensing, the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board's website ties its examination to contractors "who successfully pass the PROV licensing examination" and names electrical, HVAC, plumbing, hydronics and refrigeration as the trades, with roofing not among them. For a Powell address, check with the Powell Building Department.

What to line up before you ask for bids

- Your insurer's written estimate, if you hold one, as a file each bidder can open.

- Which office issues permits for your address: county Building Safety, the Delaware City Building Department, the Powell Building Department, or else the Dublin, Westerville or Columbus office the county's sheet lists.

- A question for each bidder on whether the job is a plain roof replacement, since the county's table treats that differently from a furnace swap or an addition.

- Any plans or documents saved as PDF files, since the county application page rejects photographs of documents.
