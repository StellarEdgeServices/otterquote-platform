The central Indiana climate and your roofing

Monroe County sits in central Indiana. NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says dangerous storms can occur in every season, that winter can bring snow and ice storms, and that thunderstorms capable of producing floods, hail and tornadoes are common in the warmer months. It records that heavy snowstorms during the first week of January 2014 dropped 1 to more than 2 feet of snow over central and northern Indiana.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Monroe County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Monroe County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Zone, wind speed and ice shield: what the state sheet says

Permit offices in Monroe County

The Monroe County Building Department sits at 501 N Morton St, Suite 220 in Bloomington, phone (812) 349-2580. It calls itself "responsible for the inspection and enforcement of adopted Indiana Building Codes throughout Monroe County." Its page gives weekday hours of 8:00 am to 4:00 pm, applications online through an account on the county permit portal, and a licensing and registration program "for electricians and plumbers." Roofing does not come up. A City of Bloomington notice from December 2025 says that starting January 1, 2026, the city's Planning and Transportation Department "will have a new permitting process for Class 2 structures," which the notice defines to include detached and attached single-family homes, duplexes and most townhouses, and that applicants "will begin the permitting process with the City of Bloomington instead of the Monroe County Building Department." The same notice says a Class 2 structure application to the Monroe County Building Department can only be submitted after the city issues a Certificate of Zoning Compliance. Roofs are not mentioned in the notice, so whether a re-roof of a house inside the city follows this process is a question for the city's Planning and Transportation Department, 401 N Morton St, Suite 130, (812) 349-3423. The notice does not say the county department stops handling these applications. The pages for Ellettsville, Stinesville and the other towns were not read, and nothing found for this page sets out a Monroe County reroofing rule.

What Indiana says about licensing roofers

Among the fields on the Professional Licensing Agency's list are home inspectors, plumbing and real estate; roofing is not one. The Attorney General's leaflet on storm chasers tells homeowners "Many localities require permits for building projects. Contact your local building department to see if a permit is needed," and adds that licensing rules differ from one community to the next. For a Monroe County address, then, the answer depends on whether the city, the county department or a smaller town handles the job.

Before you sign a Monroe County bid

- Work out which office has authority over your address: the City of Bloomington, the county, or a town such as Ellettsville.

- Put the permit question to every bidder: does a tear-off need one at your address, and who files for it?

- Ask how much notice the inspector needs and whether any inspection is required.

- Check whether the manufacturer's installation instructions for the shingles require ice and water shield, because the state table lists "No" for Monroe.

- If you rent the home out, ask each bidder how they handle access and scheduling with your tenant.

- Hand every bidder the same copy of any written estimate you hold from your insurer.
