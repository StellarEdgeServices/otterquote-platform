Storm damage to windows is easy to overlook. Broken glass is obvious, but hail damage to frames, cladding, and glazing beads is easy to miss and worth including in your documentation.

The Northeast Prairie climate and your windows

NOAA's state summary says Missouri's "location in the interior of North America and the lack of mountain barriers to the north and south expose the state to incursions of cold arctic air masses in the winter and warm, moist air masses from the Gulf of Mexico in the summer." The Climate Center says the northern half of the state averages about 110 winter days with temperatures below freezing. It says snow has been known to fall as early as October and as late as May, and that "most of it falls in December, January, and February." These are statewide or regional figures, not measurements taken in St. Charles County.

Window issues to look for after a storm

  • Hail-damaged frames and cladding — dents and fractures that compromise weather sealing even when glass survives.
  • Failed insulated-glass seals — post-storm fogging between panes; document it and ask your adjuster whether it is included.
  • Energy-efficiency step-ups — homeowners choosing between like-for-like replacement and upgraded units can ask for written bids that price the difference.
  • Water intrusion at damaged openings — interior finish damage near a damaged opening; document it and mention it to your adjuster.
  • Matching and availability questions — discontinued window lines raise the same repair-versus-replace questions that come up with siding.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for St. Charles County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

Window projects in St. Charles County often involve many openings of the same few sizes, which makes it easy to compare bids if you keep a clean list. Make a sheet with one row per window: room, width and height as best you can measure, style, whether it is original, and a short note on what is wrong with it. Include the patio door and any basement windows. Hand the sheet to whoever prepares each bid.

Ask for the replacement method in each case: a new frame set into the opening, or a new unit inserted into the old frame. Ask whether the existing trim stays, and if so what is done to protect it. Ask what the frames are made from, whether the glass has a coating and gas fill, and which maker and series are quoted. Ask for the product label details to be added to the paperwork.

Ask about grids, tilt-in sashes, locks, screens and color on each side of the window. Ask who is responsible for removal of old units and for the interior finish: paint, stain, caulk and casing. If your subdivision has rules about exterior appearance, ask whoever wrote the bid to show you the proposed color and grid pattern in writing before you agree.

Collect the written warranty and read what it says about glass seals, frames and hardware. Photograph the windows beforehand.

If any of your windows face the street or sit above a front porch, ask for a note about how ladders and lifts will be placed so the roof, flower beds and walkway are not scuffed. For tall stair windows, ask whether scaffolding is part of the plan and whether it is listed. Details like these rarely appear on a one-line quote, and a proposal that spells them out is easier to compare with the others in your folder.

Frequently asked questions

Should I replace like-for-like or upgrade?

Ask your adjuster how your policy treats replacement and upgrades. Bids that price both options make the decision concrete instead of hypothetical.

What should I do about a fogged window after a storm?

Photograph it promptly, with dates, and note when you first saw it. Whether it is included is your insurer's decision under your policy, so ask your adjuster.
