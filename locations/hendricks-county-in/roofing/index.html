The central Indiana climate and your roofing

In central Indiana, where Hendricks County is located, NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary notes that the state has a long and deadly history of tornadic storms. It identifies June 2, 1990 as Indiana's largest tornado outbreak, with 37 tornadoes, several of them at F4 intensity, and says spring and summer precipitation has generally been above average since the 1990s.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Hendricks County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Permits for roofing in Hendricks County

The county's Planning and Building Department works from 355 S. Washington Street, Suite G80, Danville, and answers at 317-745-9255. Its Contractor Listing packet, stamped "Updated: April 28, 2015" on its face, states that contractors who wish to apply for building permits from the department must first be on the county's list. The packet asks for a surety bond, liability insurance and workers' compensation insurance, and says the department keeps a registry; people who are not contractors complete an exemption affidavit. Two cautions apply. The packet is old, and it lists the office at a different suite number than the current permit application does. And the county's pages are silent on whether a re-roof needs a permit at all: its single-sheet permit requirements list names new homes, additions, accessory buildings, remodels, demolition, pools and roof-mounted solar, and leaves re-roofing out. The county's permit information page still links the packet and the affidavit, and its February 2022 portal notice mentions a Contractor Listing fee, so confirm the program's current terms with the department.

Checklist for Hendricks County bids

- Learn whether your address falls under the county department or a town office such as Avon, Brownsburg, Danville or Plainfield.

- Put the permit question to that office and to each bidder: does the re-roof need one, and who pulls it?

- If the county department issues your permit, ask whether each bidder is on its Contractor Listing registry.

- Hand every bidder the same readable copy of any written estimate your insurer has issued.

- Gather any paperwork that shows when the roof was last replaced, plus the date of the latest storm you noticed at your address.
