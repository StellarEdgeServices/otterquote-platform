A separate NWS Nashville climatology covers Middle Tennessee tornadoes from 1830 to 2003, and a third NWS Nashville study covers heavy precipitation.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Wilson County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Storm damage in Middle Tennessee can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

Reading Wilson County's NOAA file

The Wilson County file is large, and thunderstorm wind fills most of it. NOAA logs 252 thunderstorm wind entries across 78 separate dates, the first on April 6, 2016 and the latest on June 9, 2026. Estimates stand behind the gust in 247 entries and instruments behind 5; 34 entries sit between 35 and 48 knots and 218 reach 50 or more. July is the busiest month (65 entries on 17 days), with June next (50 on 16), then May (30 on 11), April (28 on 9), March (20 on 6) and December (20 on just 3). One year towers over the rest: 2016 has 86 entries, against 34 in 2017 and 27 in 2018, and the recent tally is 5 in 2021, 4 in 2025 and 16 in 2026 so far.

Much of that total traces to particular dates. July 8, 2016 supplies 19 entries between 7:58 and 8:29 p.m. CST and June 15, 2016 supplies 17 between 3:14 and 3:45 p.m. CST; three more dates carry 11 each: December 17, 2016 (11:14 to 11:21 p.m. CST), March 30, 2017 (6:41 to 7:00 p.m. CST) and November 18, 2017 (4:54 to 5:27 p.m. CST). The December 17 entry labeled Laguardo is a 70-knot estimate; NOAA's narrative describes a microburst a quarter mile wide with estimated winds as high as 80 mph, plus a house on Dana Drive that "lost most of its west facing shingles", which were blown up to 150 yards. Three entries are 74 knots, every one an estimate: Cherry Vly at 7:41 a.m. CST on March 1, 2017, where a narrative about a downburst south of Watertown mentions a house losing shingles on File Road, and Taylorsville (5:06 p.m.) and Tuckers Xrds (5:20 p.m.) on May 8, 2024. The top instrument reading is 61 knots at Lebanon, 4:03 p.m. CST on May 3, 2020, and a spotter and the airport weather station at Lebanon on July 8, 2016 read 55 and 53 knots.

At 9:42 a.m. CST NOAA filed a 3.00-inch entry under Gladeville, in which a National Weather Service employee reports hail "ranging from ping pong up to tea cup size" with an average stone of lime to tennis ball size. Two minutes before that, at 9:40 a.m., a 2.75-inch entry cites an mPing report of baseball-size hail five miles east of Rural Hill, and at 9:38 a.m. a 1.75-inch Suggs Creek entry appears; four hail entries fall on that date. May 10, 2016 has five, including 1.75-inch golf-ball-size entries at Mt Juliet (10:50 and 10:55 p.m. CST) and Silver Spgs (11:00 p.m. CST). Twelve entries are 1.50 inches or more, on six dates. The months run from January through July: May has 9 entries on 3 days, June 8 on 4, and March and April 7 each on 4. Neither 2019 nor 2026 has a hail entry.

Zone-filed wind adds 2 High Wind entries (52 and 54 knots) and 9 Strong Wind entries on 8 days, 39 to 48 knots.

Thirteen tornado entries fall on 10 days. On March 1, 2017 two arrive near Watertown: a 3.32-mile EF0 at 7:37 a.m. CST and a 6.4-mile EF1 at 7:40 a.m., which NOAA's narrative calls a high end EF-1; one home on Sparta Pike took considerable roof damage and a mobile home on Linwood Road lost most of its metal roof. County, Mt. Juliet and Lebanon offices

Wilson County Building Codes is at 233 East Gay Street in Lebanon, telephone 615-444-3025 (option 1). Its Building Codes Division page names the 2021 International Residential Code among adopted codes and says permit applications are not processed after 3:30 p.m. daily. It states that "The property owner of record or a state-licensed contractor are the only individuals that can apply for building permits in Wilson County". For residential remodels it says it permits "when there are major load changes", major use changes and major dwelling-type changes; roofing is not named on the page, so put the re-roof question to the office. The remodel checklist asks for the contractor's name, a copy of the contractor's Tennessee license, and a worker's compensation certificate of liability or exemption, or an affidavit if the owner serves as his or her own contractor.

The City of Mt. Juliet Building and Codes Department, 115 Clemmons Rd. (615-773-6225), lists Monday-to-Friday hours of 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. and the 2018 International Residential Code among its adopted codes. Its permits section applies to an owner or agent who intends to "construct, enlarge, alter, repair, move, demolish, or change the occupancy of a building or structure", and the list of exceptions (fences, sidewalks, small pools, playground equipment, awnings, retaining walls) leaves roofing out. Ask the department whether a roof job counts.

The City of Lebanon's Building Inspection and Permitting page lists most of its staff on 615-444-3647 with extensions; the city's address on that page is 200 North Castle Heights Avenue. Its Guidelines page lists the 2018 International Residential Code among current adopted codes. The Lebanon pages read here do not name roofing either.

For the state license, Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors pages read in October 2026 say a Home Improvement license for residential remodeling of $3,000 to $24,999 applies "to the counties adopting the law", and Wilson is not among the nine counties named. The contractor page requires a contractor license "for projects of $25,000 or more" for a prime contractor, and for a subcontractor doing roofing work contracting directly with a contractor when that portion is $25,000 or more. A Verify a License search is linked from the pages; it was not used here, so run the lookup yourself.

- Each bidder's Tennessee license number, checked in Verify a License.
