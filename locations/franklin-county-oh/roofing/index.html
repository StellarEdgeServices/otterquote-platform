A severe storm in Franklin County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

The central Ohio climate and your roofing

NWS Wilmington's summary of April 17, 2024 confirms an EF1 tornado in Delaware County, which is in the Central division with Franklin County. NOAA's Ohio state climate summary says dangerous storms can occur in every season, and it lists snowstorms and ice storms among winter weather. It lists thunderstorms, flood-producing rainstorms, hail and tornadoes among the convective storms it calls common in the warmer months.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Franklin County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

Permits and local rules

Columbus Building and Zoning Services settles the question on its Frequently Asked Questions page: "The replacement of a roof, siding, windows or doors require a Roof, Siding, Windows, Doors permit." The same page says "This permit only comes with one inspection" and that inspections can be scheduled "through our Citizen Access Portal or automated phone system." The department's Typical Residential Projects sheet (revision BA 3/26) puts "Replace roof" under "Requires a permit, but not plan review." Neither page gives a layer limit or an ice barrier rule, so ask the department at 614-645-7433.

The Department of Commerce describes the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board as licensing "electrical, HVAC, plumbing, hydronics and refrigeration commercial contractors." A roofer does not appear in that list, and inside Columbus the credential comes from the city, which calls it a license. Columbus defines a Home Improvement Contractor category for structural work on an existing one, two or three family dwelling, plus a Home Improvement - Limited Contractor category for work "limited to a specific scope (ex: roofing, decks, fencing, etc.)." Its application sheet asks a limited contractor for "a minimum of one (1) full year of 'hands-on' experience in that field." The Citizen Access Portal has a "Search for a Contractor" link under General Information.

Outside Columbus, the Franklin County Building Department says it is "responsible for issuing building permits for 1-, 2- and 3-family residential structures and associated development." For Brown, Clinton, Franklin, Hamilton, Madison, Mifflin, Norwich, Pleasant, Sharon and Truro townships, it says projects must apply for a Certificate of Zoning Compliance through the county's Economic Development and Planning Department. Re-roofing is not mentioned on that page, so put the roof question to the county department directly.

Frequently asked questions

Who decides whether my policy applies to roof damage in Franklin County?

Your insurer decides coverage under the terms of your policy. Read your policy, ask your adjuster what is included, and document the damage with dated photos. Our guide on filing a property damage claim walks through the process step by step.

Do I need to be home when my contractor or insurer inspects the roof?
