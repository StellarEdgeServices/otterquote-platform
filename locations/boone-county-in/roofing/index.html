Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Boone County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Storm damage in central Indiana can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

Reading Boone's clocks and gaps

Boone's 87 rows all come with coordinates, and the Census Bureau geocoder puts each in Boone County. NOAA's format guide calls its times "usually in LST" (local standard time); the Boone rows say EST, which means an hour must be added during daylight time, in force for 83 of the 87. Three rows cross midnight after conversion: Thorntown wind at 23:17 EST on August 6, 2018 (00:17 EDT August 7), the Whitestown wind row above (00:54 EDT August 7), and Thorntown hail at 23:21 EST on May 6, 2023 (00:21 EDT May 7). Not counted anywhere: 26 rows typed as forecast zones that carry the name Boone (Heavy Snow 7, High Wind 4, Extreme Cold/Wind Chill 4, Winter Storm 3, Drought 3, Winter Weather 2, and three singles), plus 8 county rows for flood and flash flood. NOAA's README describes a monthly refresh of the current year's file, so the newest Boone rows may not be final.

The forecast zone and the state's roof table

Boone County is zone INC011 in the National Weather Service's county record, assigned to the Indianapolis forecast office (IND). Indiana's amended Table R301.2(1), as reproduced in an Indiana Department of Homeland Security sheet about ice and water shield, specifies 115 mph wind, 20 psf ground snow and 30 inches of foundation depth for Boone County, and answers "No" in the column headed Ice Shield Underlayment Required. According to that sheet, the shingle manufacturer's written installation instructions still have to be followed.

Boone County's housing stock

In the Census Bureau's American Community Survey for 2020 through 2024, the DP04 profile lists 29,737 housing units in Boone County. Of those, 999 stand vacant (3.4 percent), leaving 28,738 occupied. Homeowners account for 22,641 of the occupied units (78.8 percent), renters for 6,097 (21.2 percent). Detached one-unit houses total 23,635 (79.5 percent) and mobile homes 694 (2.3 percent). Table B25035 gives the median year built as 1995, margin of error 2. Roughly three eras share the stock: 11,180 units (37.6 percent) went up before 1980, 5,840 (19.6 percent) in the 1980s and 1990s, and 12,717 (42.8 percent) in 2000 or later. The largest single decade is the 2010s at 6,226 units (20.9 percent), and 3,420 units (11.5 percent) date from 1939 or earlier. A build year says nothing about when a roof was last replaced.

Four permit offices in one county

The Boone County Area Plan Commission operates from the Boone County Office Building, 116 W. Washington St., Suite 101, Lebanon, (765) 482-3821, open 8am to 4pm on weekdays. Its office page lists one function as issuing "improvement location permits (building permits) for new construction". No page read for this research says which towns the Commission serves, so this page does not say who issues permits where. The residential permit packet (dated 6-18-2026) includes a fee table whose specialty section has a line reading "Roof Upgrade"; a 3-1-2026 packet also fetched carries the same line. The packet warns that "If work begins without a permit, the permit fee will be double." Its scope list names new homes, accessory structures, additions, fences and pools, and it leaves open whether a re-roof counts as a "Roof Upgrade", so put that to the office.

The City of Lebanon Planning Department, 401 S Meridian St, (765) 482-8845, says it is "responsible for issuing Building Permits, setting up and regulating Building Inspections" among other duties. Its fee schedule under Ordinance 2026-10, effective August 20, 2026, lists a "Reroof" line under Other Residential Permits, marked "(tear off and replacement)". The only contractor licenses on that schedule are annual electrical, HVAC and plumbing ones; roofing is not named. Fee amounts on both the county and Lebanon documents are left out here, so confirm the current ones with each office.

Zionsville's Planning and Building Department, Town Hall, 1100 W Oak St, (317) 873-5410, says on its FAQ page that re-roof and sewer repair permits are handled on a walk-in basis. A separate, undated roofing FAQ letter from the town's community and economic development director responds to the question "Is a permit or inspection required when replacing roof shingles?" with "YES". The letter says the 2020 Indiana Residential Code is enforced in full when a roof is replaced, and that ignoring a shingle maker's instructions would be a code violation. Whitestown's Building Department, 6210 Veterans Drive, (317) 732-4539, says it "processes, reviews, and issues all building permits within the Town of Whitestown"; its fee schedule effective March 12, 2025 has no roofing line.

Where state licensing stops

Roofing has no entry on the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency's profession list, which does include plumbing, real estate and home inspectors. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet says "Licensing requirements vary from community to community" and sends readers to the city or county building department. In Boone County that points to Lebanon's trade licenses, which leave roofing out, and to what each town and the Area Plan Commission say. No contractor registration page was found for Zionsville or Whitestown.

Questions to settle before Boone County bids arrive

- Identify the office that handles the address: the Area Plan Commission, Lebanon, Zionsville, Whitestown or another town.

- Ask each bidder whether a re-roof needs a permit there, who pulls it and who requests the inspection.

- Ask Lebanon or the Area Plan Commission which fee fits a full replacement, since each names a roofing line.

- Ask whether the shingle maker's instructions call for ice and water shield.

- If your insurer has already written an estimate, give each bidder the same copy.
