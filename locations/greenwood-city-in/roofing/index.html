Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Johnson County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Post-storm weeks can be congested, and quotes gathered in a hurry may be harder to compare; the weeks after the rush can be a calmer time to review bids. Whatever the calendar says, the sequence stays the same: document first, understand your policy second, compare any written bids you receive third — and take your time rather than rushing to agree with the first person who knocks on the door.

Who issues permits in Greenwood

The city's Building Commissioner is at 300 S. Madison Ave., Greenwood, IN 46142, telephone 317.881.8698. The office's page lists building permits, inspections for permitted projects, building code questions and the investigation of potentially unsafe structural issues. Under the heading "Do I Need a Building Permit?" the page speaks to roofs directly. A roof project that removes 25 percent or more of the decking needs a permit, filed on the commercial or residential remodel packet; laying new shingles and patching unsound decking does not. The page covers two other trades a roofing bid often travels with. Siding needs a permit only when the structure of the building changes. Window replacement at the same size needs none, but a change in window size does.

Homeowners associations and the permit

Greenwood's Building Commissioner page lists an "Affirmation of Notification Form (HOA Notification)" among its permit forms. The form quotes the city's Municipal Code, Chapter 10, Article 8, Section 10-130, subsection 8.03.05: the city "shall not be a part of, nor be responsible for enforcement of, subdivision covenants," except that before issuing a permit for construction the Building Commissioner "shall require the applicant to affirm in writing" that the subdivision's Architectural Control Committee has been sent notice of the proposal. The applicant checks one of five lines, running from no association existing for the subdivision to an association that has been notified and has denied the project.

A separate "For Homeowner Associations" page says the city recognizes a committee for the purpose of reviewing, among other things, "Additions to, or exterior remodeling of, single-family or two-family dwellings," and that "Final authority for issuing a permit shall, in all cases, rest with the Building Commissioner." None of these pages says whether notice is due for a shingle replacement that stays under the 25 percent decking line, so put that to the office, and read your subdivision's covenants yourself, since the city says it does not enforce them.

Licenses in Greenwood and in Indiana

Greenwood's Licensing page, which names the City Controller, lists license types from Adult Entertainment and Amusements to Peddler, Solicitors, Transient Merchant and Taxicab Driver or Business, and names no roofing or contractor registration. Whether any of the door-to-door categories applies to a roofer who knocks at your home is a question for the City Controller's office. Indiana's Professional Licensing Agency lists professions from plumbing and real estate to home inspectors, and roofing is not among them. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet says to contact the local building department to see if a permit is needed and that "Licensing requirements vary from community to community."

Questions for your Greenwood bids

- Ask the Building Commissioner's office whether the decking work in your bid reaches the 25 percent line, and note the name of the person who answered.

- Ask each bidder how much decking the bid assumes will be replaced, and what the contract says if the crew finds more damaged decking after tear-off.

- If your house is in a subdivision, ask whether it has an Architectural Control Committee and whether notice goes to it before the permit is requested.

- Ask whether the quote includes drip edge and flashing, and whether the shingle maker's instructions call for ice and water shield.

- Give every bidder the same copy of any written estimate from your insurer.
