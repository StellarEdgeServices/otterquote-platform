The West Tennessee climate and your roofing

NWS Memphis studied tornadoes from 2007 to 2020 in its warning area and found that April accounted for 57 of 276 tornadoes, with May next at 50. The study reports that tornadoes occurred most frequently between 5:00 and 6:00 PM, with 40 of the 276 in that hour. It adds that tornado activity dramatically lowers from June through November. NWS Memphis also says severe weather reports peak in the late afternoon and early evening hours, and the Memphis warning area includes Madison County's region.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Madison County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Storm damage in West Tennessee can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

Reading the labels and the clock

Even so, a label can borrow a neighboring name: the May 3, 2020 row labeled Fairview carries "Four trees were knocked down in Three Way." NOAA's bulk-data documentation says its times are usually in local standard time (LST). Madison rows say CST throughout, so during daylight time add an hour; 94 of the 108 rows fall in that stretch. Memphis forecasters and the local emergency agency

The National Weather Service's county record lists Madison County as zone TNC113 and names the Memphis forecast office. That office's headline table defines a severe thunderstorm watch as conditions favoring storms with hail an inch across or larger and/or gusts of 58 mph or stronger, equal to 50 knots. The Jackson-Madison County Emergency Management Agency, 239 Grady Montgomery Drive, (731) 427-1271, lists "protect and preserve life and property" as its mission.

Renters hold 15,255 of the occupied units (38.0 percent) and owners 24,898 (62.0 percent). One-unit detached houses make up 30,947 (70.2 percent) and mobile homes 2,288 (5.2 percent). Two permit offices, one road

Madison County's Building Department, 100 E. Main St., Suite 100, Jackson, (731) 423-6014, serves weekdays from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. The department's permit page tells any owner or agent planning to construct, enlarge, alter, repair, demolish or move, among other listed work, to apply to the Building Commissioner first, under the 2018 International Building Code plus the matching residential code. Inspection requests are due by 3:30 p.m. the day before. The residential exemption list runs through sheds up to 200 square feet, fences, decks, prefabricated pools no deeper than 24 inches and more, but never roofing, so put the re-roof question to the office.

The City of Jackson keeps a separate Building and Codes office at 119 East Main St., Suite 208, (731) 425-8262. For a home within the city, its Construction Guide, revised January 2022, states "Residential Building Permits are required for all construction, alteration or repairs, fences, accessory buildings, etc. when the total value is $100.00 (one hundred dollars) or more." That paragraph does not name roofing. Three Way's town website reports that Madison County's Building Department handles permit issuance there, and its short list of required permits makes no mention of re-roofing. No other town's office was read.

Licensing, as three offices word it

The state's contractor-licensing board says a subcontractor hired by a contractor, not the owner, needs a license for roofing work from $25,000 upward for that portion. On Madison County's license page, a prime contractor needs a license "prior to offering a price, bid, or signing a contract" at that same level. The city guide words the line as exceeding $25,000, which makes a bid sitting exactly there worth asking about. A separate Home Improvement tier, for work between $3,000 and $24,999, applies only in nine named counties that opted in; Madison is not one. The state's license look-up link opens a page titled "Verify Public Search" with no readable text in a plain fetch, so no lookup was done.

Lining up your Madison County bids

- Work out which office covers the property: Madison County's Building Department, City of Jackson, or the town where the property sits.

- Ask whether a replacement roof needs a permit, since neither the county exemption list nor the city paragraph says.

- Settle who gets the permit, and who phones in inspections by the 3:30 p.m. deadline.

- Check whether any single bid lands exactly on the licensing line, and how each office words it.

- Your insurer's written estimate, if one exists, shown to every bidder.
