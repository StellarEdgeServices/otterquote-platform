Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Sumner County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

Three offices, and the state license

Sumner County Building and Codes sits at 355 N. Belvedere Drive, Room 208, in Gallatin (615-452-1467). Its page calls the department "committed to ensure that a high quality of construction is maintained", lists "Newly Adopted 2021 ICC Building Codes", and notes that the 2015 county codes are "No longer enforced as of 5/19/23". The building permit application says a copy of the contractor's license, a certificate of liability made out to Sumner County and proof of workers compensation insurance must accompany it. Neither the page nor the application names roofing, and neither says which addresses the county covers, so put the re-roof question to the department.

The City of Hendersonville Building and Codes page gives Monday-to-Friday permitting hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., offers 615-822-3802 for help with its online portal, and lists the 2021 International Residential Code with amendments among adopted codes. The City of Gallatin Building Codes Department is at 132 W Main Street, Room 202 (615-451-5968), and says it handles construction permits inside the city limits. Its permit-requirements page lists common projects without listing re-roofing, so ask the department about a re-roof.

On the state side, Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors pages read in October 2026 say a Home Improvement license for residential remodeling of $3,000 to $24,999 applies "to the counties adopting the law", and Sumner is not among the nine counties named. The contractor page requires a contractor license "for projects of $25,000 or more" for a prime contractor, and for a subcontractor doing roofing work contracting directly with a contractor when that portion is $25,000 or more. A Verify a License search is linked from the pages; it was not used here, so run the lookup yourself.

Before bids arrive

- Your insurer's written estimate, if you have one, in a form each bidder can read.

- Which office answers for your address: the county, Hendersonville, Gallatin or another city.

- If you are in Hendersonville, the city's roofing sheet, so a bidder can say how ice barrier, drip edge and valley lining are handled.

- A question for each bidder: who applies for the permit, and who supplies the license, liability and workers compensation papers the county's application asks for?

- Each bidder's Tennessee license number, checked in Verify a License.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be home when my contractor or insurer inspects the roof?

For the exterior portion, usually not — but being present means you see the documented damage yourself and can ask questions of whoever inspects the roof.

Should I repair or replace after partial-slope damage?

Shingle availability and the age of the roof both come into it; your insurer can tell you how your policy addresses matching. Written bids that price both paths let you compare the two side by side.
