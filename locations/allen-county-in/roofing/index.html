A roof in Allen County can take hail strikes and wind uplift during a storm, and when it fails, the damage rarely stays confined to the shingles.

The northern Indiana climate and your roofing

Allen County sits in northern Indiana. NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says the state's location in the interior of North America, with no mountains to the north or south, exposes it to bitterly cold Arctic air masses in winter and warm, humid air masses from the Gulf of Mexico in summer. It adds that dangerous storms can occur in every season: winter can bring snow and ice storms, and thunderstorms capable of producing floods, hail and tornadoes are common in the warmer months.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Allen County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

A short list of heavy storm dates in NOAA's file

Filter NOAA's Storm Events bulk files for 2016 through 2026 to Allen County, Indiana, and you get 238 entries, the first dated March 27, 2016 and the latest June 11, 2026. NOAA marks the 2026 entries as preliminary. The counts here leave out 51 more rows that NOAA files under forecast zones named Allen, such as High Wind and winter weather entries; NOAA warns that events filed under forecast zones may be missing. Thunderstorm wind makes up 156 of them, but they fall on only 39 days, so the county's wind history is concentrated on a small number of dates. Hail adds 63 entries on 25 days, tornadoes 5 entries on 4 days, and flooding 9 entries. Of the wind entries, 25 give a measured gust and 131 an estimated one. July holds 54 wind entries on 8 days, more than any other month, followed by March with 28 on 4 days, August with 25 on 6 days, June with 21 on 8 days and May with 20 on 8 days.

Two years stand out. NOAA files 42 wind entries on 6 days in 2022, and 31 on 6 days in 2020. The single busiest date is July 5, 2022 with 22 entries; the episode narrative says "Widespread wind damage occurred in an area from Steuben County through Allen County in Indiana." March 6, 2022 follows with 14. One entry that day reads "The ASOS at Fort Wayne International Airport recorded a 67 mph wind gust," and another says county dispatch received "reports of power flashes and tree damage across the entire county."

The hail entries are a different pattern: 41 of the 63, on 14 days, give 1.00 inch or more. Three entries read 2.00 inches, two filed under Georgetown on April 19, 2017 and one under Greendale on June 1, 2019. Three more read 1.75 inches: Royal Oaks on May 16, 2019 and again on July 23, 2022, and Huntertown on June 13, 2022. June 1, 2019 is the heaviest hail date at 10 entries; NOAA's narrative says surface CAPE values were "in excess of 3000 J/KG" and that thunderstorms "became prolific hail producers." Winter is not hail-free here. February holds 8 hail entries on 2 days, six of them on February 19, 2026, when the narrative notes that low freezing levels produced "efficient hail producing storms" and that one storm left "a swath of 1 to 2 inches of pea size or larger hail along its path."

What the entries say about buildings is mostly about shingles and trees. On March 1, 2017 an entry from Wallen reports that "the roof of a business was damaged as well as parts of the interior of the building." A May 19, 2019 entry from Aboite describes "shingles blown off roofs." On June 21, 2021, in Zulu, "A large pole barn had the entire metal roof ripped off and blown across the road towards the east," and debris from it struck two homes about 220 feet away. A February 27, 2023 entry from Westlawn says "A home sustained some shingle and siding damage," and a second from Cedar Canyons that day notes "some roof damage to a residence."

The five tornado entries include an EF3. It was recorded on August 24, 2016, and ran 5.16 miles from Milan Center to Georgetown. NOAA's narrative says "A poorly anchored home was wiped off its foundation," that a two-story barn was leveled, and that maximum winds were estimated at 160 mph. An EF2 on March 31, 2023 began on the northeast side of Fort Wayne at Maplewood Park and, in the narrative's words, "raced northeast toward the town of Harlan. Many homes and farms had minor to major damage." A brief EF0 formed near Harlan the same day. On August 20, 2016 an EF0 near Brookside Estates left "minor shingle damage" at one Eby Road residence, and an EF0 on July 29, 2023 crossed the county line from Whitley County and brought minor roof and siding damage to one home. The file does not say how many addresses lay along each path.

An entry is a report, not an inspection. The entries above cite spotters, members of the public, emergency managers and airport weather stations. The tallies in this section are the page's own counts of those rows.

Permits and local rules

The Allen County Building Department, at 200 E Berry St., Suite 180 in Fort Wayne, publishes a one-page Residential Permit Guide marked 03/04/2026. Its flowchart first asks whether you are adding or expanding a structure. If not, it asks: "Does the work involve any mechanical, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or structural alteration?" Where the answer is yes, the list headed "PERMIT REQUIRED FOR" includes an item for roofing and siding, with one submittal item, a construction plan. Where the answer is no, the guide says "You do not need a permit from the Allen County Building Department," then adds that "Additional permits or approval may be required from another agency." The chart's pictures put roofing and siding on the yes side. The department's Introduction to Home Projects page is plainer: its list of "Examples of projects needing permits" begins with "Re-roofing" and includes "Replacing windows." Questions go to (260) 449-7131.

Licensing is addressed in the department's Rules and Regulations, nineteenth edition, effective 9/6/2024. Section A.7.b says an individual who contracts in "the improving, remodeling, and repair of existing one- and two-family structures shall be required to be licensed with the Building Department," and the sub-contractor list in A.8 includes a roofing category. The department's FAQ asks "Is my project in the Allen County jurisdiction?" and answers with a link to a jurisdiction map. Since November 7, 2025, permit applications for properties inside Fort Wayne's local historic districts follow a new process that the department says is meant to ensure consistent review of historic preservation requirements.

One more wrinkle concerns the codebook. The department's page lists an Indiana Residential Code based on the 2018 International Residential Code, effective December 26, 2019, and the page does not say whether a later edition has taken its place. Ask which edition applies to a project before you assume a detail.

What to settle before you ask for bids

- Whether your address sits in the department's jurisdiction map or in a town that issues its own permits.

- Whether the written bids say who obtains the permit and who calls for inspections.

- The date of the most recent storm to reach your street, with photos taken afterward.

- The year your roof was last replaced, and whether any record of that work exists.
