A severe storm in Shelby County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

The West Tennessee climate and your roofing

Shelby County is part of the Western Grand Division defined in Tennessee Code Annotated section 4-1-204. NWS Memphis says June is the most active month for thunderstorm wind events in its warning area, and that the majority of those events occur between March and July. It says hail occurs most frequently from March through June, with May the most active month.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Shelby County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Post-storm weeks can be congested, and quotes gathered in a hurry may be harder to compare; the weeks after the rush can be a calmer time to review bids. Whatever the calendar says, the sequence stays the same: document first, understand your policy second, compare any written bids you receive third — and take your time rather than rushing to agree with the first person who knocks on the door.

Weather reports on file for Shelby County

The hail file for Shelby County is smaller than the wind file, but it has some winter dates. From March 2016 to April 2026, NOAA's Storm Events Database logs 66 hail reports across 32 days, and its 2026 entries are preliminary. Hail appears in 11 calendar months and never in October. April and May carry 34 of the 66 reports, and May has hail on 9 separate days, more than any other month. The busiest year is 2025, with 17 reports, 10 of them on April 2, 2025.

The largest stone in that NOAA file is 2.00 inches, logged under Capleville on January 25, 2021, with a narrative reading "Two-inch hail reported on Shelby Drive." On April 2, 2025, NOAA lists a 1.75-inch report from Lenow and another, filed under Collierville ARPK, whose narrative is a Facebook report of golf-ball-size hail near Shelby Drive and Forest Hill Irene Road. Seven NOAA reports from February 2023 through April 2025 give 1.50 inches: Millington (February 15, 2023), Bailey (April 27, 2023, where the narrative puts ping-pong-size hail at Houston Levee Road and Poplar Avenue), White Station (August 9, 2023), Germantown (September 5, 2023), Brunswick (July 8, 2024, along Highway 70 near Lakeland City Hall), Ellendale (March 30, 2025) and Cordova (April 2, 2025).

Thunderstorm wind is the bigger count. Between March 2016 and April 2026, NOAA records 188 thunderstorm wind reports on 77 days, and May, June and July supply 111 of them; July alone has 41. The busiest year in that NOAA record is 2023, with 55 reports, including 10 on June 25 and 7 each on July 18 and July 21. Earlier, May 27, 2017 produced 10 reports under labels such as West Union, Frayser, Cordova and Berclair. The top estimated gust in the file is 78 knots at Raleigh on March 9, 2017, in a narrative that sets the scene near the old Raleigh Springs Mall. A 74-knot report at Memphis International Airport on March 31, 2023 comes with a narrative about straight-line winds across southern and southeast Shelby County that evening. Another 18 NOAA entries are filed as High Wind or Strong Wind; two of them quote Memphis International Airport gusts of 55 mph on March 25, 2024 and 58 mph on September 1, 2024.

NOAA's file for January 2016 through April 2026 holds a single tornado report for the county: an EF1 on October 21, 2019, which began at Memphis International Airport and lifted in the Balmoral area of East Memphis. These are weather entries for the whole county. None describes a specific house, and none predicts a coming storm.

Owners live in 196,379 of the occupied units (54.1 percent) and renters in 166,362. Permits and licenses for a roof replacement

Memphis and Shelby County Construction Enforcement, on develop901.com, says it reviews plans, issues construction permits and inspects projects in "Memphis, Arlington, Germantown, Lakeland, Millington, and unincorporated Shelby County." Bartlett and Collierville are not on that list, so a home there should check with its own town. Its Policy for Roof Installations, revised June 8, 2022, answers the reroof question for a single-family home: "No, a permit is not required unless the decking is to be repaired or altered." It adds that "New sheathing or repair to sheathing does require a permit," and that because reroofs need no permit, "we may never see the work that was done."

The policy sets Chapter 9 of the 2021 International Residential Code as the standard, with no amendment "other than we do not require an ice barrier." It answers "Yes" on underlayment and on a drip edge. For existing decking, it allows gaps up to an average of 1/4 inch; beyond that, the gaps must be filled or the deck overlaid with 3/8-inch structural panel sheathing, and an overlay "will require a letter from a structural engineer." Where a code violation is brought to the office's attention, the policy says "we will cite the owner of the property." On how many roof layers a house may have, it says the answer depends on site-specific circumstances and the materials used, and it points the reader to the building code, so ask the office.

The policy also defines three terms. A roof repair is "reconstruction or renewal of any part of an existing roof," recovering is installing "an additional roof covering over a prepared existing roof covering without removing the existing roof covering," and replacement is removing the covering, repairing "any damaged substrate" and installing a new one.

For permit records, the Construction Enforcement page offers a permit activity search that lets you "Search and download permits and associated documents for submitted applications." Its questions and answers say a homeowner may make improvements to a single-family dwelling under TCA section 62-6-103 when the home is not for resale, rent or lease, and that a statement of ownership letter is needed before the permit issues. A separate answer says Tennessee-licensed contractors register that license with Construction Enforcement, which asks for a registration fee and a surety bond except for work regulated by the State Alarm Board, and it gives the Licensing Section's number, 901-222-8390.

The roof policy asks whether a Tennessee license is required for roofing in Shelby County and answers "Yes, when a project is over $3,000." The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors pages, read in early October 2026, give two tiers: a Home Improvement license for residential remodeling "from $3,000 to $24,999" in nine counties, Shelby being the last one listed, and a contractor license "for projects of $25,000 or more." The Verify a License search on tn.gov looks up a number.

Before you ask for bids on a Shelby County roof

- Your insurer's estimate, if you have one, kept at hand for every bidder to read.

- A question for each bidder about underlayment and drip edge, which the county policy says it enforces.

- A question for each bidder: will the job involve deck repair or new sheathing, and who pulls the permit if it does?

- The license number for each bidder, searched in Verify a License, plus the bidder's registration with Construction Enforcement.
