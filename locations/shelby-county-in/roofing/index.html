A severe storm in Shelby County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

The central Indiana climate and your roofing

Shelby County sits in central Indiana. NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says dangerous storms can occur in every season, that winter can bring snow and ice storms, and that thunderstorms capable of producing floods, hail and tornadoes are common in the warmer months. It records that heavy snowstorms during the first week of January 2014 dropped 1 to more than 2 feet of snow over central and northern Indiana.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Shelby County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Post-storm weeks can be congested, and quotes gathered in a hurry may be harder to compare; the weeks after the rush can be a calmer time to review bids. Whatever the calendar says, the sequence stays the same: document first, understand your policy second, compare any written bids you receive third — and take your time rather than rushing to agree with the first person who knocks on the door.

Searching the agency's yearly Storm Events files for 2016 through 2026 for the county name SHELBY turns up 62 thunderstorm wind entries on 41 dates, beginning March 31, 2016 and ending June 17, 2026, against only 8 hail entries and 6 tornado entries. Fifty-three wind entries are estimates (EG) and 9 are measurements (MG), and every one but a single 45-knot report is at 50 knots or stronger. June 18, 2021 supplies five entries and July 27, 2020 four.

The strongest values come from one afternoon. On May 21, 2022, two Mount Auburn entries carry 78 knots, stamped 14:53 EST (15:53 daylight time) and 14:57 EST (15:57 daylight time). The first, from an emergency manager, reports winds of 75 to 90 mph that downed or uprooted numerous trees along an EF1 tornado track in the far south of the county. Since both rows describe the same storm, they should be read as one event, not two. The next value down is 70 knots at Waldron on July 29, 2021, 19:04 EST (20:04 daylight time), from a 911 call center, with "Minor tree and roof damage". Morristown on March 31, 2023, 22:56 EST (23:56 daylight time), records 61 knots and a gas station canopy brought down.

Automated airport stations supply nine entries, all filed under a Shelbyville Municipal Airport label; a tenth entry under that label is a 911 center report. A mismatch sits in one: on June 18, 2021 at 18:59 EST (19:59 daylight time) the magnitude column says 59 knots while the narrative says "A 69 MPH thunderstorm wind gust was measured at the KGEZ ASOS." Fifty-nine knots works out to about 68 mph, so the two figures do not agree and neither is used as a county peak here. The March 31, 2023 airport entry says gusts reached "as high as 67 mph", which does fit 59 knots.

Roofs appear in several narratives. Smithland, June 1, 2016, 14:55 EST (15:55 daylight time): roofs "were off of barns". Fairland, July 11, 2017: "A roof was partially blown off a barn." London, July 29, 2019: roof damage to an apartment complex, severity unknown. Shelbyville, July 29, 2021, 18:54 EST (19:54 daylight time): "Roofing material blown from a building at the intersection of South Pike Street and Howard Street." London, June 11, 2026, 22:11 EST (23:11 daylight time): "significant roof damage to east side of 2 story building." At Geneva on March 7, 2022 the entry reads 02:22 EST; daylight time had not yet begun that year, so the clock needs no change. It notes minor roof damage and four to six telephone poles snapped at ground level.

Six tornado entries cover four dates. July 29, 2021 has two EF1 entries. The Prescott one, 18:58 EST (19:58 daylight time), ran 2.5 miles and opens with a strong rear flank downdraft that "ripped the roof off a building in the southeast part of Shelbyville" before the tornado itself began. The Waldron one, 0.71 miles at 19:14 EST (20:14 daylight time), crossed the Flatrock River and toppled gravestones in a cemetery. May 21, 2022 has two more EF1 entries at Mount Auburn: a 14.6-mile track up to 100 yards wide with peak winds estimated at 110 mph, and a 0.23-mile one in the Auburn Hills subdivision. May 7, 2024 brought an EF0 at Middletown, 0.78 miles at 19:02 EST (20:02 daylight time), of which NOAA says nearby homes had "little damage other than some shingles off and gutters torn off." The newest, an EF1 labeled London at 22:07 EST (23:07 daylight time) on June 11, 2026, started in Johnson County; 9.47 of its 11.94 miles lie in Shelby County, and the narrative puts the peak wind at 105 mph.

Counting the rows and what was set aside

NOAA's source column names the public for 22 entries, law enforcement for 12, emergency managers for 12, airport weather stations for 9, storm surveys for 6 and 911 centers for 5, with the remaining 10 split among four other kinds of reporter. All have coordinates, but the Census Bureau's geocoder places only 72 in Shelby County: three fall in Hancock County (the Pleasant View and Fountaintown wind entries of May 28, 2017 and the Fountaintown hail entry of July 2, 2023) and one in Decatur County (Waldron wind, May 16, 2019). NOAA files all four under Shelby, so they stay in the counts, and the Fountaintown wind entry is not featured because of it.

Per NOAA's format guide, times are "usually in LST" (local standard time). Shelby entries say EST, and daylight time adds an hour for 69 of the 76. Excluded from every count are 28 forecast-zone entries carrying the name Shelby (Heavy Snow 7, High Wind 6, Extreme Cold/Wind Chill 5, Winter Storm 3, Excessive Heat 3, and four single entries) and 8 county entries for flood and flash flood. Zone INC145 and the state's design table

Shelby County is zone INC145 in the National Weather Service record and sits under the Indianapolis office (IND). The county's line in the amended Table R301.2(1), reproduced in an Indiana Department of Homeland Security ice and water shield sheet whose file title reads "Ice and Water Shield Update 2020-02-25", gives 115 mph for wind, Seismic Design Category B, 20 psf ground snow, a 30-inch foundation depth and "No" for Ice Shield Underlayment Required. Per the sheet, a "No" county can still require the layer when the manufacturer's written instructions do.

Two registration systems and a county permit chart

Shelbyville's rules and the county's differ, so the address matters. The City of Shelbyville Planning and Building Department, 44 West Washington Street, (317) 392-5102, says a contractor must be registered with the city's Building Commission before any permit application is submitted. The page lists a $50 registration fee, a certificate of general liability insurance and a $10,000 surety bond payable to the city, with every registration expiring at the end of the calendar year; homeowners working on their own residence are excepted. The city's 2027 combined improvement location and building permit application, which asks for the signature of a registered contractor, has no reroofing line and its page does not say whether a reroof needs a permit.

The Shelby County Building Inspector, 25 W. Polk St., Room 201, (317) 392-6480, open Monday through Thursday 7 am to 5 pm, serves unincorporated land and the Town of Fairland, though the page also lists a separate Fairland contact. Its chart, "Projects that Require a Building Permit", marks re-roofing "NO" and roof decking "NO" but replacing or repairing roof rafters "YES", and a footnote defines re-roofing as tearing off old shingles and laying new ones, with no more than two layers of any roofing material. The county's contractor form carries a $90 fee, a liability certificate and a $5,000 surety bond payable to the county commissioner. The county page says general contractors must register unless the owner acts as general contractor; it does not say which category covers a roofer. Morristown's Planning and Building page names a building inspector and says to allow up to 48 hours for an inspection; its residential application package is a scan whose cover says last revised February 2009, and only that cover was read.

Roofing licenses in Indiana

The Indiana Professional Licensing Agency's profession list names plumbing, real estate and home inspectors and has no roofing entry. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet says to call the local building department about permits and that licensing requirements vary by community. In Shelby County, that leaves the two registrations above and whatever the other towns require.

Questions for your Shelby County bids

- Find out whether the address is inside Shelbyville, in Morristown or Fairland, or in the unincorporated county.

- Ask each bidder for the registration it holds with that office, and what bond and insurance sit behind it.

- Ask whether the job counts as re-roofing only or includes rafter work, because the county chart treats them differently.

- Ask whether the shingle maker's instructions call for ice and water shield, since the state table says "No" for Shelby.

- Give every bidder the same copy of any written estimate from your insurer.
