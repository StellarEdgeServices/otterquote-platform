Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Stark County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Stark County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Wind is what this county's file is mostly about. The Storm Events Database holds 88 thunderstorm wind entries over 40 days, June 16, 2016 through June 10, 2026, while hail has 27 entries over 16 days, a ratio of better than three to one. Gust figures came from estimates in 86 entries and from instruments in 2, and each of the 88 reads 50 knots or higher. June's share is 19 entries on 13 days. April, by contrast, packs 21 entries into just 3 days, and July tallies 15 entries across 8. Looking year by year, 2019 is highest (21) with 2020 next (18).

A handful of single dates stand out. Seven entries on April 1, 2023 are stamped between 11:30 and 11:46 a.m. EST; five are 60-knot estimates under Pinn, Cairo, Harrisburg, Avondale and Canal Fulton. The night of April 7, 2020 supplies 12, each stamped between 11:00 and 11:30 p.m. EST, which a daylight-time wall clock would already call April 8; most read 55 knots, under Canal Fulton, North Lawrence, Newman, McDonaldsville, Navarre, Hills and Dales, Perry Heights, Massillon, Magnolia, Louisville and Canton. July 2, 2019 has six, while March 14, 2019 and May 25, 2019 have five each.

Two entries are 65 knots, the county's top figure, and both are estimates. One is an Avondale entry from March 14, 2019 at 7:20 p.m. EST. The other, labeled Congress Lake, is stamped 11:39 p.m. EST on August 24, 2023 (12:39 a.m. on August 25, daylight time), and its narrative says a garage at an apartment complex in Hartville was destroyed. Instruments peak at 56 knots, at the entry labeled Louisville Yoder Arp on May 25, 2019.

Narratives mention roofs only here and there. The April 1, 2023 Avondale entry reads "Roof blown off at Glenwood Intermediate School in North Canton", and a Canton entry from that morning reads "Large pine tree downed onto a house and caused minor roof damage in Canton".

Hail is sparser. Among 27 entries, 17 (on 12 days) are an inch or bigger. Wilmot holds the largest, 1.75 inches on June 16, 2022, described via a social media photo of roughly golf ball-size stones. Next comes Louisville at 1.50 inches on June 13, 2017, with hail the size of walnuts, and the same afternoon logs a 1.25-inch Canton entry. By month, June totals 8 entries across 5 days and April 5 across 2. Cold-season entries do exist: January 8, 2019 brings two at 0.75 inches (Pinn), and October 6, 2024 brings two at Louisville (0.75 and 1.00 inches). April 7, 2020 has four, the first at 11:08 p.m. EST. No hail entries are filed for 2016, 2021, 2023 or 2025, while 2026 has three: one on March 22 and two on June 25.

Wind with no thunderstorm attached is filed by forecast zone: 6 High Wind entries over 5 days (five estimated at 50 knots, plus a measured 51 on December 29, 2024). The file holds no Strong Wind entries.

Tornado entries number 6 and fall on 5 dates. March 14, 2019 has two EF0 lines the same evening: 8.7 miles from Brewster toward Perry Heights at 7:11 p.m. EST, and a 1.6-mile line labeled Middle Branch at 7:23 p.m. EST. NOAA's narratives speak of uprooted trees, missing shingles and siding, and, for the short line, a garage moved off its foundation. An EF0 of April 14, 2019 is listed at 3 miles in Stark County, though its narrative describes a 3.6-mile path that ended just south of the Nimisila Reservoir in Summit County. April 7, 2020 delivered the long one, 14.49 miles from Massillon to East Sparta at 11:10 p.m. EST, with ten turbine fans torn off a warehouse roof and a home losing part of its roof. October 21, 2021 produced the county's only EF1, 2.96 miles, its winds estimated at 110 mph, beginning at a park in Jackson Township and ending near Interstate 77. The most recent, June 5, 2025, is a 0.15-mile EF0 labeled Justus, and the narrative says it removed between 50 and 100 slate tiles from a barn roof.

Permits in Stark County

The Stark County Building Inspection Department sits at 7235 Whipple Ave NW, Suite A in Canton (330-451-1770), and its county page lists weekday hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. and says the department was created in 1967. By its own account it covers the unincorporated areas and the villages and cities that have contracted with the county, naming Brewster, East Canton, Hills and Dales, Limaville, Magnolia, Meyers Lake, Minerva and Waynesburg. The page also says "Alliance, Canton, Massillon and North Canton have their own building inspection departments", and that East Sparta, Louisville and Hartville run residential building departments of their own while the county inspects only their commercial structures.

Among the county's forms is a Residential Reroof Form, headed Residential Guidelines for Reroof and drawn from Section 908 of the residential code. Roof replacement, the sheet says, means taking existing layers down to the roof deck, and a recover is not allowed over a roof that is water soaked or has deteriorated into an inadequate base, over slate, clay, cement or asbestos cement tile, or over a roof that already carries two or more applications of any roof covering. On ice barrier, where the code's climate table shows a history of ice along the eaves, it describes at least two cemented layers of underlayment or a self-adhering sheet that runs 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, and 36 inches along the slope for roofs of 8 in 12 or steeper. Ventilation is set at a minimum net free ventilating area of 1/150 of the vented space, and a certification line for the owner applicant closes the sheet.

Canton's own Building and Code Enforcement office is at 424 Market Avenue N, third floor (330-430-7800), open weekdays 8:00 to 11:45 a.m. and 1:00 to 4:00 p.m., and it says it enforces the 2019 Residential Code of Ohio with amendments effective April 15, 2024. The Canton pages read here do not name roofing, so ask that office how a roof job is handled. Pages for Alliance, Massillon and North Canton were not read.

Before you ask for bids

- Whatever written estimate your insurer sent, to hand to each bidder.

- Which office answers for your address: the county, a contract village, or the building department of Alliance, Canton, Massillon or North Canton.

- The county's Residential Reroof Form, if your address falls under it, so bidders can say how its layer, ice barrier and ventilation lines apply to your roof.

- The number of layers currently on the house, since the form addresses roofs with two or more applications.
