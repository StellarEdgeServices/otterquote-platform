The East Tennessee climate and your roofing

NOAA's Tennessee state climate summary says temperatures decrease as elevation increases, averaging a 3 degree F decline per 1,000 feet. It describes the Cumberland Plateau as averaging 2,000 feet in elevation and Smoky Mountains peaks as reaching 6,000 feet, and it says these higher areas are cooler than lower-elevation areas. These are statewide statements that include Anderson County.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.

What to expect locally

Homeowners in Anderson County navigating a storm claim juggle three parallel tracks: the insurance process (adjuster inspection, scope, settlement), the contractor process (bids, scheduling, materials), and their own documentation. Keeping those tracks separate is the single most useful habit — your insurer decides coverage under your policy; your contractor proposes the repair scope; and written bids let you compare scope and pricing side by side.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

Anderson County: NOAA rows, Census counts and permit counters

Tallied from NOAA's annual Storm Events detail files covering 2016-2026, Anderson County, Tennessee carries 51 qualifying rows, split 14 hail, 37 thunderstorm wind, plus no tornado row. Several states besides Tennessee have an Anderson County of their own, so a Tennessee filter was applied. A row reports weather somewhere in the county. No row points at one roof or forecasts what comes next.

Hail is rare in this file, but when it shows up it measures an inch or more: all 14 rows. Twelve read 1.00, one reads 1.25 (Norris, June 11, 2023) and the biggest reads 1.75 (Oak Ridge, April 10, 2025). Those 14 rows land on seven separate dates. June 20, 2019 comes first; 2023 adds four: May 7, May 16, June 5 plus June 11; 2025 adds two: April 10 and March 15. Eight calendar years of the eleven never recorded hail; the closing entry is dated April 10, 2025. Weather.gov's severe thunderstorm page treats an inch as the cutoff for severe hail and then notes: "Hail this size can damage property such as plants, roofs and vehicles."

In 2025, March 15 yields four rows (Oak Ridge, Norris and Batley twice), 19:20 through 19:45 EST, equal to 20:20 through 20:45 EDT. April 10 yields three rows, 16:06 through 16:13 EST (17:06 through 17:13 EDT), and ends on the 1.75 at Oak Ridge.

Wind produces 37 rows over 28 days, February 24, 2016 through August 1, 2024. Each carries NOAA's EG flag, an estimate of gust speed; none is flagged as measured. The 50-knot reading appears 16 times, 52 knots 15 times, 55 knots five times, and 60 knots once. Summer carries it: 22 rows land in June through August, on 18 separate days; July by itself supplies 11, on 10 days. The files hold no wind row from 2018, 2025 or 2026. The busiest day is February 24, 2016, three South Clinton rows stamped 08:15, 08:16 and 08:40 EST. Daylight saving did not start until March 13, 2016, so those stamps equal local time.

The 60-knot row is Batley's, dated April 14, 2019 (15:15 EST, 16:15 EDT). Narrative text on file: "Multiple trees fell on commercial buildings, homes, and across roads" and, on Warehouse Road, a building that lost part of its roof (spelled "rook" [sic] in the file). A sibling row, same minute, 50 knots, holds the one nonzero injury count among all 51: a single direct injury at a Nesper Road home. The damage column holds 48 blanks and three readings of 0.00K; no dollar figure appears.

Labels are loose. Six of the wind entries read South Clinton at one identical coordinate pair (36.09, -84.13), and two narratives put downed trees "across the county."

Geocoding at the Census Bureau lands all 51 map points within Anderson's boundaries, Oak Ridge ones included. Wikipedia says the city straddles Anderson and Roane counties; every Oak Ridge row here sits under Anderson. NOAA fixes these rows to EST year-round; for 45 of the 51, wall clocks had already moved an hour past that stamp, onto daylight time. Separately, forecast-zone entries stay out of every count; four wind ones carry the Anderson name (High Wind twice, Strong Wind twice). Morristown, at 5974 Commerce Blvd., hosts the NWS forecast office covering the county; weather.gov's record calls the county zone TNC001. Anderson's EMA (111 South Charles G Seivers Blvd, Clinton, 865-264-6394) lists a volunteer Damage Assessment Team for state and federal aid requests. Its page carries a REPORT STORM DAMAGE button (not opened for this page) and never mentions roofs.

The office to call hinges on the street address. Anderson County's Building and Codes Enforcement Department supervises building work inside the "non annexed parts of the county", operating from Room 127 of the courthouse, 100 N Main Street, Clinton: 865-457-6244, hours weekdays, 8 until 5. Its page requires a permit for structures above 100 sq ft, or costing at least $1000, and excuses sidewalks, driveways, painting, cabinets, small decks and comparable finish work. Reroofing is on neither list, and the page warns its no-permit list may be incomplete. A scanned county handout names the 2018 International Residential and Building codes as the ones enforced; its separate form for power, pool, HVAC and solar work asks solar applicants for an "Engineer letter (can roof support solar panels)".

Inside Clinton, the codes area of the city website offers "Apply for a Building Permit (Building, Trades, Demolition)" with City Hall at 100 N Bowling Street, 865-457-0424. The application behind that link has a checkbox for alterations and repairs of a dwelling, gives codes@clintontn.net, and ends the permit after 180 days if work has not commenced. Clinton's municipal code, Title 12, adopts twelve codes and standards, including the 2021 International Residential Code. No roofing line appears in that section or the application.

In Oak Ridge, Codes Enforcement is the office that issues every building permit and enforces the construction ordinances. Applications are filed on the city's web portal; call 865-425-3532 or write codes@oakridgetn.gov. Its list of adopted codes starts with the 2024 International Residential Code. Since December 20, 2024, trade permits go to holders of a valid Oak Ridge license, or of a state-issued license covering the trade. Because part of the city lies in Roane County and the Oak Ridge pages consulted do not explain how that part is handled, ask which side of the line your address is on.

Norris, 20 Chestnut Drive, 865-494-7645, publishes its I-Codes, including the 2021 International Residential Code. There, the city "requires a permit be obtained prior to work commencing" for construction or alteration. Offices in Rocky Top, Oliver Springs and Lake City went unread.

Statewide, the license line for bidding or contracting is drawn at $25,000 by the contractors' board. A Home Improvement license tier covers residential remodeling jobs of $3,000 to $24,999, but only inside counties that elected to participate. Its roster names Marion, Hamilton, Knox, Shelby, Bradley, Haywood, Rutherford, Robertson and Davidson, nine counties in all. Neither Anderson nor Roane appears, and the page does not describe smaller bids in such counties.

Questions to settle first

- Which of these takes your street: the county, Clinton, Oak Ridge or Norris?

- Will a reroof there need a permit, and who applies for it?

- Does each price include permit charges? Ask for the permit number afterward.

- For bids from $25,000 upward, request from every bidder the license number the board issued, and look the number up at the board.

- Your insurer's written estimate, if one exists, shown to every bidder.
