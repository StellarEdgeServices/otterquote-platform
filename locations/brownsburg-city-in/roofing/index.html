The central Indiana climate and your roofing

In central Indiana, where Hendricks County is located, NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary notes that the state has a long and deadly history of tornadic storms. It identifies June 2, 1990 as Indiana's largest tornado outbreak, with 37 tornadoes, several of them at F4 intensity, and says spring and summer precipitation has generally been above average since the 1990s.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Hendricks County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Storm damage in central Indiana can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

Brownsburg, Indiana: hail, wind and the 2025 tornado in NOAA's storm files, 2016 to 2026

The Storm Events detail files that NOAA published for 2016 through 2026 carry no place called Brownsburg, only Hendricks County, so entries were placed in the town by map point. Hendricks County holds 98 hail, thunderstorm wind and tornado entries, and the Census Bureau geocoder puts 10 of them inside the town of Brownsburg by start point: 2 hail entries on 2 dates and 8 thunderstorm wind entries on 7 dates. No tornado entry starts inside the town. Four of the county's six tornado entries carry a Brownsburg label, two from August 15, 2016 and two from April 2, 2025, and the section below on the 2025 tracks explains why they are not in the count.

Hail is the thinnest part of the record. Both entries are public reports of 1.00 inch, the first at 19:12 EST (20:12 daylight time) on May 16, 2025 under a Tilden label and the second at 18:51 EST (19:51 daylight time) on June 16, 2026 under the airport label SPEEDWAY ARPT. Neither entry carries a narrative of its own, so nothing in the file says what the stones hit. Nothing is filed for hail inside the town from 2016 through 2024.

Wind runs from May 11, 2016 to December 28, 2025. Of the 8 entries, 7 are estimated gusts (EG) and 1 is measured (MG), and 7 of the 8 reach 50 knots. Year by year, entries then dates: 2016 1 and 1, 2019 2 and 2, 2020 3 and 2, 2025 2 and 2; nothing is filed for 2017, 2018, 2021 through 2024 or 2026. By month, August has 3 entries on 2 dates, May 2 on 2, and April, July and December one each. Values in knots are 45 (1), 50 (1), 51 (1), 52 (3), 54 (1) and 55 (1). The 55 is an estimated gust from a trained spotter at 04:28 EST (05:28 daylight time) on April 19, 2025, with $10,000 in NOAA property damage: "Numerous trees damaged or snapped by thunderstorm wind gusts near and in the town of Brownsburg."

August 10, 2020 is the only date with two wind entries, at 18:14 and 18:15 EST (19:14 and 19:15 daylight time). One is an NWS employee's 54-knot estimate with $15,000 in NOAA property damage, where a six to eight inch tree came down on utility lines, the line was snapped in 4 places, and the narrative says "power out for more than 36 hours." The other is a public report of a tree "blown down by thunderstorm winds." On May 11, 2016 a tree was snapped in the Austin Meadows subdivision, and on May 16, 2019 an amateur radio operator measured a 59 mph gust, which NOAA enters as 51 knots. An estimated 60 mph gust followed on August 18, 2019. The December 28, 2025 entry, a downed large tree at 19:21 EST, is the only one outside April through August and the only one stamped in standard time.

One entry is different in kind. On July 27, 2020 at 14:48 EST (15:48 daylight time) a law enforcement report describes a partly built, wood-framed building that collapsed in gusts ahead of a line of storms. NOAA enters 45 knots, with $50,000 in property damage, and its narrative says small limbs nearby showed gusts under 50 knots. It involves a structure with no roof on it, so it says nothing about finished roofs, and it is counted here only because NOAA files it as thunderstorm wind.

The 2025 tornadoes and the town line

This is where a city count and a county count part ways. NOAA files the April 2, 2025 EF2 as starting 1.33 miles southwest of the airport label BROWNSBURG ARPT and ending 6.73 miles northeast of the airport label SPEEDWAY ARPT. The geocoder does not place that start point inside Brownsburg and puts the end point in Indianapolis, so the tornado is not among the 10. The file lists it at 13.03 miles long and 200 yards wide with 1 direct injury, and its narrative says it passed through the southern portion of downtown Brownsburg. The companion EF1 is filed at 1.53 miles and 300 yards wide, with its start 2.64 miles east-northeast of the Brownsburg label point. The Weather Service's preliminary storm reports for that night, issued the next afternoon, include a thunderstorm wind damage report of "Damage in Branches subdivision neighborhood in Brownsburg" and a "Housing Development Damaged" report one mile north-northeast of the Brownsburg reference point. A track that begins outside the line can still cross it, so the count of 10 understates what happened inside the town on that evening.

The earlier tornado entries are smaller. On August 15, 2016 NOAA files an EF1 under the Brownsburg label, 0.42 miles long and 100 yards wide, starting 1.65 miles west of the Brownsburg label point, outside the town line, and ending inside it, with $7,500 in property damage. The same evening an EF0 only 0.01 miles long was filed near the county line, 4.95 miles north of Brownsburg.

Who issues permits in Brownsburg

The town's Building and Inspections page says the division reviews and inspects residential projects such as additions, remodels, new homes, accessory structures and pools, and that applicants can apply, pay and request inspections through an online portal. Its list of small projects includes fences, decks, patios and gazebos, and under Remodeling it lists "Any structural changes." The page does not name roofing, so whether your re-roof needs a permit is a question for the division. The page invites anyone unsure to email a project outline, and the phone line it gives for help with inspection requests is 317-852-1128.

The town's Unified Development Ordinance, dated August 13, 2026 on its cover, lists activities that may begin without a building permit, including "maintenance and repairs to an existing structure or site features." Neither the ordinance text nor the Building and Inspections page names a residential re-roof, so do not assume which side of that line yours falls on. Ask.

Covenants and the permit

In its section on accessory uses and structures (section 3.8), the ordinance tells property owners to refer to any Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions, says it "does not abrogate any private covenants that may apply to property," and says that approval under private covenants does not waive the ordinance's own requirements. If your street has a homeowners association, its approval and the town's permit are separate questions, so ask the association what it requires before a re-roof.

Contractor registration and roofing licenses

The Indiana Professional Licensing Agency's profession list names plumbing and home inspectors, among others, and has no roofing entry. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet says to contact the local building department and that "Licensing requirements vary from community to community." The Brownsburg pages read for this page let a contractor request inspections through the portal but describe no contractor registration rule.

Questions for your Brownsburg bids

- Ask the Building and Inspections division whether a re-roof at your address needs a permit, since neither the page nor the ordinance names one.

- Ask each bidder who pulls the permit, and who requests the inspections through the town's portal.

- Ask who will be on site when the inspector arrives, since the town fails an inspection that is not ready when the inspector arrives and charges a re-inspection fee.

- Ask whether the quote includes drip edge and flashing, and whether decking will be replaced if it is found damaged.

- Ask for the final inspection record, and keep it with your house papers.

- Give every bidder the same copy of any written estimate from your insurer.
