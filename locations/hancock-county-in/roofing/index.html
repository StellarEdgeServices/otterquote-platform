The central Indiana climate and your roofing

Hancock County sits in central Indiana. NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says dangerous storms can occur in every season, that winter can bring snow and ice storms, and that thunderstorms capable of producing floods, hail and tornadoes are common in the warmer months. It records that heavy snowstorms during the first week of January 2014 dropped 1 to more than 2 feet of snow over central and northern Indiana.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

Homeowners in Hancock County navigating a storm claim juggle three parallel tracks: the insurance process (adjuster inspection, scope, settlement), the contractor process (bids, scheduling, materials), and their own documentation. Keeping those tracks separate is the single most useful habit — your insurer decides coverage under your policy; your contractor proposes the repair scope; and written bids let you compare scope and pricing side by side.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Storm damage in central Indiana can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

NOAA's format guide calls its times "usually in LST" (local standard time); Hancock rows say EST, so an hour is added in daylight time, which covers 73 of the 80 rows, and none crosses midnight. Greenfield, two towns and a county gap

Greenfield's Planning Department, 10 S. State St., (317) 477-4320, linked a list (in the copy of its page saved June 7, 2026) titled "Residential Work Requiring a Permit" whose entries include "Reroofing with five or more sheets of decking being replaced". A companion document, "Residential Work Not Requiring a Permit," could not be read for this research and may say more about shingle-only jobs. The city's building page also says ice and water shield underlayment "is not required within the City of Greenfield, Indiana," unless the shingle maker's installation instructions call for it. That page still cites the 2005 Indiana Residential Building Code, while the state's sheet says the 2020 Indiana Residential Code governs statewide, so find out which edition the inspector uses.

McCordsville's Planning and Building Department, 6280 W. 800 N., (317) 335-3604, publishes a Permit Guide, undated, that sends applicants to an online portal. Under remodel and addition work it lists "replacing shingles or siding" among jobs that need no permit when they do not involve structural, electrical, mechanical, plumbing or HVAC items. Its inspection-procedure PDFs are linked but were not opened. Fortville's Planning and Building page, Town Hall at 714 E. Broadway St. (hours 8 am to 4 pm, Monday to Friday), gives (317) 482-4050 for its director's line, accepts applications in person or through its portal, and says a contractor must have a form on file before getting the access code used to request inspections. Its application fee schedule, dated 1.6.20, has no roofing line. For land outside the incorporated towns, Hancock County's Planning and Building department (111 American Legion Place, Suite 146, Greenfield, (317) 477-1133 for building, Monday through Friday 8 am to 4 pm) covers unincorporated land and the towns of Spring Lake and Shirley; its Permit Requirements page lists permit instructions for residential accessory or alterations work but states no roofing rule, so ask that department about reroofing at those addresses. The New Palestine office was not read.

What state pages say about roofer licensing

Indiana's Professional Licensing Agency lists plumbing, real estate and home inspectors among its professions, but not roofing. On contractor licensing, the Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet answers that "Licensing requirements vary from community to community," and tells readers to check with the city or county building department. In Hancock County the registration question is therefore local: Fortville's contractor form, and whatever Greenfield, McCordsville or the county ask; no Greenfield registration page was found.

Preparing for your Hancock County bids

- Find out which office handles the address: Greenfield, McCordsville, Fortville, another town or the county.

- Ask each bidder how many sheets of decking they expect to replace, since Greenfield's permit line turns on that count.

- Ask whether the town wants a form on file from the contractor before inspections can be requested.

- Find out which edition of the residential code the inspector uses, and whether the shingle maker's instructions call for ice and water shield.

- A copy of any written estimate from your insurer, handed to each bidder alike.
