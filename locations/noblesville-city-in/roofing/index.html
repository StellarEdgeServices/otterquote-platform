Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Hamilton County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

The forecast zone and the state's code

In National Weather Service records the county is zone INC057, handled by the Indianapolis forecast office (IND). Table R301.2(1) as amended for Indiana appears in an Indiana Department of Homeland Security sheet filed as "Ice and Water Shield Update 2020-02-25"; Hamilton's row there has 115 mph for wind, Seismic Design Category A, 20 psf of ground snow, a 30-inch foundation depth and "No" for Ice Shield Underlayment Required. The sheet says the 2020 Indiana Residential Code governs Class 2 residential construction statewide, "Regardless of any established local enforcement policies or practices to the contrary," and that local governments must require compliance. Noblesville's historic districts and older neighborhoods

A preservation group's site, noblesvillepreservation.org, lists three National Register historic districts by name on its National Register page, though another page on the same site counts four. The South 9th Street Historic District has 39 contributing buildings and 1 contributing site, the Noblesville Commercial Historic District has 54 contributing buildings, and the Catherine Street Historic District, also called the North 9th St. Historic District, has 50 contributing buildings and 1 contributing site. The same site says National Register listing does not stop private owners from altering their property and does not restrict its use or sale.

The site also divides the city into twelve Zones of Significance, which it describes as an educational tool with no legal meaning for historic districts. Its descriptions give a sense of how old the housing is. Gentlemen Farmers has large homes built in the 1880s and 1890s as country homes for local businessmen, Gilded Age holds many of the more ornate homes from the 1870s through the 1920s, Midland began as worker housing in the 1920s, and New Frontier and Broadview Manor are 1940s and 1950s houses. None of these descriptions mentions roofs.

The city's own rule is separate. Noblesville's Unified Development Ordinance says its site plan review applies to any development, redevelopment or minor modification in a Historic Preservation Overlay District. The portion read for this page does not say whether a shingle replacement counts as a minor modification, so a homeowner in an older neighborhood should ask the permit office whether a given address sits in such a district before the work is scheduled.

Roofing licenses in Indiana

On the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency's list of professions, plumbing, real estate and home inspectors appear, and roofing does not. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet says to "Contact your local building department to see if a permit is needed" and that "A contractor should not start work until the permit is issued." In Noblesville that leaves a city guide that says shingle replacement does not typically need a permit and an office to ask about anything beyond it.

Questions for your Noblesville bids

- Ask the permit office whether your job counts as replacing shingles or as changing roof area, since the city's guide treats them differently.

- Ask each bidder whether it checked with the city, and what it was told.

- Ask whether the quote covers replacing damaged decking, and how it is priced per sheet.

- Ask whether the shingle maker's written instructions require ice and water shield, even though the state table says "No" for Hamilton County.

- If your insurer wrote an estimate, give every bidder the same copy.
