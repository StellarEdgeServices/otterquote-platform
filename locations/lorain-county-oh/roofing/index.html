Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Lorain County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

Where a roof permit is handled

No Lorain County building department page turned up for this write-up, so the first question is which office covers your address. Two city offices are described below.

The City of Elyria Building Department is at 131 Court St., Suite 101 (440-326-1491). Its Required Building Permits flyer for residential exterior and interior improvements lists "Roof - New shingle, slate / other" with Yes in the permit, inspection and estimated job cost columns, No for plot plans and drawings, and Yes under historical district approval; gutters and downspouts and roof louvers are marked No in the permit column. The Repair/Remodel Existing Space application, for homes of one to three families, lists roofing among its work, with separate check boxes for reroofing over one existing layer and for remove and replace roofing.

The application's notes say a deck that is water-soaked or deteriorated to the point of being unacceptable as a base must be replaced, and that attic ventilation is required at 1 sq. ft. of vent for each 150 sq. ft. of attic area unless approved otherwise.

The same notes put the securing of permits on the owner or agent and the requesting of inspections on the contractor, and warn that "Beginning work without permit in hand shall result in payment of a double fee".

Elyria also keeps a registration for contractors, issued to the individual rather than the business, according to its instruction sheet. For building classes the sheet asks for an application, three letters of reference or three other registrations, a certificate of insurance naming the City as certificate holder, and, in the sheet's words, "25K Bond and a $125 fee". The sheet does not name roofing among its classes, so ask the department whether a roofer needs one.

The City of Lorain Building Division, at City Hall, 200 W Erie Avenue (440-204-2045), says it "issues building permits for most construction and renovation projects". The Lorain pages read here do not name roofing. Its contractor registration page says "Any contractor doing work in the City of Lorain must be registered, licensed and bonded", so ask the Building Division how a roof job is treated. No permit pages were read for other Lorain County cities, villages and townships.

Before bids arrive

- For Elyria or Lorain, a question to each bidder: who is registered with the city, and who requests the inspections?
