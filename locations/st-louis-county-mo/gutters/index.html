Gutter damage can be the visible tip of larger storm damage. Hail that dents aluminum gutters may also have hit the roof above them, which is why a proper storm inspection treats gutters, downspouts, and roof surfaces as one system.

The Northeast Prairie climate and your gutters

St. Louis County is in the Northeast Prairie, which NOAA's climate division list uses as division 2 of Missouri's six. The Missouri Climate Center says that "mean July precipitation is greatest in northeastern Missouri, largely the result of high-intensity convectional precipitation (4.4 inches), and least in southwestern Missouri (3.2 inches)." It also says that "The metropolitan areas of St. Louis and Kansas City exert a significant and measurable effect on their climates," with temperatures "elevated in both regions by a few degrees."

Gutter issues to look for after a storm

  • Downspout crushing and disconnects — drainage failures that surface as foundation and grading problems.
  • Overflow staining and landscape erosion — evidence adjusters and contractors both read when reconstructing what the storm did.
  • Gutter guards damaged or displaced — an item that is easy to leave off a first scope.
  • Seam and end-cap leaks — often storm-initiated, always worse after a freeze cycle.
  • Hail-flattened profiles — dents that reduce water-carrying capacity and often point to damage on the roof above.

What to expect locally

After a storm in St. Louis County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing matters in St. Louis County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Water is the main subject of the gutter record. NOAA's Storm Events Database lists 25 flash flood entries for St. Louis County from January 2016 through December 2025, each on its own date, and August alone has 11 of them, with July next at 6. The July 26, 2022 entry says up to 10 inches of rain fell in a period of 8 hours, and that St. Louis-Lambert International Airport "observed a new all-time record for daily precipitation: 8.64 of rain fell since midnight Central Standard Time." On August 8, 2020, up to 7 inches fell in about four hours, with "especially across the northern half of the county" flash flooding and at least 13 water rescues. On July 22, 2019, up to 6 inches fell, and the narrative puts the hardest hit area in the southern sections of downtown Eureka. May 14, 2023 brought between 3 and 6 inches over southeastern parts of the county, with flooded roads in Affton, Crestwood, Shrewsbury and Oakville.

River and creek flooding is filed separately as Flood. The entry for May 2, 2017 says the Meramec River at Eureka and Valley Park "crested well above major flood stage" and that the river flooding closed I-44 and Highway 141 where it goes under I-44. The November 4, 2024 entry says Gravois Creek at Mehlville "saw its highest crest ever recorded," while Deer Creek at Ladue and Grand Glaize Creek at Valley Park had their second highest crests on record, and it describes flooding of basements, yards and cropland. On April 5, 2025, NOAA says the Meramec at Valley Park rose into major flood and that floodwater closed Missouri Route 141 at I-44 for two days, April 8 through 10. Counting entries, NOAA files 6 Flood entries for the county in these years, 3 of them in April.

NOAA's narratives also mention gutters in connection with wind. The March 14, 2024 EF0 near Lackland Road, filed under Hanley Hills, "caused minor shingle, soffit and gutter damage to two houses." The May 16, 2025 EF2 entry filed under Clayton says the tornado touched down in west Clayton, "twisting power poles, guttering, and trees," before continuing east toward the City of St. Louis, whose part of that track is not counted here.

The Missouri Climate Center describes where this water comes from. Its Missouri climate page says that "Spring, summer, and early fall precipitation comes largely in the form of showers or thunderstorms," that thunderstorms "are most frequent from April to July," and that "Occasionally, these produce some very heavy rains." The same page says that "Tributary flooding resulting from heavy rains and which may be expected once or twice in most years, and flash flooding along minor streams following heavy thunderstorm rains, occur most frequently in the spring and early summer, April to July, but may occur during any month." It lists the Meramec River as one of the streams that drain directly into the Mississippi inside the state, entering just below St. Louis. When you compare repair estimates for gutters and downspouts, ask where the water is meant to go, since a downspout that empties beside a foundation is a different job from one tied into a drain.

For a gutter project in St. Louis County, begin with a walk around the house during or just after a steady rain, because that is when a leaking seam, a sagging run or a downspout that dumps beside the foundation becomes obvious. Take notes and photographs: which corners overflow, where water stains the fascia, and where the ground below stays soggy. A bid is only as useful as the list of problems you hand to whoever wrote the bid.

Gutters come in different shapes and sizes, and the bid should say which are being used. Five-inch and six-inch widths, aluminum and steel, seamless runs formed on site and sectional pieces joined at the ground are all things you may see offered. Ask for the profile, the material, the thickness, the color, and the number and size of downspouts. A larger downspout or an extra one at a long run is a detail worth asking about directly.

Look at the way the runs are hung. Ask whether hidden hangers or spikes are used, how far apart they sit, and what is being attached to: a solid fascia board, or wood that has begun to soften. If the fascia has to be repaired first, that should be on paper. Ask what happens to the old pieces, whether the end caps, corners and outlets are sealed, and where each downspout will discharge.

Guards and screens are optional extras that people often decide on late. Ask what the guards are made of and whether the panels come off for cleaning. Take the same questions to everyone who prepares a bid so the totals line up, keep the signed pages.

Frequently asked questions

Should I document dented gutters?

Yes. Hail that dents gutters has often hit the roof too, so gutter dents are worth photographing both as a repair item and as a sign of the storm's severity. Ask your adjuster whether they are included in the scope.

Do gutter guards complicate the repair scope?

They add a line item and occasionally a matching question. Make sure your bids list them, and ask your adjuster whether they are included in the scope.
