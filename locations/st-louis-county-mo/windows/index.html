In St. Louis County, replacement windows are both a storm-repair item and an efficiency upgrade. When wind or hail compromises frames and seals, homeowners face a choice between like-for-like replacement and stepping up to modern units — and written bids that price each option can help you compare that choice.

The Northeast Prairie climate and your windows

St. Louis County is in the Northeast Prairie, which NOAA's climate division list uses as division 2 of Missouri's six. The Missouri Climate Center says that "mean July precipitation is greatest in northeastern Missouri, largely the result of high-intensity convectional precipitation (4.4 inches), and least in southwestern Missouri (3.2 inches)." It also says that "The metropolitan areas of St. Louis and Kansas City exert a significant and measurable effect on their climates," with temperatures "elevated in both regions by a few degrees."

Window issues to look for after a storm

  • Water intrusion at damaged openings — interior finish damage near a damaged opening; document it and mention it to your adjuster.
  • Screen and hardware damage — small items that are easy to leave off a first scope; list them in your documentation.
  • Failed insulated-glass seals — post-storm fogging between panes; document it and ask your adjuster whether it is included.
  • Energy-efficiency step-ups — homeowners choosing between like-for-like replacement and upgraded units can ask for written bids that price the difference.
  • Matching and availability questions — discontinued window lines raise the same repair-versus-replace questions that come up with siding.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for St. Louis County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Ask any contractor for an itemized written estimate, proof of insurance, and local references. The process can take longer after a widespread storm, when repair schedules fill up. Patience and paperwork usually serve homeowners better than speed and pressure.

Season and timing

Timing matters in St. Louis County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Replacing windows in St. Louis County starts with a decision about method. A full-frame replacement removes the old frame and trim and sets a new unit into the bare opening; an insert, sometimes called a pocket replacement, leaves the existing frame in place and fits a new sash assembly inside it. The first gives access to the framing and flashing around the opening, the second keeps more of the interior trim. A bid should say which approach it uses for every opening.

Measure and count before anyone visits. List each window by room, note how it opens (double-hung, casement, slider, fixed), and mark any that are oversized, arched, or sit above stairs. Photograph each one inside and out. Ask whoever wrote the bid to write the same list back to you with the size, the style and the frame material, so there is no confusion about which line refers to which opening.

Glass specifications are written in a shorthand worth asking about: double or triple panes, a low-emissivity coating, gas fill between the panes, and the spacer along the edge. Ask for the manufacturer's name and series, the stated energy ratings that appear on the product label, and the written warranty for the frame, the glass and the hardware. Ask whether screens, grids and locks are included and what color the interior and exterior will be.

Then ask about the finish work: interior casing, caulk, exterior trim wrap, foam around the frame, and the clean-up of old glass and debris. Ask how furniture and floors inside will be protected. Keep every page of what you sign.
