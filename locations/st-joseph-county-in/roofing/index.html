A severe storm in St. Joseph County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

The northern Indiana climate and your roofing

St. Joseph County is in the northern part of Indiana. NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says that in northwestern Indiana Lake Michigan moderates temperature, producing cooler summers and warmer winters, and that the lake is also the source of lake-effect snows, which can extend as far inland as Elkhart. It reports that some northern locations near Lake Michigan average more than 70 inches of snow a year, while the southwest averages about 14 inches.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.

What to expect locally

After a storm in St. Joseph County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

The offices that issue roofing permits in St. Joseph County

One department covers much of the county. The Building Department, which its county roofing form heads with both St. Joseph County and the City of South Bend, is at 125 S Lafayette Blvd, Suite 100, South Bend, phone 574-235-9554. Its residential permitting guide lists "Remodeling worth more than $500 including roofing, siding, windows/doors, and interior remodeling" among work that needs a permit and says "Work requiring permits also requires a registered contractor." The guide has the department handling the city, "All 5 digit County addresses" and, after Town Board approval, the towns of Lakeville, Roseland, Indian Village, North Liberty and Osceola. It says it does not cover four-digit-or-shorter addresses in Mishawaka, New Carlisle or Walkerton.

That department also publishes a "County Residential Roofing Permit Application" with a cost line reading "TEAR-OFF/RE-ROOF: $ OR OVERLAY: $". The form warns "If work has been started without a permit, you will be triple fined" and states "Receipt of application does not mean a permit has been issued." It allows 6 months from issuance to start the work and 2 years to finish it, and a missed deadline means reapplying. Mishawaka's Building Department, 100 Lincolnway West, (574) 258-1607, open weekdays from 7:30 am to 4:45 pm, says it "handles contractor licenses and building permits"; its page does not mention roofing. New Carlisle and Walkerton were not checked.

Roofer licensing: what the state publishes

Roofing is not on the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency's profession list, which includes home inspectors, plumbing and real estate. A consumer leaflet from Indiana's Attorney General sends readers to their building office to learn whether a permit is needed, and to their city or county for licensing rules where they live. In St. Joseph County the practical answer comes from whichever of the South Bend department, Mishawaka or a town office covers your address.

Questions to settle before you sign a St. Joseph County bid

- Identify the office that covers your address: the South Bend department (city, 5-digit county addresses and some towns), Mishawaka, New Carlisle, Walkerton or a town.

- Have each bidder say whether the job is a tear-off or an overlay and put that in the written bid, because the county application has a cost line for each.

- Confirm each bidder holds a contractor registration with whichever office issues your permit, because the South Bend department's guide ties permit work to registered contractors.

- Find where ice and water shield appears in the written bid, because the state table lists "Yes" for St. Joseph.

- Ask the bidder to confirm the permit has been issued before work starts, since an application receipt is not a permit.

- Hand every bidder the same copy of any written estimate you hold from your insurer.
