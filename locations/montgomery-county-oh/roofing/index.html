The southwest Ohio climate and your roofing

NOAA's Ohio state climate summary records that the Xenia tornado, rated F5, was part of the Super Outbreak of 1974. Xenia is in Greene County, in the Southwest division that also contains Montgomery County. The same summary calls the Great Flood of 1913 Ohio's greatest weather disaster, and it states that dangerous storms can occur in every season. It also lists snowstorms and ice storms as winter weather in Ohio.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Montgomery County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing is worth planning. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors, and winter weather can limit exterior work. Document the damage first, then take the time you need to compare any written bids you receive.

Permits and local rules

Two kinds of office matter here. Montgomery County's Building Regulations Division, at 451 West Third Street in Dayton, says on its FAQ page that every township in the county falls under its jurisdiction and that some villages and cities contract with it for part or all of their enforcement. The same page warns that other jurisdictions, naming Dayton, Centerville and Kettering, "may have different requirements for contractor licensing or registration."

The county division's memo on roof coverings for shingle roofs, from its Chief Building Official and dated 10/09/2020, answers the permit question this way: "No permit is required for tear-offs and re-roofs that don't involve structural repairs, but they must still be installed in accordance with the provisions of the current Residential Code of Ohio (RCO), or the current Ohio Building Code (OBC)." Where the code table calls for it, the memo describes an ice barrier of "at least two layers of underlayment cemented together or of a self-adhering polymer modified bitumen sheet" from the eave edge to a point at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, and it says existing layers must come off first where "the existing roof has two or more applications of any type of roof covering." The memo is dated 2020, so ask the division at 937-225-4622 whether it still stands.

Inside the City of Dayton, the Building Inspection Division's FAQ poses "Do I need a permit to install or re-roof my house?" and replies "If you are simply applying a new layer of shingles you do not need a building permit", while replacing or repairing any of the wood sheathing under the shingles, or the rafters or trusses, does require one. It adds that you "might need to obtain a Certificate of Appropriateness (COA) if your home is located in a historic district." The division says it manages a registration program for "plumbing, mechanical and electric contractors", and its registration forms and lists cover electrical, pipe-laying, plumbing and warm air heating contractors, not roofing, so ask the division at 937-333-3883 how a roofer is treated. The county FAQ says state licensing is not required for "contractors that only do 1, 2, 3 Family Residential work."

What to line up before you ask for bids

- Your insurer's written estimate, if you hold one, kept where every bidder can read it.

- Whether your address falls under the county division, the City of Dayton or another city's building office, since the rules above differ between them.

- A note of whether the roof deck or rafters need repair, because both offices tie a permit to structural work, and a question to each bidder about how its bid handles that.
