The southwest Ohio climate and your roofing

Xenia is in Greene County, in the Southwest division that also contains Butler County. The same summary calls the Great Flood of 1913 Ohio's greatest weather disaster, and it states that dangerous storms can occur in every season. It also lists snowstorms and ice storms as winter weather in Ohio.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Impact damage that hides from the ground — hail strikes are hard to see without getting on the roof, which is why documentation matters.

What to expect locally

Homeowners in Butler County navigating a storm claim juggle three parallel tracks: the insurance process (adjuster inspection, scope, settlement), the contractor process (bids, scheduling, materials), and their own documentation. Keeping those tracks separate is the single most useful habit — your insurer decides coverage under your policy; your contractor proposes the repair scope; and written bids let you compare scope and pricing side by side.

Ask any contractor for an itemized written estimate, proof of insurance, and local references. The process can take longer after a widespread storm, when repair schedules fill up. Patience and paperwork usually serve homeowners better than speed and pressure.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Butler County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Stones of an inch or more are named in 21 entries across 11 days. Two entries reach 2.00 inches: Newkirk at 2:16 p.m. EST on February 27, 2023, and Kyles at 8:06 p.m. EST on May 16, 2025. Four companions share that Kyles evening, a 1.50-inch Oakland entry, a 1.25-inch Hamilton Arpt entry and one-inch entries for Mayfield and Lemon. By count, the heaviest hail day is April 8, 2020. Seven entries, stamped 9:03 to 9:27 p.m. EST, name Kyles, Bethany, Monroe, Hamilton, Jacksonburgh and Maud, and the largest is 1.25 inches.

Cold-season hail is part of the picture here. February accounts for 5 entries over 2 days (February 24, 2017 and February 27, 2023). April has 9 entries over 3 days, May has 8 over 4, July has 6 over 4 and June has 4 over 4. Nothing is filed for 2019 or 2026.

Wind outweighs hail. NOAA files 83 thunderstorm wind entries across 50 days, beginning June 15, 2016 and ending June 11, 2026. That final entry is stamped 11:35 p.m. EST, so a wall clock on daylight time already read 12:35 a.m. on June 12. Gust values were estimated for 77 of the entries and measured for 6. The month with the most is June (31 entries, 12 days), and July comes second (19 entries, 15 days). One afternoon dominates: June 13, 2022, nine entries between 4:45 and 4:59 p.m. EST under eight labels (Trenton, Woodsdale, Maud, Monroe, Stockton, Middletown, Furmandale and West Middletown). The ceiling for this county is an estimated 60 knots, shown on five entries, Monroe's entry that afternoon is one. The top measured figure is 59 knots at Woodsdale, that same afternoon; the gust was "measured at mesonet station EW6002 in Hamilton", according to the entry.

A few narratives dwell on roofs. The Furmandale entry of April 8, 2020 reports numerous shingles taken off one roof. The Woodsdale entry of July 19, 2020 says "Several houses had shingles and siding blown off". New Miami's entry for March 30, 2025, an estimate of 60 knots, says "Multiple trailers were knocked off of their foundations".

Gusts with no thunderstorm attached get filed by forecast zone instead. Butler's zone holds 6 High Wind entries, all at 50 knots (five estimated, one measured), dated April 3, 2016, November 27, 2019, December 30, 2019, March 25, 2021, February 9, 2023 and September 27, 2024. It also holds 4 Strong Wind entries, from 42 to 49 knots, dated February 19, 2016, February 24, 2019, November 15, 2020 and March 13, 2026.

The tornado list holds 10 entries over 7 days, earliest June 18, 2021, latest June 18, 2026. Of these, four started outside the county: a 0.71-mile EF0 out of Preble County, Ohio on June 18, 2021, and EF0 entries out of Franklin County, Indiana, of 0.32 miles (May 7, 2024), 8.05 miles (April 2, 2025) and 1.68 miles (June 18, 2026). The April 2, 2025 entry carries an 11:26 p.m. EST stamp, so on a daylight-time wall clock it was 12:26 a.m. on April 3.

EF1 is as high as the county's ratings go, and two entries earn it. The Jacksonburgh line of February 27, 2023, 4.17 miles long, took fascia and shingles off several homes, per NOAA's narrative, and removed the roof from the upper story of one house; winds were estimated at 110 mph. The Woods Station line of May 7, 2024, 2.38 miles, damaged the roof and garage of a house, and the narrative gives roughly 110 mph for that area. A third entry that day is a 0.28-mile EF0 labeled Oakland, where several structures lost roofing material or metal siding. March 30, 2025 brings a pair of further EF0 entries: 6.89 miles from North Hamilton Arpt toward Le Sourdsville, and 1.98 miles at Maud.

On timekeeping, NOAA's format guide describes times as "usually in LST" (local standard time), and every Butler County entry here is stamped EST, so any daylight-time wall clock ran one hour ahead of the stamp. The 2026 file behind these counts was created by NOAA on September 18, 2026 and is refreshed monthly, which means 2026 totals can move. All of this is county-wide weather. It says nothing about one address and forecasts nothing.

Who answers for a re-roof

Butler County's Building and Zoning page describes its staff as "responsible for enforcing the building code, electrical code and zoning regulations for the unincorporated areas of Butler County". Find the office at 130 High Street in Hamilton, telephone 513-887-3205. Inspectors work 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., Monday to Friday, according to the page, and take questions at the office roughly 8:00 to 9:00 a.m. and 3:30 to 4:00 p.m.; the county keeps Eastern time. An inspection schedule on the page puts building inspections for Hanover, Morgan, Reily and Ross townships on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and for College Corner, Madison, Milford, Oxford, St. Clair and Wayne on Tuesday and Thursday, with electrical inspections on the opposite days; it lists inspections every day in Fairfield, Lemon, Liberty, Trenton and West Chester townships.

Re-roofing gets separate paperwork. The county's residential procedures sheet says "Permits for HVAC Replacement, Re-roof, Electric repair have their own applications". On the Re-Roof Application and Addendum, the applicant marks Owner, Contractor or Other; hands in the roofing contract or an N/A; picks Repairing, Recovering or Replacing on a scope line; attaches a dimensioned roof plan or photo showing slopes and vents; and attaches manufacturer installation instructions. A ventilation note sets a minimum net free area of 1/150 of the attic space. A county building official's memo from September 3, 2015 (version 8-25-15) is an older document; it marks a new roof (re-shingling) Yes for a building permit.

For addresses inside Hamilton, the City's permits page names a Roofing Permit among its permit types. Hamilton's contractor registration page, which gives 345 High Street as the office, ties registration to "Electric, HVAC, Plumbing, Gas, Hydronics/Wet Heat or Sewer work", and the list omits roofing. Other Butler County addresses may answer to a different city or a township office, and those pages were not read here, so ask the office that serves your address.

Before you ask for bids

- Any written estimate from your insurer, kept where each bidder can read it.

- Your jurisdiction: an unincorporated township answers to the county department, while Hamilton issues a Roofing Permit of its own.

- One question for each bidder: who fills in the Re-Roof Application and Addendum, and whose name goes on it as applicant?

- The roofing contract itself, because the county addendum asks for the contract or an N/A.

- How many roof layers sit on the house now, and whether the scope line should say Repairing, Recovering or Replacing.
