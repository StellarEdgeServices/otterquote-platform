A severe storm in Sevier County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Sevier County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Ask any contractor for an itemized written estimate, proof of insurance, and local references. The process can take longer after a widespread storm, when repair schedules fill up. Patience and paperwork usually serve homeowners better than speed and pressure.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Sevier County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Sevier County notes

Sevier County storms on NOAA's books: Sevierville, Pigeon Forge, Gatlinburg

Take NOAA's per-year Storm Events archives, keep Tennessee, filter the county column to SEVIER, keep hail, and 21 entries remain, falling on 13 different dates. They span April 22, 2016 through October 21, 2025. Per year, entries then days: 2016 4/2, 2018 1/1, 2020 1/1, 2022 1/1, 2024 2/1, 2025 12/7. Four years in between (2017, 2019, 2021, 2023) are blank, and so is 2026. Stone sizes run 0.75 inch (1 entry), 0.88 (2), 1.00 (11), 1.25 (4), 1.50 (2) and 1.75 (1). Counting only stones of an inch or more leaves 18, and each of those 13 days produced at least one. The Morristown weather office's safety booklet calls hail "one inch in diameter" or larger severe, and its chart equates 1.50 with a Ping-Pong ball and 1.75 with a golfball. The lone golfball entry, filed as BEECH SPGS, comes from February 6, 2025 at 21:40, a winter reading where EST is the wall clock.

One downtown report places hail "in the downtown area of Sevierville". Its clock reads 15:30 EST for April 22, 2016 (16:30 EDT), measures 1.25 inches, and NOAA's label reads CATLETTSBURG while the narrative says Sevierville. Pigeon Forge logged hail twice on July 8, 2016: at 17:03 EST (18:03 EDT), then at 17:05 EST (18:05 EDT), the later stone 1.50 inches. Gusts followed: Elkmont, an estimated 55 knots at 17:57 EST (18:57 EDT), then an estimated 60 knots for Pitner, logged 18:10 EST (19:10 EDT). Pitner's narrative has a roof coming off the "Dollar General Store on highway 441". Of all 70 wind narratives, that is the only place a roof appears.

Hail makes the shorter list and wind the longer: 70 entries on 47 days between April 22, 2016 and September 4, 2025. Of those, 68 are flagged EG, NOAA's code for estimated, and the other two carry MG, for measured. Per year: 2016 9/6, 2017 5/3, 2018 4/4, 2019 15/10, 2020 5/4, 2021 6/5, 2022 2/1, 2023 11/6, 2024 7/5, 2025 6/3. The warm months dominate: 52 entries on 34 days in the May-to-August stretch, August alone supplying 16. Knots (entries): 50 (32), 52 (28), 55 (3), 56 (2), then 60, 61 and 69 once apiece, and 74 (2).

Both 74s date from 2023. A tower on Cove Mountain, filed under LINE SPG, recorded one at 02:00 EST on March 25 (03:00 EDT; clocks had sprung forward on March 12): "The wind gust was measured at the Cove Mountain fire tower." The other, an estimate at CATES XRDS logged 13:45 EST, August 7 (14:45 EDT), came from an NWS survey whose narrative runs "just west of the Splendor Oaks subdivision off of Highway 139 in Sevier county." The county's second measurement, 69 knots at 18:00 EST on January 11, 2020 (winter, so no shift), is credited to "Cove Mountain Tower" and adds "Time of occurrence was estimated." In August 2024 a utility company's report gave an estimated 61 knots at Pigeon Forge, 16:50 EST (17:50 EDT), its narrative ending in "4400 customers without power."

Who issues permits here

State license rules

Tennessee's contractor regulators want a prime contractor licensed once a project reaches $25,000, bidding included. A second, lower-dollar license called Home Improvement ($3,000 up to $24,999) reaches only counties that adopted it; nine are listed, and Sevier is absent. County Building Inspections points to a "Contractor lookup"; the state's own Verify a License page opens "Verify Public Search", and no lookup was attempted here.
