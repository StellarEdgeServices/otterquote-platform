The central Indiana climate and your roofing

Morgan County sits in central Indiana. NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says dangerous storms can occur in every season, that winter can bring snow and ice storms, and that thunderstorms capable of producing floods, hail and tornadoes are common in the warmer months. It records that heavy snowstorms during the first week of January 2014 dropped 1 to more than 2 feet of snow over central and northern Indiana.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Morgan County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Storm damage in central Indiana can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

Who issues permits in Morgan County

The county's own permit page, at 180 S Main St. in Martinsville, offers an online building permit link and pages for septic systems, food sales, pools and tattoo parlors; it states no rule for roofs. The City of Martinsville's Building Services page, read in an Internet Archive copy of September 26, 2025 because the live site refused this research's request with an access-denied error, lists 59 S Jefferson Street, (765) 349-4923, and hours of 8:00 am to 4:00 pm Monday through Friday, closed from noon to 1:00 pm. It says contractors "must be licensed with the City" and names electricians, plumbers and heating and cooling firms for licenses; no roofing line appears. That page also links a separate application for projects outside the city limits.

The Town of Mooresville's Building Department, 4 E. Harrison St., (317) 831-9545, is the one office read whose page speaks about roofs: "Permits are not generally needed for roofing, siding and window replacements. However, if you are replacing the decking on a roof, you will need a permit." It adds that being a licensed contractor is not necessary to apply for a permit, and that an inspection request needs 48 hours of notice. The offices in Monrovia, Brooklyn and Paragon were not read, and no document found here gives a Morgan County rule for reroofing outside the two towns above.

What state pages say about roofer licensing

The Indiana Professional Licensing Agency lists plumbing, real estate and home inspectors, among others, and no roofing profession. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet tells homeowners to ask the local building department whether a permit is needed and says licensing requirements vary by community. So in Morgan County the question is local: Martinsville's contractor licenses, Mooresville's no-license-needed statement, and whatever the county and the smaller towns decide.

Preparing for your Morgan County bids

- Find out which office covers your address: Martinsville, Mooresville, a smaller town or the county.

- Ask each bidder whether a tear-off needs a permit there, and ask about the decking, since Mooresville ties its answer to it.

- Ask who pulls the permit and how many hours of notice the inspector needs.

- Ask whether the shingle maker's instructions call for ice and water shield, since the state table says "No" for Morgan.

- Hand every bidder the same copy of any written estimate you hold from your insurer.
