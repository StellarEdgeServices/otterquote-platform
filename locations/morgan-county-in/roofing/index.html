The central Indiana climate and your roofing

Morgan County sits in central Indiana. NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary says dangerous storms can occur in every season, that winter can bring snow and ice storms, and that thunderstorms capable of producing floods, hail and tornadoes are common in the warmer months. It records that heavy snowstorms during the first week of January 2014 dropped 1 to more than 2 feet of snow over central and northern Indiana.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Hail bruising and granule loss — impact marks that shorten shingle life even when no leak appears immediately.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.

What to expect locally

After a storm in Morgan County, the useful work is slow: getting damage documented, raising questions with your insurer, and having the repair scoped carefully. Many homeowners find it helps to slow the process down at the start — documenting damage before tarps and repairs change the evidence, reading their policy before the first phone call, and getting more than one written bid before signing anything.

Be appropriately skeptical of anyone who shows up unsolicited after a storm, pressures you to sign paperwork on the spot, or quotes a price without getting on the roof or examining the damage up close. Storm-chasing crews can show up after any major storm, and one practical defense is unhurried, written bids that you can check against each other.

Season and timing

Storm damage in central Indiana can arrive in clusters: a single hail event can affect many roofs, gutters, and siding elevations in an area at once. Comparing more than one written bid for the same scope is one way homeowners check pricing when demand spikes.

Reading the Morgan entries

Those 78 entries carry 20 place labels in NOAA's data. Paragon has 15 (6 hail, 9 wind), Martinsville 11, Martinsville Airport 8, Mooresville 8 and Monrovia 7. A label is a spot a forecaster typed in for a report, which is not necessarily the town a homeowner would name. As for who reported: NOAA credits the public with 27 entries, trained spotters with 11, amateur radio operators with 10, National Weather Service employees with 8 and storm surveys with 5, while the remaining 17 come from nine other kinds of observer. Map coordinates are present on every entry, and the Census Bureau's geocoder places all 78 inside Morgan County.

The format guide from NOAA describes its times as "usually in LST", which means local standard time. These Morgan entries say EST, so one hour is added wherever daylight time applies; that holds for 74 of the 78 and pushes none past midnight. Excluded from every count are 18 entries typed as forecast zones bearing the name Morgan (Heavy Snow 7, High Wind 4, Winter Storm 3, Extreme Cold/Wind Chill 2, Ice Storm 1, Excessive Heat 1), along with 12 county-level flood and flash flood entries. The 2026 file used in this count bears a stamp of October 26, 2026 from NOAA. The README states that the current year's file gets a monthly refresh, so recent counts can move.

Which offices handle permits in Morgan County

Morgan County's own permit page, at 180 S Main St. in Martinsville, links to an online building permit and has pages on septic systems, food sales, pools and tattoo parlors; it gives no rule for roofs. The live site of the City of Martinsville's Building Services refused this research's request with an access-denied error, so an Internet Archive copy of its page from September 26, 2025 was read. It lists 59 S Jefferson Street, (765) 349-4923, and hours of 8:00 am to 4:00 pm Monday through Friday, closed from noon to 1:00 pm. The page says contractors "must be licensed with the City" and names electricians, plumbers and heating and cooling firms for licenses, with no roofing line. It also links a separate application for projects outside the city limits.

Of the offices read, the one whose page speaks about roofs is the Town of Mooresville's Building Department, 4 E. Harrison St., (317) 831-9545: "Permits are not generally needed for roofing, siding and window replacements. However, if you are replacing the decking on a roof, you will need a permit." The page adds that applying for a permit does not require being a licensed contractor, and that an inspection request needs 48 hours of notice. This research did not read the offices in Monrovia, Brooklyn and Paragon, and none of the documents found gives a Morgan County rule on reroofing outside Martinsville and Mooresville.

Roofer licensing under Indiana state pages

The Indiana Professional Licensing Agency's list includes plumbing, real estate and home inspectors, among others, but no roofing profession. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet advises homeowners to ask the local building department whether a permit is needed, and says licensing requirements vary by community. In Morgan County, then, the question is a local one: Martinsville's contractor licenses, Mooresville's no-license-needed statement, and whatever the county and the smaller towns decide.

Questions to settle before your Morgan County bids

- Work out which office covers your address: Martinsville, Mooresville, a smaller town or the county.

- Ask every bidder whether a tear-off needs a permit there, and bring up the decking, since Mooresville ties its answer to it.

- Ask who pulls the permit, and how many hours of notice the inspector needs.

- Ask whether the shingle maker's instructions call for ice and water shield, given that the state table says "No" for Morgan.

- Hand every bidder the same copy of any written estimate you hold from your insurer.
