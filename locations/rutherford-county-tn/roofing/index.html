The Middle Tennessee climate and your roofing

NWS Nashville's heavy precipitation climatology for Middle Tennessee, based on 1961 to 1990 data from 43 cooperative stations, calls November through May the wet season, when two-thirds of annual precipitation falls. It gives March as the wettest month at 5.76 inches and October as the driest at 3.38 inches. These are regional figures for the area that includes Rutherford County.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Decking and underlayment issues — discovered only at tear-off, and can be a source of change orders worth understanding in advance.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.

What to expect locally

The practical sequence for Rutherford County homeowners after storm damage: document everything with photos before any cleanup, review your policy and ask your insurer how to report damage, and gather written repair bids so you can compare scope and pricing side by side. Nothing in that sequence requires committing to a contractor early — you can keep your options open while you review any written bids you receive.

Ask any contractor for an itemized written estimate, proof of insurance, and local references. The process can take longer after a widespread storm, when repair schedules fill up. Patience and paperwork usually serve homeowners better than speed and pressure.

Season and timing

Post-storm weeks can be congested, and quotes gathered in a hurry may be harder to compare; the weeks after the rush can be a calmer time to review bids. Whatever the calendar says, the sequence stays the same: document first, understand your policy second, compare any written bids you receive third — and take your time rather than rushing to agree with the first person who knocks on the door.

Twenty-seven of them are an inch or larger, spread over 17 dates, and ten reach an inch and a half or more, over 7 dates. Two are logged at 2.00 inches. The first, May 6, 2024, is labeled Christiana and described as "Estimated 1.5 to 2 inch hail". The second, March 30, 2025, is labeled La Vergne, and its narrative says "Estimated 2 inch hail was reported between Nolensville and Smyrna." Next down are two at 1.75 inches: the May 26, 2024 report labeled Murfreesboro ("Golf ball size hail was reported west of Murfreesboro") and the May 20, 2025 report labeled Eagleville ("Estimated golf ball size hail was reported near Christiana from a tornado-warned thunderstorm"). March through June account for 30 of the 36 reports and 18 of the 22 dates; May has 14 reports on 7 dates, and four reports come from July. Four reports on May 26, 2024 make that the busiest hail date.

For thunderstorm wind, the file lists 138 reports on 64 dates from March 31, 2016 through June 9, 2026, with an instrument behind 8 and an estimate behind 130. By month: June has 32 reports on 11 dates, March 26 on 9, July 20 on 11 and August 19 on 9. March 21, 2017 and August 13, 2019 share the top spot with 9 reports each.

April 1, 2023 has three entries, EF1, EF1 and EF2. The EF2, 12.4 miles between the labels Rucker and Kittrell, is placed "six miles south of downtown Murfreesboro near County Farm Road", then northeast along Elam Road, with peak winds estimated at 130 mph. An EF0 on July 29, 2024 began just east of Memorial Boulevard and ended southeast of Middle Tennessee State University on Copper Hollow Drive, with winds estimated at 75 mph. The EF2 of November 6, 2018 covers 8.55 miles between the labels Newtown and Plainview.

How to read the clock. Dates follow NOAA's records; its format guide says times are "usually in LST" (local standard time), and every Rutherford County row counted here is stamped CST. A stamp made during daylight time therefore sits an hour behind a wall clock in CDT. The La Vergne hail entry, stamped 11:05 p.m. CST on March 30, 2025, was already March 31 on a wall clock. NOAA's 2026 data is still preliminary, and its README says the current year's file gets updated every month. Everything above describes weather across the county, not any one property, and none of it predicts what comes next.

Codes offices and the state license

Rutherford County and the City of Murfreesboro each run a codes office, and Smyrna, La Vergne and Eagleville, which came up above, may answer for their own addresses; this page did not read their permit pages. The county Building Codes Department, 1 South Public Square, Room 101, 615-898-7734, covers re-roofing in its Building Code FAQ. The FAQ gives the hours as Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:15 p.m. Central, and says the office "closes promptly" at 4:15 p.m. Central. Asked "Am I required to obtain a permit to re-roof my house?", it answers "A building permit is not required only if re-roofing or covering does not require the replacement and/or repairs of roof sheathing, rafters or support beams." On layers, it answers "Two." and says that when the second layer is replaced "both layers of old roofing should be removed prior to installing new roofing."

The Murfreesboro Building and Codes Department, 111 West Vine Street, 615-893-3750, Monday through Friday 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Central, has an FAQ stating "A permit is required in order to construct, alter, convert, repair, enlarge, move or demolish any building or structure or part thereof". A second answer places "minor repairs to chimneys, porches, underpinning or roofing" among "normal maintenance repairs" that need no permit. It never says whether a complete replacement counts as minor, so check with the department. For the Historic Zoning District, it tells homeowners to call the Murfreesboro Planning Department at 615-893-6441 before exterior renovations.

On the state side, Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors pages read in October 2026 say Home Improvement licensing applies "to the counties adopting the law", list Rutherford eighth of nine, and define home improvement to cover "roofing". The Board's contractor page ties a state license to "projects of $25,000 or more". The Board's pages link a Verify a License search, which was not used for this page, so run the lookup yourself.

Lining up before bids come in

- Any written estimate from your insurer, kept where each bidder can read it.

- Whether your property is in Murfreesboro or under the county department, since each posts its own permit answers.

- A question for every bidder: if the job needs sheathing, rafter or support-beam work, who applies for the permit?

- A count of the shingle layers on the roof today, and a plan for what comes off.

- Each bidder's Tennessee license number, checked in the Verify a License search.
