A severe storm in Hamilton County can leave roofs with the full menu of problems: hail bruising, wind-lifted shingles, damaged flashing, and the slow leaks that follow. Getting more than one written bid is one way to understand what a repair may cost.

The central Indiana climate and your roofing

In central Indiana, where Hamilton County is located, NOAA's 2022 Indiana State Climate Summary notes that the state has a long and deadly history of tornadic storms. It identifies June 2, 1990 as Indiana's largest tornado outbreak, with 37 tornadoes, several of them at F4 intensity, and says spring and summer precipitation has generally been above average since the 1990s.

Roofing issues to look for after a storm

  • Ventilation and code items — older roofs may need code-related upgrades; ask your contractor and your building department how local code items are handled.
  • Slow leaks after a storm — minor damage can go unnoticed until water stains show indoors.
  • Partial-slope damage — storms can damage one or two elevations, raising repair-versus-replace questions that comparing bids can help you think through.
  • Wind-lifted and creased shingles — broken seal strips let later storms drive rain under the roof surface.
  • Flashing and penetration damage — check chimneys, vents, and valleys, where post-storm leaks can start.

What to expect locally

Homeowners in Hamilton County navigating a storm claim juggle three parallel tracks: the insurance process (adjuster inspection, scope, settlement), the contractor process (bids, scheduling, materials), and their own documentation. Keeping those tracks separate is the single most useful habit — your insurer decides coverage under your policy; your contractor proposes the repair scope; and written bids let you compare scope and pricing side by side.

Local demand can also move in waves. After a widely publicized hail event, contractors across a region may get busy at once. If you receive more than one written bid, comparing them gives you a way to check pricing when demand spikes, and can surface scope differences — what one bidder saw that another missed — before the work starts rather than after.

Season and timing

Timing matters in Hamilton County. The weeks after a storm can be busy for adjusters and contractors. Documentation is the part that does not depend on the calendar: photograph damage once it is safe and note the date for your adjuster. Ask your insurer whether your policy sets a deadline for reporting damage.

Fishers, Indiana: thunderstorm wind and a little hail in NOAA's storm files, 2016 to 2026

Fishers is mostly a wind story in NOAA's records. NOAA's Storm Events files for 2016 through 2026 list Fishers reports under Hamilton County, not the city, so the Census Bureau's geocoder was run on the map point of all 128 hail, thunderstorm wind and tornado entries in the county, and each was assigned to the incorporated place that contains it, if any. Eighteen land inside the city of Fishers: 15 thunderstorm wind entries on 14 dates and 3 hail entries on a single date, with no tornado. The wind list begins February 28, 2017 and ends May 19, 2026. Twelve entries are estimated gusts (EG) and 3 are measured (MG). By year, entries then dates, it reads 2017 with 2 and 2, 2019 with 1 and 1, 2020 with 4 and 3, 2021 with 1 and 1, 2022 with 2 and 2, 2023 with 1 and 1, 2025 with 3 and 3, and 2026 with 1 and 1; nothing is filed for 2018 or 2024.

By month, May, March and August each hold 3 entries, on 3, 2 and 3 dates, April and July hold 2 each on 2 dates, and February and June one apiece. Values run from 48 to 60 knots: 48 (1), 50 (3), 52 (4), 54 (3), 55 (2), 56 (1) and 60 (1). March 28, 2020 is the only date with two entries. The highest is a measured 60 knots at 21:20 EST (22:20 daylight time) that evening, from a trained spotter, who "measured a 69 mph thunderstorm wind gust." Two other measured entries are a 56-knot gust on February 28, 2017 at 23:05 EST, described as "A 65 mph thunderstorm wind gust was measured in this location," and a 54-knot reading at 21:00 EST (22:00 daylight time) on August 16, 2025 from the airport's automated weather station, "62 mph recorded at Indianapolis Metro Airport AWOS in Fishers."

Buildings show up in three narratives. At 21:20 EST (22:20 daylight time) on March 28, 2020, someone reported "Concrete siding ripped from a couple of houses." At 19:43 EST (20:43 daylight time) on April 8, 2020 there was structural damage to a CVS at 131st Street and State Road 37. At 03:20 EST on March 6, 2022, before daylight time began that year, a trained spotter wrote that "Thunderstorm winds blew many shingles off of roofs while bits of siding were ripped from houses"; NOAA's damage field for it reads $18,000, the highest on the Fishers list. A July 17, 2023 entry from an NWS employee reports a few large trees down between Fishers and Fortville amid small hail. Most other entries are limbs and power lines, such as the 2017 report of limbs that "downed several power lines" with outages, and a tree across a roadway on Quarry Court on May 19, 2026.

The hail is thin. All three entries fall on March 30, 2017: a 0.88 at 15:27 EST (16:27 daylight time) from a trained spotter, who noted that "No damage was spotted," and two 0.75 entries at 15:38 and 17:40 EST. The forecast zone and the state's roof rules

Hamilton County is zone INC057 in the National Weather Service's records, under the Indianapolis forecast office (IND). The Indiana Department of Homeland Security sheet titled "Ice and Water Shield Update 2020-02-25" reprints Hamilton's row of Table R301.2(1), as amended for Indiana, which gives 115 mph, Seismic Design Category A, 20 psf of ground snow and a 30-inch foundation depth. That sheet also reprints the shingle rule: "A drip edge shall be provided at eaves and rake edges of shingle roofs," with adjacent pieces overlapped "not less than 2 inches." Where an ice barrier is required it must reach at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line. For Hamilton the table says "No" under Ice Shield Underlayment Required, though the sheet adds that a maker's instructions can still call for it.

Who issues permits and registers contractors in Fishers

The city's Planning and Zoning page says that department handles permitting and inspections and gives 317-595-3155 for zoning questions. The city's fee schedule, in section 35.30 of its posted code, lists a Reroof entry under the Building Division at $75 for one and two family inspections and $100 for all other construction types, so ask permit staff what a reroof at your address requires. None of the Fishers pages read for this page gives the building office's street address or hours, so call before assuming how a given job is handled.

What the city does state is a contractor registration rule. Its Contractor Registration page says contractors and permit holders must register to perform work in the city, "except a homeowner performing the work themselves," and that registration is done online through ViewPoint Cloud. The city's registration ordinance, section 115.01, asks applicants for a general liability certificate with at least $500,000 for each occurrence of death or bodily injury and at least $100,000 for property damage, or a combined single limit of $500,000. It says coverage must not be limited to a single job and that the Engineering Department must receive written notice at least 15 days before cancellation. The page says the city does "not in any manner ensure or warrant the competency of the contractors registered," so a registration shows paperwork on file, not a judgment of the work. Section 115.02 makes a registration valid for one year and section 115.03 sets the fee at $100; sections 115.01 through 115.03 name no bond and do not mention roofers.

The Nickel Plate Review Committee and the city's design districts

Fishers also has a Nickel Plate Review Committee. The city's page for it says the committee helps review projects in the Nickel Plate District, looking at a proposal's design, architecture, materials and compatibility with surrounding development, and that it can act on waivers from the district's code standards. Its territory is the Nickel Plate District together with the City Center Residential Zoning District. Meetings start at 5 p.m. on the fourth Wednesday of the month: from January through July at Launch Fishers Theatre, 12175 Visionary Way, and from August through December at the Municipal Center Theater, 1 Municipal Drive.

The page says the committee handles only projects that reach certain size, intensity or density thresholds, and it names no roof work on a single house. A homeowner who lives in or near those two districts can ask Community Development, at 317-595-3120, whether the committee has any part in a reroof. Outside those districts, the registration rule and the permit question above are the local items to raise with each bidder.

Roofing licenses in Indiana

The Indiana Professional Licensing Agency's profession list names plumbing, real estate and home inspectors and has no roofing entry. The Attorney General's storm-chaser leaflet asks, "Is the contractor licensed, bonded and insured?" and says licensing requirements vary from community to community. In Fishers, that leaves the city registration and whatever the permit office says about your job.

Questions for your Fishers bids

- Ask the city's permit staff whether a reroof at your address needs a permit, and keep a note of who answered and when.

- Ask each bidder for its Fishers contractor registration and the liability certificate behind it, and whether that coverage is limited to one job.

- Ask whether shingles and siding are priced separately, since two Fishers narratives mention siding and one mentions shingles.

- Ask whether the quote includes drip edge, and whether decking will be replaced if it is found damaged.

- Hand each bidder an identical copy of your insurer's written estimate, if there is one.
